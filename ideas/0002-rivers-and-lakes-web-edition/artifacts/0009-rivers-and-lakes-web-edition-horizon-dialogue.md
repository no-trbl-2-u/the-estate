# Horizon, explored: Rivers and Lakes, web edition — the dialogue

The Forager, back from the first layout proposals with the operator's
redirect in hand. Same type in, sharper out: the six-month picture
stands; what changed is the surface the player meets.

The operator's words, verbatim, on seeing ten sheet-centred layouts:

> Imagine 20 prompts in, this sheet would be huge. I want this to play
> more like a video game where it's more of a dialogue than it is
> creating one whole sheet. The actually resulting story sheet can still
> exist as a tab within the game, just not as the only interface. I'm
> imagining something more like within the layout, there'd be tabs (ie.
> Game, items, scars, allies, enemies, etc. I don't actually know the game
> all that well). Also, the ledger I don't think is needed, that reads
> more like game logs to me.

Four choices followed, live, with the recommendation taken each time.

## What changed in the picture

**The play surface is a dialogue, one beat at a time.** The entry's prose
appears as the book speaking. Each of its questions is then asked on its
own with a place to answer. Each mechanical instruction is a choice the
player makes with a tap ("Check a Tag: Water Child · Quick Hands"), the
obvious option pre-selected as before. The Story sentence is the last
beat. Then the dice. A sitting is a sequence of screens, not a form.

**The sheet lives behind six tabs**: Game, Story, People, Things, Self,
Arts. Story holds Legends and Songs. People holds Characters grouped by
the book's three types (Righteous, Evil, Ordinary), not by ally and
enemy, because the book says the type is a reputation and "it is not
always so black and white" (Findings R27). Things holds Resources. Self
holds Tags and Scars. Arts holds Techniques. Each tab carries a small
count, and pulses when the dialogue just changed it.

**A thin HUD strip stays visible during play**: the Prompt number and
entry position, Legend slots used, Tags checked. Nothing else of the
sheet is on the Game screen.

**The ledger leaves the interface.** It stays in the save and the
export, so a finished game can still be audited against the book and
undone. On screen there is no log.

## What did not change

Verbatim Prompts, the advisory engine that proposes and never refuses,
both modes from one save, in-browser dice with manual entry, oracles
rolled in-site, local-first with one game per device and a restart, the
capped share link with its three warnings, the unlisted deploy, the
fork on the author's answer, and every reading the engine labels as a
reading. The Horizon's date is still 2027-03-05.

## What the redirect adds, and the rule it passes

Two additions, both permitted under "nothing that answers a Prompt for
the player, and nothing the paper game forbids":

- **The interface teaches the game as it goes.** The operator said "I
  don't actually know the game all that well." The first time a beat
  touches a trait, the beat says in one line what that trait is, in the
  book's words. Bookkeeping and projection, not content.
- **Beats are a projection of the entry, not a rewrite of it.** The
  prose is shown whole and verbatim first; the questions and
  instructions are the same sentences, split at the book's own full
  stops. The split is the site's reading and is labelled so.

## The sitting, retold (2027-03-05)

You open the unlisted address on the phone. The Game tab is up; the
strip reads *Prompt 6 · second entry · Legends 3/5 · Tags 1/3*. The
book speaks: the entry, whole. You tap on. *What happened?* You write
four lines. *How did you help them?* Two more. *Check a Tag* — Water
Child is already lit; you leave it. *Gain a new Resource* — you name the
copper bell. *Gain a new Scar* — the burn. *The Story, one sentence* —
you write it, and the beat says it will join Legend 3, the one about Lau
Sai Fung; you agree. The Self tab pulses once, the Things tab once, the
Story tab once. You do not open them. You tap the dice.

Later, on the laptop, you open Story. Three Legends with their Stories,
two empty slots, no Songs yet. You open People and see the four of them
under Ordinary and Righteous. Nothing here asks anything of you; it is
the sheet you would have kept on paper, kept for you.

## Falsifiers, re-examined

1. **The sheet keeps itself.** Unchanged: falsified if the next Xiake
   starts on paper. Check 2027-03-05.
2. **The book.** The earlier version rested this on a visible ledger. It
   now rests on the dialogue: every beat is labelled as the book's words
   or the site's reading, and the export carries the ledger. Falsified
   if a player who has finished a game cannot say, from the play screens
   alone, which sentences were the author's. Check 2027-03-05.
3. **For anyone.** Unchanged: the ask made or not by 2027-03-05, and its
   answer.

The Advocate's sixty-day silence rule and the export nudge before
restart are carried forward, still marked as the Architect's proposals.

## Consequences for the route

The Trajectory's fifth leg, "sheet and play UI", now means a dialogue
play surface plus six sheet tabs and a HUD strip. Its dependencies and
its human-attention tag (the operator chooses the layout) are unchanged.
The operations table and the engine are unaffected: a beat is a view over
the same proposals. No re-chart is needed; `phase` will decompose the
fifth leg with this shape.

## Left open by this exploration

- Whether a player may go back a beat within an entry, and whether that
  is an undo or a re-answer. The book has no such thing; the engine's
  advisory posture suggests re-answer.
- How the Quick Game mode looks in a dialogue: probably the questions
  collapse into the single Story beat. Not decided.
- Whether the tab names are the final words (Story, People, Things,
  Self, Arts) or placeholders for the operator's taste.
