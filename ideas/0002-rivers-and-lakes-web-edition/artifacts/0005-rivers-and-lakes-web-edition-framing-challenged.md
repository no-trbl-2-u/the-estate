# Framing, challenged: Rivers and Lakes, web edition

The Advocate, dispatched fresh-eyes with the packet, the Framing, the two
Sparks, the Findings and state 0003, and no memory of the session that
argued the Framing into being. The operator was live but did not join; every
attack below is filed unanswered, and an unanswered attack is weaker
evidence than a defended one. Where the Framing held it says so and why.
Where it bent, the revised text stands in the body and the attack that bent
it sits beside it. Nothing here is a gate.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The Framing's centre holds: the tension
between needing the author's text and having to define what the author
left to the player is the truest sentence in the document, and the attack
only sharpened it. The Framing bends at three joints. Its problem statement
conflates *what the thing is* (an edition of the book) with *what problem
it solves* (the book's bookkeeping, which the Findings show is the real
barrier). Its falsifier tests the framing it rejected and fires on a
finished game. And it carries "nothing added" as a tension when it is a
debt the record already knows how to pay. A dozen smaller assumptions were
pressed; most held with a cost named, a few were revised.

---

## The problem underneath

**The book is a notebook with no sheet in it.** Rivers and Lakes is played
by reading a Prompt, writing a sentence, and keeping a sheet of Legends,
Tags, Resources, Techniques, Characters and Scars honest under rules that
move traits between checked, struck and restored. The rulebook says "write
in this book in the spaces provided" and provides no record sheet and no
layout (Findings §7); the "spaces" are the white space between entries. To
play it you hold a 136-page PDF open at the Prompt pages, keep a sheet you
designed yourself, and roll two dice. The dice are the smallest part of
that.

The operator's answer to "what is this really about" was **the book,
playable, for anyone**. The Advocate reads that answer as a claim about
*identity*, not about the problem: the site is an **edition** of Rivers and
Lakes, not a tool for playing it. That is what forbids bring-your-own-book,
what makes the licence a gate on publication, and what the operator chose
against the Gardener's recommendation. The problem the site works on
day to day is still the Gardener's: the sheet keeps itself. The two are not
rivals; one says what the thing is, the other says what it does. The
Framing had them competing for one sentence, and the identity won a
contest it did not need to enter.

The question this record works on is unchanged: **can the book be made
playable in a browser without ceasing to be the book?** Two things threaten
that. The text belongs to its author, and no terms for it exist in the
sources (Findings §7). And the book's rules chapter is a small, closed
engine while its Prompts demand roughly twenty operations the chapter never
defines (Findings §4.6), so a site that plays the book must decide things
the book left to the player.

> **Attack: "A PDF and two dice are a barrier" is not what the Findings
> show.** Two dice and a PDF are the equipment of every solo journaling
> game; the book itself says any random generator will do (R01) and any
> recording medium will do (R02). What the Findings show is a book with
> no record sheet, whose Prompts strike, unstrike, retype, lock and defer
> across six traits with rules the chapter never wrote down. The barrier
> is bookkeeping, and bookkeeping is the mechanism framing the Gardener
> recommended and the operator set aside.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The barrier sentence now says what the
> evidence supports. The operator's choice survives as the identity claim,
> because that is the work it actually does in the record: it decides the
> licence posture. The reason for keeping the operator's weight is stated
> in state 0003 as load-bearing, and nothing the Advocate read contradicts
> that it is load-bearing; it was only mislabelled as a problem.

> **Attack: "for anyone" names a problem the record cannot work on.**
> Everything in scope is buildable and testable privately. The only thing
> "anyone" adds is the gate, and the gate is a person outside the estate.
> A framing whose chosen problem is tested by someone else's reply is a
> framing that cannot be worked, only waited on.
> **Outcome: held, with the cost named.** The Framing's own "meanwhile"
> posture is the answer: the build does not wait, and the identity claim
> costs nothing until the day of the ask. What the attack adds is that the
> *ask* is therefore the test of "for anyone" (see the falsifier below),
> and the record should hold it as one.

> **Attack on rejecting "the sheet keeps itself".** The rejected framing
> is the only one the falsifier tests. If the operator stops playing, the
> likeliest reason is that keeping the sheet in the browser was not better
> than keeping it on paper; that is the mechanism thesis failing.
> **Outcome: bent; absorbed.** Not restored as *the* problem, because the
> operator's weight is on the identity, but recorded as the **test
> surface**: the sheet keeping itself is what play will exercise and what
> disuse will indict.

> **Attack on rejecting "a legacy that outlives the notebook".** The
> game's closing question is "what kind of songs will they sing about
> you?", and the explored Spark calls the share page "the web-native
> Song". If the Song is the point, legacy is the framing.
> **Outcome: held.** A legacy framing makes the product a place where
> things are kept, and the record has already refused to keep anything:
> no server, no account, nothing stored. Legacy as a *feature* is
> coherent; legacy as *the problem* contradicts the constraints. One
> observation survives the attack and is recorded under scope: the share
> page is the only part of the site that carries no word of the author's.

## Who it is for

- **Anyone who wants to play Rivers and Lakes**, on a laptop or a phone,
  without a PDF and dice. This is the framing's audience, and its cost is
  named: "on a phone" is a commitment to journaling on a phone keyboard,
  and "no sync" means **one game lives on one device** unless the player
  carries the file. Both are accepted, not hidden.
- **The operator first.** Publishing waits on terms; private play does
  not. The operator's first test is one player, and the *ask* is the
  second test (see below). A tool for anyone whose first test is one
  player.
- **Wym Lawson**, whose text it is, is not the audience but the gate. The
  gate has no known address: the only attribution in the sources is a
  credits page (Findings §7), and finding a channel is part of the
  `research` the record already owes. The Findings name a second author,
  Tim Hutchings, whose system this is; whether his terms for derivatives
  of Thousand Year Old Vampire touch a web edition of a hack is unknown to
  this record and belongs in the same `research`. The shape of the ask is
  deliberately open until there is something to show.

> **Attack: the Framing has a posture for "yes" and for nothing else.**
> An ask returns yes, yes-with-conditions, no, or silence, and for a
> small-press author silence is the common case. On "no" the site is a
> private tool forever and "for anyone" is falsified by the gate, not by
> disuse. On silence the same, after a time nobody has named. The explored
> Spark shelved bring-your-own-book as "the fallback if permission is
> refused", but the Framing does not say when silence becomes refusal, or
> that the fallback is not licence-clean either: an engine whose
> operations table encodes all 222 entries (Findings §4.3) is a structural
> derivative of the Prompts even with the prose removed, and the 84 oracle
> tables are the author's text as much as the Prompts are.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The audience section now names the second
> author and the missing channel. The three outcomes other than "yes" are
> filed as an open question for `envision` and `decide`: what the record
> does on refusal, and after how long of silence. The Advocate does not
> answer it; the operator said the ask's shape waits on something to show,
> and that is a defensible sequencing, but "what to show" and "what to do
> if the answer is no" are different questions and only the first has been
> deferred on purpose.

> **Attack: showing the author a finished verbatim edition is showing them
> a fait accompli.** Some authors are delighted; some read it as "I have
> already done the thing I am asking permission for."
> **Outcome: held, as a risk the operator chose.** "Built fully and shipped
> nowhere public" is private use of a purchased book, and the Advocate
> found nothing to say it is more than that. The manner of the showing is
> the operator's, and the record leaves it open on purpose.

## In scope

- The whole game, and "whole" is heavier than one line: Xiake creation in
  the book's order (birth Story, the first-reputation Story from three
  Tags, three trait Stories; Findings §5 E); the 80 Prompts with their
  entries verbatim; d10 − d6 movement with visit counting; the six traits
  plus Songs; the game-over conditions; the book's content guidance and
  its **skip for uncomfortable topics** (R03), which is a rule and not a
  courtesy; both play modes switchable at any time. It includes an
  alternate identity that is a second full sheet inside the first (21A), a
  Resource that can only be lost when it is the last one (35A), an effect
  deferred to a later repetition of an act (68C), and whole-sheet
  operations that strike everyone from a place (1A, 54B, 70A).
- An **advisory rules engine**: it reads the landed entry's instructions
  as proposed sheet changes, applies what the player confirms, flags
  illegal states, and never refuses a sheet change. **Every entry's
  proposal is a reading**: the engine cannot show a proposal without
  having encoded each of the 222 entries as operations, so the labelling
  regime that covers the twenty book-undefined operations covers the
  encoding of every entry, and the verbatim prose is always beside the
  proposal so the player can see where the site's reading and the book's
  words part. Every operation the Prompts demand and the rules never
  define is a named engine operation citing the Prompt that demands it.
  Player-judged selectors are player choices.
- **Game over is declared, not enforced.** The book ends the game when a
  required check or loss cannot be satisfied (R53, R54) and asks the
  player to narrate the demise. The engine declares the end and marks the
  sheet ended; whether a player may write past that mark is the one place
  "never refuse" needed an exception and did not have one. The Advocate's
  reading — declare and mark — is offered, not decided.
- Readings the book leaves open, taken for the engine and labelled as
  readings: two Technique axes (type and reputation); both readings of
  "use a Technique with none Acquired" offered; zero rolls and clamps to
  Prompt 1 count as landings; skips chain forward across exhausted Prompts.
- Dice rolled in the browser by default, with manual entry for physical
  dice. The engine sees two integers. This is not an addition: the book
  permits any random generator (R01).
- The oracle tables, rolled in-site with results inserted into the field
  being filled: 42 tables and 5 composition procedures per language half,
  84 tables in all (Findings §6). The Mandarin half is a structural mirror
  of the Cantonese half — same dice, same counts, same prose — so shipping
  it costs little engine and a great deal of proof-reading that the
  Findings did not do (§8). One table (the two-column Verb on page 16) has
  no stated rolling procedure and needs a labelled reading like the rules
  do.
- Local-first: no account, no server; the file is the save; export to a
  file. The consequence is accepted: an ordinary game is roughly
  thirty-five to forty-five answered entries (the Advocate's arithmetic
  from a mean roll of +2), which is several sittings, and a game that
  lives in one browser's storage is lost with that storage unless the
  player has exported it.
- A read-only share page for a Xiake's Legends and Songs, payload carried
  in the link, rendered by the same static site. Its properties are
  named: **unrevocable** (no server means no takedown; whoever holds the
  link holds the text), **unauthenticated** (the payload is data; anyone
  can decode, alter and re-issue a link), and **unbounded by rule** (a
  Song's description may be "a novella", R16). It carries no word of the
  author's, only the player's, so it is the one surface that could be
  public before terms exist — if its renderer can be deployed without the
  Prompts.
- A website that works on a phone.
- Meanwhile: built fully and shipped nowhere public, with the author's text
  — Prompts **and oracle tables** — loaded from a file the operator owns
  and kept out of the repository. Whether the engine's operations table
  for the 222 entries lives in the repository is an open question the
  build cannot proceed without answering (below).

> **Attack: "advisory: propose, flag, never refuse" is a form between the
> player and every Prompt.** On paper a checked Tag is a tick. In the site
> it is: read the proposal, pick which Tag, confirm. "Check a Tag" appears
> in 54 entries, conditional creation in 43, "Alternatively" in 19
> (Findings §4.5); the player-judged selectors ("a beloved Character", "a
> positive Tag") mean the engine must ask before it can propose. The
> playability risk is that the site is slower than a pencil, and that is
> exactly the failure the falsifier would report as "I stopped playing".
> **Outcome: held, as the design's central risk.** The posture is right —
> the book's looseness forbids refusing — but the Framing should not
> pretend the posture is free. It is recorded here as the thing the sheet
> layout must solve, and the reason the layout is not a cosmetic question.

> **Attack: "never refuse" and the end of the game cannot both be true.**
> If a sixth Legend is flagged but allowed, is a game over flagged but
> allowed? If yes, the game has no ending; if no, the engine refuses.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** A bullet now names the exception. The
> Advocate's proposed reading (declare and mark) follows the book, which
> ends the game by telling the player to narrate the end rather than by
> stopping their hand.

> **Attack: the proposal is a second text.** The site will ship verbatim
> prose *and* a hand-encoded reading of every entry. When they disagree,
> which is the book? The Framing labels twenty operations as readings and
> leaves 202 entries as if their encoding were mechanical. It is not: the
> Findings' table is "the Factor's normalisation" (§4.2) and says so.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** "Every entry's proposal is a reading" is now
> in scope. This is the sharpest thing the attack found: fidelity has a
> third leg beside the printed book and the text file — the site's
> operations table — and it is the one the player will actually
> experience.

> **Attack: "both modes switchable any time" hides a lock.** The book
> lets journal entries be adjusted freely but locks Legends except under
> Prompt instruction (R13). In Quick mode the Story is the whole answer,
> so a Quick player is locked out of editing their own words; in
> Journaling mode they may edit the passage and not the Story.
> **Outcome: held.** One save shape is sound — a Story always, a passage
> sometimes — and the advisory engine flags the lock rather than enforcing
> it. The asymmetry is the book's, not the site's, and carrying it is
> fidelity.

> **Attack: the link-carried share page.** Fragments are not sent to
> servers, which is the privacy the Framing wanted; but a fragment
> survives in history, in copy-paste, in every chat client that mangles
> long links, and in any shortener the player reaches for — at which
> point the payload sits on a third party's server, and "nothing stored"
> is false by the player's own hand. The content is the game's content:
> abuse, self-harm, dead children (R03). A link is a publication with no
> unpublish. And "read-only" is a rendering choice, not a property of the
> data.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The bullet now names the three properties.
> The Framing was right that this keeps local-first intact; it was wrong
> to count "nothing stored" only as a virtue. The observation that the
> page carries no author text is the attack's gift to the record: it may
> be the first public thing.

> **Attack: "the whole game" on one line.** The Framing lists the game as
> seven clauses. The Findings show a recursive sheet (21A), locks,
> deferred effects, whole-sheet strikes, 84 tables, an unproof-read
> Mandarin half, a table with no rolling rule, and the skip rule the
> Framing's list omitted. A stranger sizing the work from the Framing
> would be wrong by a multiple.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The scope bullet now says what "whole"
> weighs. The boundary itself holds: nothing in the list is optional if
> the site is an edition, and the identity claim is what makes it
> non-optional. The Mandarin question is reframed from "ship or not" to
> "who proof-reads it", which is the question that actually has a cost.

## Out of scope

Stated by the operator as certain:

- Accounts, login, cloud sync.
- Native mobile apps.
- Other games in the Thousand Year Old Vampire family. One game; the
  engine is not a platform.

**Not yet in scope, and not built until decided: generated prose** (a
model writing Stories, passages or Songs). The operator declined to rule
it out when offered, and the Advocate does not rule it out either. But the
Framing's own rules already fence it: the constraints carried in forbid any
external service the site cannot run without, so generated prose could
only ever be optional and non-critical; and the revised reading of
"nothing added" below excludes anything that answers a Prompt for the
player. "Not excluded" is not "in"; a stranger reads this as: do not build
it.

> **Attack: leaving generated prose open is a hole a stranger falls
> into.** "Not excluded" invites a builder to add a model dependency to a
> site whose constraints forbid dependencies.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The open question is preserved as the
> operator left it, and the fence around it is now stated so that the
> question can stay open without the build being ambiguous.

## The tension that makes it interesting

**To be the book it needs the author's text; to play the book it must
define what the author left to the player.** Fidelity pulls both ways at
once. Verbatim Prompts were chosen over bring-your-own-book knowing that
publication is then blocked on an outside party, because anything less is
not the book. And an engine that only implemented the rules chapter could
not run 20 of the operations the Prompts demand, so the site must add
definitions and readings the book does not print, while claiming to be the
book. The advisory posture is the chosen way to live with the second half:
propose, flag, never refuse, and label every reading as one — and, after
the attack, **every entry's proposal is a reading**, not only the twenty.

> **Attack.** The Advocate tried to break this sentence and could not. The
> only pressure that landed was that it understates itself: the engine
> does not add twenty things to the book, it reads all 222 entries, and
> the "book" the player meets is the prose, the text file's labels, and
> the site's proposals side by side.
> **Outcome: held, and sharpened.** This is the sentence a stranger should
> build from.

The Spark's "nothing is added that the paper game does not do" is no longer
carried as an unamended tension. The Advocate counts the additions the
record has already chosen: the twenty named operations, the readings, the
operations table for every entry, the share page, a sheet layout (the book
has none), entry letters if shown, and an applied-operation history. Dice
in the browser are not an addition (R01). The rule the sentence was
reaching for is: **nothing that answers a Prompt for the player, and
nothing the paper game forbids.** Bookkeeping and projection are permitted;
content is not. Under that rule every chosen addition is legal and
generated prose is the first thing the rule refuses — which is why that
question is worth the operator's deciding rather than the record's
inferring.

> **Attack: a framing that keeps a sentence it knows to be false is
> carrying a debt, not a tension.** The explored Spark said the sentence
> "survives with two named exceptions"; the Framing said "unamended". Two
> exceptions became seven on an honest count.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** Replaced with the rule the sentence meant.
> The Advocate notes this is a revision of the Spark's words, which is why
> it is written here and the Spark is untouched.

The remaining small tension rides along: fidelity to the printed book
against fidelity to the text file the site would ship from (A/B/C labels,
21 semicolons, the count of 80 read off the pages). It is small and it is
real; the Findings §4.1 verified the words match, so what is at stake is
labels and punctuation, and the site must pick which it shows.

## Constraints carried in

From the operator's standing preferences, unchanged: functional style,
pure functions over immutable sheet state, no classes; explicit
documentation for every function from the lowest abstraction up; no
external service the site cannot run without.

> **Attack.** None landed. One observation: "no external service the site
> cannot run without" is doing more work than the Framing credited it for
> — it is the fence around generated prose and the reason the share page
> cannot use a shortener of its own.
> **Outcome: held.**

## The falsifier

The Framing's falsifier is the operator's own: "if I stop playing it
myself". The Advocate's test is whether that sentence would fire on a
failure of what the Framing claims.

> **Attack.** It fires on success. A solo journaling game is finite: the
> Xiake dies, the player closes the book, and may not open it again for a
> year. "Stopping" is the natural end of a game, not evidence against the
> site. It also tests the wrong claim: disuse indicts "the sheet keeps
> itself" (the rejected framing) and says nothing about "the book"
> (fidelity — an operator could happily keep playing a site that had
> drifted from the text) or about "for anyone" (nobody else has to play
> for the operator to keep playing). And it has no date and no check,
> where the estate's own project falsifier has both (`system/FALSIFIERS.md`).
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** A Framing is not a Horizon and does not owe a
> dated falsifier; that is `envision`'s work. But a Framing that names a
> falsifier should name one that tests its own claims, so the record now
> carries three, one per claim, for `envision` to date:

- **The sheet keeps itself** is falsified if the operator, having finished
  one Xiake in the site, starts the next on paper or in the PDF. Not "if
  I stop playing"; *if I go back*.
- **The book** is falsified if a player finishes a game and cannot tell,
  from the site alone, where the book's words end and the site's readings
  begin. The labelling regime is what this tests.
- **For anyone** is falsified if the site is finished and the ask is never
  made — because then access was not the problem, the operator was — or
  if the ask is made and refused, in which case the identity claim fails
  at the gate and bring-your-own-book is what the record has left.

The third is the one the Framing could not see from inside the session:
**the ask is the falsifier of "for anyone"; disuse is the falsifier of
"the sheet keeps itself".** They were being asked to do each other's work.

## What a stranger can do with this

Build the engine and the sheet's **data model** now from the Findings and
this framing. The engine's tests can run against fixtures, because a
stranger does not have the operator's text file; what a stranger needs
first, and the Framing did not give, is the **loader contract** — the shape
of the file the operator owns, for Prompts and for oracle tables — and a
decision on whether the **operations table** for the 222 entries lives in
the repository (it is not the prose, but it is a derivative of it). Without
the first the site cannot load the book; without the second the stranger
cannot encode a single proposal.

Not: publish, decide the ask, add generated prose, or *choose* the sheet's
layout for the operator. But a stranger may *propose* a layout, and should,
because "build the sheet" and "do not choose the layout" cannot both be
instructions — a sheet without a layout is a schema — and because the
layout is where the advisory engine's friction is either solved or not.

> **Attack: the first session a stranger tries to build from this.** They
> open the Framing, read "build the engine and the sheet now … against the
> operator's local copy of the text", and have no copy, no file format, no
> operations table in the repo, and an instruction not to choose a layout
> for a thing called a sheet.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The paragraph above is what the stranger
> needed. The loader contract and the operations-table question are filed
> as open.

> **Attack: the first game a player tries to finish.** Forty entries over
> several evenings on a phone; the browser clears its storage; or the
> player picks up the laptop and finds no game there. Somewhere around the
> middle they land on 21A and the sheet becomes two sheets. Somewhere
> they override a proposal they think misreads the prose, and nothing
> shows that they did.
> **Outcome: held, with three consequences recorded.** One game per
> device is now stated. The alternate identity is now named in scope as
> a recursive sheet so it is not modelled flat. And state 0003's open
> question 4 — whether the applied-operation history is shown — is
> promoted from a detail to a fidelity question: a player who cannot see
> their own overrides cannot audit the site against the book, and the
> "book" falsifier above depends on it.

> **Attack: the first time the ask is made.** Covered under audience: the
> record has one posture for four outcomes, and the channel is unknown.
> **Outcome: bent; revised there.**

## Filed for the Steward

Attacks that held, in one line each, so the record does not re-fight them:
the identity claim as load-bearing (it decides the licence posture); the
rejection of legacy-as-problem; both modes from one save shape; dice both
ways as no addition; local-first with its cost; the out-of-scope list; the
constraints; the central tension, sharpened; the private build as private
use.

Attacks that bent the Framing, revised above: the barrier sentence; the
problem/identity conflation; the game-over exception to "never refuse";
every entry's proposal as a reading; the share link's three properties;
the weight of "whole"; the fence around generated prose; "nothing added"
restated as a rule; the falsifier, split into three; the stranger's first
hour.

Open questions this version adds to state 0003's six: what the record does
on refusal, and after how long of silence; the loader contract; whether
the operations table lives in the repository; whether the share renderer
can be deployed without the Prompts; who proof-reads the Mandarin half;
whether the end of the game may be written past.
