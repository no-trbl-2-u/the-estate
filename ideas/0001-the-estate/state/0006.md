# State 0006

## Established

T asked for the OKF announcement's content to be added to the bundle as its
own concept. Written as `reference/okf-rationale.md` — the format's stated
purpose, the concepts it represents, the bundle shape, its intended consumers,
and a scope table giving the test ADR 0019 turns on: *how is this document
read?* Opened and catalogued → frontmatter earns its keep. Injected verbatim →
it does not. Consumed by the machinery, or generating other files → its own
schema, already excluded by ADR 0017 decision 4.

Registered in `reference/index.md` beside the vendored spec and in
`reference/log.md`. Frontmatter parses, `type` present, every relative link
resolves — the conformance check ADR 0017 named.

## Decisions

- **Summarized, not vendored.** `okf-spec.md` stays the only verbatim mirror
  (Apache-2.0, attributed). The announcement is a blog post; the concept says
  so, cites it, and does not pretend to be it.
- **The announcement is not the spec, and the concept says where they
  differ.** The blog lists `timestamp` among the frontmatter fields; the
  vendored v0.2 spec uses the actor convention and `stale_after:`. Precedence
  is stated explicitly: vendored spec over announcement, `system/` over both.
- **No `stale_after:`.** The summary tracks v0.2 and will rot when the spec
  moves, but by no estimable schedule — and ADR 0018 sets that field only when
  the shelf life is honestly estimable. The concept says to treat it as stale
  the moment the vendored spec is revised.

## Gold nuggets

- The bundle now answers *what OKF is* (vendored spec) and *what it is for and
  where it stops* (this concept) as two separate documents. The second is the
  one that was load-bearing for a decision, and it was the one nobody had
  written down.
- A reference concept that records where its own source disagrees with a more
  authoritative one is more useful than a tidier concept that hides it.

## Tensions
