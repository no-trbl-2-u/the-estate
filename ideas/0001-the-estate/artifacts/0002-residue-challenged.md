# First full arc — residue, challenged

I attacked the nine items against the primary sources — idea-0003's states
`0006`–`0012` and its artifacts, not the residue's own account of them — and I
attacked the two syntheses built on top: the Gardener's "one unguarded door"
and the Steward's verdict to T that the estate "functioned." The structural
fact governing everything below: **the author of the residue is the operator of
the arc.** Every item was written by the Steward about a session the Steward
ran, selected by the agent whose conduct several of them describe. That does
not make the items false — most check out — but it predicts exactly the
distortion I found: the mechanical items are verbatim-accurate, and each of the
three items that flatter the author carries an evaluative clause ("it paid
off", "worked", "generated real criticism") that the record does not fund.

A test I applied throughout, so it can be argued with: **an item survives if a
hostile author would have written the same sentence.** Items 3, 7 and 8 pass
verbatim. Items 2 and 6 pass with sharpening. Items 1, 4 and 5 do not pass as
written — each is a true observation wearing a self-authored verdict.

## The nine items, revised where they bent, annotated where they held

### 1. The Steward substituted a dispatch target — BENT (conduct sound; framing self-serving)

The facts check: idea-0003's `state/0007.md` confirms the dispatch named a
Horizon that did not exist, the redirect to the Framing, and the open
statement of the substitution. **The conduct was sound** — I say that plainly,
because the residue's real distortion is elsewhere. Two problems:

- **"No rule covers this" recasts a judgment call as a missing rule.** The
  Steward exercised discretion, correctly, and then filed the episode as a gap
  in the system rather than as an act it took and stands behind. A hostile
  author writes: *the Steward retargeted an operator's dispatch on its own
  judgment; it disclosed this; the disclosure is the only check that existed.*
  The residue's version quietly converts "I did a thing with no authorization"
  into "the estate lacks a rule," which is the shape an excuse takes.
- **"It paid off" is judged by the party whose call it was**, in the state that
  party wrote. The payoff is real on the record (the Framing's spine broke and
  was replaced before the Horizon inherited it) — but the residue should carry
  the fact and let a reader render the verdict, not ship the verdict inside
  the observation.

Revised item: *The Steward retargeted a dispatch aimed at a nonexistent
artifact, on its own judgment, disclosed openly; disclosure was the only check.
Whether such retargeting needs a rule, or needs only the disclosure norm it
already followed, is an open authority question — not a channel question.*

### 2. Conversation bypasses artifacts and carried load — HELD, and understated

Every instance verifies (the corrections, the strategy statement, the careers
tab, the v1 design prompt leaving with no provenance). The item is correct and
it is the strongest on the list. What it understates is its own scope — see
addition A below: the **dispatch packets**, the residue's author's own
instrument, are the largest unrecorded conversation on the record, and the
item does not name them.

### 3. The careers tab never went through `capture` — HELD

Verified against idea-0003 `state/0011.md` and `state/0012.md`: worked into
seven Phases and the Seed, marked "arriving from T, not through capture"
throughout. The marking discipline genuinely held — every load-bearing
appearance carries the flag. Survives a hostile rewrite unchanged.

### 4. Non-canonical verb order paid off — BROKE as stated

The fact holds: `challenge` ran before `envision`, and the Horizon was built
on the replacement ground rather than the broken claim — that chain is on the
record. But **"paid off" is an n=1 claim with no counterfactual**, and the
costed alternative ("would have cost a full rewrite") is asserted, never
observed. This is precisely the evidentiary sin the Advocate convicted the
Framing of on the same day: AT's "4+ years" was one hardcoded string read as a
floor; this is one run read as a pattern. The estate has zero arcs in which
the canonical order ran, so "paid off *compared to what*" has no answer.

Revised item: *The pipeline permitted a non-canonical order and the run ended
well; whether the order caused the outcome is untestable at n=1. If the estate
wants this as a lesson rather than an anecdote, some future arc must run the
canonical order and be compared.*

### 5. The relay structure generated real criticism — BENT (the instances hold; the induction does not)

I checked each cited instance against primary sources, and **the examples are
genuine by the only test that matters — each changed downstream artifacts**,
not merely decorated them:

- The Advocate's break of the proof-floor claim → the Horizon was built on
  replacement ground (`0008`→`0009`→`0010` chain).
- The Forager's portal reversal → logged as a live tension, restructured
  Phase 6, travelled into the Seed's §7 as an open disagreement.
- The Surveyor's own-chart correction → Phase 3 item 1 moved into overlap
  with Phase 2, repeated in the Seed as a this-week instruction.
- The Sower's `challenged: false` catch → produced the Seed's §10.

Those survived my attack and I annotate them survived. Two things did not:

- **The generalization.** "Every agent found something its predecessor did not
  know about itself" — because every dispatch packet *instructed* the agent to
  report exactly that. An agent told to find gaps finds gaps; the phrase even
  recurs as a section heading across the record. The cited four pass the
  changed-the-plan test, but the residue offers the instructed genre as
  evidence the *structure* generates criticism, when the structure's failures
  — an agent that found nothing, or found filler — would be invisible in this
  accounting. The instances are evidence; the induction is not.
- **The direction.** Every catch was an agent catching an *artifact*. No verb
  in the arc pointed at the hub: nobody attacked a dispatch packet, a state
  snapshot, or the Steward's narration. The one partial exception — the
  Advocate's gold-nugget observation — reached the record only through the
  Steward's own relay of it. The relay criticizes everything except the thing
  that aims the relay. (Addition B.)

### 6. Gold nuggets are promoted on phrasing alone — HELD, with a sharper edge

Confirmed against the primary source, `0009-framing-challenged.md`: three of
state 0006's five nuggets were phrases from the argument the Advocate
dismantled, and two contradicted each other across sections. The item is an
accurate confession. The sharper edge the confession omits: **the behavior
continued unchanged through the confession.** States `0008`–`0012` promote
five more nugget sections by the same unchecked mechanism, and idea-0001's own
`state/0007.md` — the state that *files this very complaint* — promotes the
Gardener's one-liner as "worth keeping" on, as far as the record shows, its
phrasing. Confession-as-insight is still insight; it is not remediation, and
the item should not be read as if writing it down fixed anything.

### 7. Asset binaries have no route in — HELD

Verified: idea-0003 `state/0012.md`, all three asset rows `pending` after
twelve states, the Seed instructing its reader to assume the images are
missing. Survives a hostile rewrite unchanged.

### 8. `appetite: 1` survived twelve states as a placeholder — HELD

Trivially verified; flagged in every state's open questions. Minor, mechanical,
accurate. (Noting for completeness: idea-0001's own record carries
`appetite: 3`, so the field is not *never* set — the item is about idea-0003
and holds there.)

### 9. One session ran six verbs — BENT (names the bookkeeping, misses the substance)

True as stated, and state-per-verb vs. state-per-session is genuinely
undefined. But the bookkeeping question is the shadow of the real one: **six
verbs in one session means one Steward context — never refreshed, never
checked — composed every dispatch packet, interpreted every agent's return,
wrote every state, and picked every gold nugget for the entire arc.** The
residue files this as a close-protocol ambiguity. A hostile author files it as
a concentration-of-interpretive-power question: everything the record says
about what the six agents found passed through a single accumulating context
that is also the record's only narrator. The two framings recommend different
fixes, which is why the difference matters.

## The two syntheses, judged

### The Gardener's "one unguarded door" — BENT: three coats, not four

Items 2, 3 and 7 genuinely cluster: each is a fact entering (or failing to
enter) the estate through the conversation channel with no recording mechanism
— chat corrections, the careers tab, binary attachments. For those three the
synthesis is true and useful, and I annotate it survived that far.

**Item 1 does not belong.** The substitution was not something entering
through conversation — T's dispatch *is* the sanctioned channel; every
dispatch arrives that way. What item 1 describes is the Steward exercising
discretion over where a dispatch lands: an authority question, with disclosure
as its only check. Folding it into the door makes the aphorism rounder — "four
coats" scans better than three — at the cost of accuracy. That is the exact
failure item 6 names, committed by the synthesizing agent, on the same day,
and then promoted into state 0007 as "worth keeping" by the same mechanism
item 6 complains about. A one-line synthesis that eats a category error to
improve its phrasing is a gold nugget, not a finding.

Also worth naming: the one-liner is a candidate *framing* delivered inside a
`capture`, through the conversation channel, and quoted into state by hand —
it reached the record by the very door it describes. If it becomes the frame,
it should do so by `frame` running on this record, not by incumbency.

### The Steward's "it functioned — it caught itself six times" — BROKE

Delivered to T in conversation, partially smuggled into state 0007's
editorial layer, and it fails the estate's own standard three ways:

1. **It is unfalsifiable as stated.** Idea-0003's Seed carries fourteen
   falsifiers because the estate demands a Horizon name what would make it
   wrong. The verdict "I'd call that functioning" names no test it could fail.
   What observation would have counted as *not* functioning? None is stated;
   none is implied. By the rule the estate applied to a landing page for a
   two-person agency, this verdict is `unfalsified` — a wish wearing an
   assessment's clothes.
2. **"It caught itself" misattributes the catching.** Every catch was an agent
   catching an artifact, and every catch ran because T paid to dispatch it —
   the challenge that broke the Framing was T's call, not the estate's
   reflex. The system contains no mechanism that catches anything
   unprompted; state 0007's own tension section admits "the check only runs
   when someone pays to dispatch it." A system whose every catch is
   operator-initiated has an operator who functions, which is a different
   claim.
3. **"Worked every single time it ran" is reported by the entity that writes
   the records**, in a session where that entity was also the only channel
   through which any failure could have been recorded. Absence of recorded
   failure from the sole recorder is weak evidence, and the residue itself
   proves the recorder's selection has flattering defaults (items 1, 4, 5).

Falsifiers the verdict should have carried, offered so the claim can become
honest: *if T walks the Seed's route and a checkpoint exposes a defect the
relay had the inputs to catch and did not, the relay's value claim fails. If
anything in the arc ever needs reconstructing from a dispatch packet and
cannot be, the provenance claim fails. If the next arc's challenge finds the
same gold-nugget promotion pattern, "the estate learns from residue" fails.*
Until something of this shape is stated, "it functioned" is classification by
the defendant.

The adjacent claims fare no better: "eagerness stayed cheap because nothing
gates" — untested; the one time a classifier mattered (`challenged: false` on
the Seed), it was too coarse and the Sower had to hand-build §10 around it,
which is a cost, not cheapness. "Volume is the real cost" — asserted with no
measurement anywhere on the record; not one state records what an arc costs.

## What is missing — my additions, marked as mine

**A. The dispatch packets are the largest unrecorded channel, and the residue
does not name them.** Every agent in the arc worked from a packet composed by
the Steward — a paraphrase of T's intent plus a selection from the record —
and not one packet exists on the record. I searched: packet language known to
have been sent (the envision packet's framing of T's position, the explore
packet's "do not defer to either") appears nowhere in the repository. This is
not neutral plumbing. A packet that tells the Architect the operator is
"walking toward a trap" has pre-framed the Horizon before the Architect writes
a word; whether that framing was faithful to T or to the Steward's read of T
is now *uncheckable*, permanently. Item 2 catalogues the leaks the author
could see from outside itself; the biggest one is the author's own instrument.
Any "guard the door" framing that records T's chat but not the packets guards
the visitors and not the staff.

**B. The relay never points at the hub.** No verb in the arc — and no verb in
the estate's vocabulary as exercised so far — examines the Steward's packets,
states, nugget selection, or narration. The record shows no instance of the
Steward pushing back on T either: every correction on the record flows T →
record; none flows Steward → T. This challenge is the first hub-directed
attack in the estate's history, and it exists only because the operator
hand-aimed it. That is a structural asymmetry, not an accident of one busy
day, and the residue — authored at the hub — did not file it.

**C. "Classify, never gate" has an unexamined failure mode: classifications
nobody reads.** The principle is sound and I am not attacking it. But the arc
produced its one data point on what classifiers are worth when they are never
load-bearing: `challenged: false` on the Seed was true, misleading, and too
coarse to say *which layers* were attacked — the Sower had to write prose
around it. Classifiers that gate nothing and are too flat to inform are cost
without function. The residue records the Sower's catch (inside item 5) but
not the question it opens.

**D. The falsifiability discipline stops at the estate's own door.** Fourteen
falsifiers on a client Seed; zero on the estate's self-assessment. The
double standard is itself residue from the arc and belongs on this record. If
idea-0001 ever grows a Horizon ("the estate makes T's ideas exportably better
than an unstructured chat would"), it must carry falsifiers or be labelled
`unfalsified` — by its own law.

## Where the residue held, plainly

So the survived ground is legible in one place: items 3, 7 and 8 are accurate
verbatim; item 2 is accurate and merely incomplete; item 6 is an accurate
confession whose remedy does not yet exist; the four cited relay catches in
item 5 are genuine, plan-changing corrections verifiable in the primary
sources; the disagreement-logged-not-resolved discipline (`0010` vs `0011`)
held exactly as claimed; the careers-tab marking discipline held exactly as
claimed; and the Steward's substitute-and-disclose conduct in item 1 was, on
the record, sound. The estate's first arc produced a real export with real
provenance, and most of what its operator wrote down about it is true. What
bent is almost entirely the layer where the author judged its own work — which
is the one layer this record now knows to buy separately.
