### The Architect's central move

The feature-richness-versus-no-stubs tension is **dissolved, not traded off.**
Every feature in the reference set sorts into two species:

- **Assertion machines** — a surface claiming something the visitor cannot see.
  Foxy's LIVE dashboard, its toasts, its chat input, its calculator; AT's press
  logos; Eros's login stack. These need a *business* behind them to be true,
  which is why all of them are hollow, and why building them early is exactly
  the failure the no-stub rule names.
- **Disclosure instruments** — a surface showing something that already exists
  and letting her check it. Needs no machine behind it, only a fact in front
  of it.

**Starvu is poor in track record and unusually rich in facts.** So the
constraint inverts from a budget into a generator: build every feature out of
something already true and the no-stub rule cannot be violated by construction
— which means T can have **as many features as he likes.**

### What the page is at six months

One long, dense, expensive-looking instrument that does what no competitor's
site does: **it discloses the whole business up front** — the entity, the man,
the agreement she would sign, the admission standard, the split, the 90-day
exit — and then books fifteen minutes against real availability. By month six
it has grown three things it could not have had on day one: **photographs of
real creators, Starvu's own small checkable numbers, and a FAQ made of the
questions creators actually asked on calls.** It is not short. **It is long the
way a prospectus is long.**

### The inventory, split three ways

- **Real on day one — twelve.** Identity block; the agreement on the page with
  clause headings and a download; the eligibility standard verbatim; an
  interactive eligibility check (arithmetic over a real policy — and it doubles
  as the category-standard second qualifier); the first ninety days drawn as a
  schedule; services in the present tense; booking against **true** availability
  (few slots because there are few — the only honest scarcity device in the
  record); a named-agenda confirmation with the agreement attached; WhatsApp
  fallback with pre-filled message; the "How did you hear about Starvu?" field;
  per-channel link tags; the compliance surface.
- **Real soon, each blocked on one named event — six.** Creator photographs
  (permission landing — **the highest-value unlock**); one creator sentence with
  no figure in it; Starvu's own numbers (one month of operating and writing it
  down); the published split (**the friend's business decision, not ours**); the
  real FAQ (~ten calls plus a notes habit); a services page as a route (one
  service delivered end to end so it can be described from a real week).
- **Not yet — seven.** Earnings calculator; testimonials with figures; any
  "live" dashboard; social-proof toasts; press logo wall; chat input of any kind
  (**WhatsApp is the permanent answer**); portal/login/blog/lead magnet/referral.

## Decisions

- **The earnings calculator is refused** — at v1 and at month six, in the
  generator form the category uses. The Framing left it on the line as a design
  call; design calls are The Architect's, and he took it. **Replaced by the
  split table**: what Starvu takes, what she keeps, what Starvu pays for.
- **Two riders on day one**, both requiring the friend's word first: publishing
  the agreement and publishing his name.
- **The services page moved off v1** to the soon tier, gated on one service
  delivered end to end.
- **A v1 design prompt went to T directly**, built from the flyer, for
  claude.ai/design. It is not an artifact on this record.

## Gold nuggets

- **"Assertion machines" vs. "disclosure instruments."** The whole sorting, in
  two phrases.
- **"It needs no machine behind it, only a fact in front of it."**
- **"Starvu is poor in track record and unusually rich in facts."**
- **"It is long the way a prospectus is long."**
- **"The category answers *what could you make?* with a fantasy; Starvu answers
  *what would this cost you?* with a fact."**
- **"Few slots because there are few"** — true scarcity, the only honest version
  of a device the whole category fakes.
- **"A calculator is an assertion machine with a slider"** — the cheapest
  possible way to turn the most verifiable business in the set into the least,
  in an afternoon.
