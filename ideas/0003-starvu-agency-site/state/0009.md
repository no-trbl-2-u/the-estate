# State 0009

## Established

T opened a new dimension on the record: **what AI tooling could be built for the
friend's venture** — in the repo, in the website, or for the clients — and
*"I'm starting to think v2 of the website will need a login service."* The
Forager developed the Horizon along all three surfaces without collapsing them,
because `0010`'s honesty test binds each with wildly different force.

> **Internal tooling makes no claim to anyone, so the assertion/disclosure test
> does not reach it at all. The marketing page is the most tightly bound surface
> on the record. A signed creator is not a stranger being persuaded — she is a
> counterparty checking that a promise is being kept, and showing her that is
> disclosure.**

### Surface 1 — internal tooling: yes, and nearly all the value is here

Fifteen candidates developed with costs and dependencies. The finding that makes
them belong to this record rather than being a side quest: **four of `0010`'s six
"real once one cheap thing exists" rows are blocked on somebody writing something
down, and nothing on the record produces the writing.** Internal tools are the
supply line to the page. *Build the tools that produce facts, because this page
is made of facts.*

### Surface 2 — AI on the marketing site: essentially no, and the refusal got stronger

`0010` refused chat as a **stub**. The Forager closed a door the Architect left
ajar: **a working AI chat satisfies the no-stub rule as literally written and is
worse.** *Foxy's chat input lies by doing nothing; an AI chat input would lie by
doing something* — unreviewed representations about an agreement, a split and an
earnings expectation, at 2am, per visitor, in prose that sounds like a person
promising. The question it would be asked most (*what could I make?*) is exactly
the question `0010` refused to let a slider answer.

**One exception, defended:** a **retrieval-only clause finder** over the
published Talent Management Agreement. It generates nothing — it scrolls to and
highlights the actual clause, verbatim. The proof it is a disclosure instrument:
**remove the AI and replace it with keyword search, and the feature still works,
just worse.** *Remove the AI from a chat widget and you have Foxy's corpse.* Its
most valuable output is its failures — queries the document cannot answer are a
list for the friend's lawyer.

The reframe worth keeping: **the right place for AI on the marketing site is the
repository, not the page.**

### Surface 3 — the v2 login: earned, and earlier than `0010` said

The Forager's main disagreement with the Architect, and it is precise.
**`0010` sorted the portal by category evidence rather than by its own test.**
Its evidence — Foxy's 404s, AT's SPA catch-all, Eros's unlinked "Copy of
Template" stack — is entirely evidence about *a login on a marketing site with
nobody behind the door*. That is an assertion machine with a password field and
should be refused forever.

**A client portal is a different object that shares a word.** Run `0010`'s own
test on it: her signed agreement, her verification status, her trial clock, her
weekly numbers, her takedown log. Does it claim something she cannot see? No.
**It is a disclosure instrument with an audience of one** — the Architect's
central move, pointed through the door.

**The resolution that keeps every word of `0010`'s refusal true:** the portal is
real, and **the public site never links to it.** No `/login` in the nav, no
account offered to a stranger; the URL arrives on signing or by magic link.
*Eros's failure was not having a login — it was advertising one with nothing
behind it.*

**What earns it, as a trigger rather than a date:** **the second signed creator
plus one deliverable that repeats.** With one creator a portal is a worse
WhatsApp; the economics flip on more than one recipient and weekly rather than
once. The flyer already promises weekly meetings, so the repeating deliverable
is contractual rather than speculative — plausibly **month two or three**, which
puts the portal in `0010`'s middle tier, not the far horizon.

**The build is a weekend. The cost is not the build** — it is that the portal is
the first thing in this business that can leak. That produces a design rule:
build it so a total breach discloses as little as possible. No legal names
(vendor holds identity, portal stores `verified: true`), no ID documents ever,
no warehoused earnings, magic-link auth, expiring URLs. **The constraint is the
design**, the same way the no-stub rule turned out to be a generator.

## The shortlist, and the ordering rule

Ordering rule, stated so it can be argued with: **facts produced per hour of T's
time, then hazard avoided.**

0. **Choose the verification vendor before any ID touches anything** — hours, a
   decision not a build. The only item where delay compounds.
1. **Call-notes capture** — an afternoon. Highest leverage on the record: funds
   the real FAQ, funds Starvu's own numbers, and **makes `0010`'s sharpest
   falsifier (*what made you book?*) operable instead of merely cheap.** The
   Architect wrote that falsifier knowing it was a habit; *sentences that cost
   nothing still do not get written.*
2. **Claims ledger + staleness check** — an afternoon. Converts falsifier 8 (the
   month-six no-stub audit) from memory into a build step, and is itself the
   purest disclosure instrument in the document.
3. **Weekly numbers pack** — a weekend. Produces the "own numbers" unlock `0010`
   names but has no mechanism for, and becomes the portal's first recurring
   payload.
4. **Takedown assistant, manual-assist** — a weekend, or buy the monitoring.
   Largest disclosure upside per hour.
5. **The thin portal, unlinked** — a weekend, only after 3 and 4 give it
   something to hold.
6. **Response-time meter** — an afternoon, but needs an exportable channel and a
   month of data.
7. **Clause finder** — an afternoon, no model in v1, gated on the friend
   publishing the agreement.
— **Chat drafting** — last, possibly never in the *send* direction.

> **Items 1 and 2 cost an afternoon each and change what the page can honestly
> say. Everything below costs a weekend and changes what the business can do.**

## Hazards that genuinely bite

- **Identity documents — build the tracker, never the vault.** Vendor-held,
  pass/fail, timestamp. The operational half matters as much: **IDs must not
  arrive over WhatsApp** — a photo of a licence lives in a phone backup
  indefinitely, outside anything T builds and past any retention policy. *If the
  current process is "text me a photo of your ID," changing that is worth more
  than any tool in the document.*
- **Doxxing is the actual threat model** — not fraud. A hostile party linking a
  stage name to a legal identity, where the consequence is physical. Cheap to
  decide at the start, expensive to retrofit.
- **Earnings figures — the calculator refusal generalised.** *Display, never
  project.* The refusal has to travel **through the login**, where it is *easier*
  to violate because the audience is friendly and a projection feels like a
  service.
- **Model-provider content policy — new to the record and structural.**
  Mainstream commercial model APIs prohibit generating adult sexual content,
  entirely separate from OnlyFans's rules. **This re-prices the tool everyone
  reaches for first:** chat drafting is not an afternoon, it is an infrastructure
  decision. The clean consequence — business-facing tools unblocked,
  creator-content-facing tools gated — is **the same ordering value alone would
  recommend.**
- **Platform automation, and who pays.** OnlyFans's automation ban lands on **her**
  account, not Starvu's. That converts a compliance question into a **fiduciary**
  one: *the downside is borne by the person who trusted you.*
- **Drafts versus sends** — the line must not be reachable by a config flag.
- **Pennsylvania is all-party consent** for recorded oral communications, unlike
  most states, so the notes tool is notes-*after*, not recording-*during*.
  Flagged for counsel, not asserted as legal advice.
