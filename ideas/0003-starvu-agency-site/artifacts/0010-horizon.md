# The Starvu page, six months in

I am The Architect. What follows is one drawing of one object: **the landing
page**, standing finished at month six. Not the web presence, not the business.
The Advocate left me a page that has to earn a stranger's trust by being
checkable rather than by claiming performance, and T left me a sentence — *as
many meaningful features as possible* — that sits directly on top of the
record's hardest surviving constraint. I take that collision as the commission.

Two corrections arrived after `0009` and both of them fund this drawing rather
than dent it. **The flyer is true as written** — Starvu LLC exists, the ID and
platform verification are real, the per-partner rule for couples is real, the
earnings disclaimer is real, the Fenix non-affiliation is real, and the Talent
Management Agreement is real and is handed over before signing. Every line in
the Advocate's verifiability inventory is publishable today. And **the business
is very early**: a few real creators, currently freelance, being bundled into
the company, with permission to publish their photographs coming soon.

Those two facts together are the whole design. One of them says the page can be
dense with substance on day one. The other says the page must have somewhere to
grow that is not decoration.

---

## The move that makes both halves of T's brief true at once

Read the reference set one more time and sort its features by what has to exist
for the feature to be honest.

Foxy's LIVE dashboard has to have a data pipeline behind it. It has none — zero
`fetch()` on the whole homepage. Foxy's social-proof toasts have to have a
stream of applications behind them. They have a hardcoded array with Sarah from
Miami in it. Foxy's chat input has to have a person behind it. The string
`faq-chat-send` appears in no script anywhere on the site. Foxy's ROI calculator
has to have outcome data behind it. Its own source comments call its constants
*"realistic and believable."* AT's press logos have to have press behind them.
The filenames begin `imageye___-_imgi_2_`. Eros's login stack has to have a
product behind it. It is titled "Copy of Template."

Every single one of those is the same species. **They are assertion machines: a
surface that claims a thing the visitor cannot see, and needs a business behind
it to be true.** Building one before the business exists is exactly the failure
the record's no-stub constraint names, and the category's most impressive sites
are almost entirely made of them.

There is a second species and the reference set contains almost none of it.
**A disclosure instrument does not claim anything the visitor cannot see; it
shows her something that already exists and lets her check it.** It needs no
machine behind it. It needs a fact in front of it.

Starvu has almost no track record and an unusually deep pile of facts: a real
entity in a real jurisdiction, a named man who answers the phone, a written
agreement that exists today, an admission policy with rules in it, a stated
90-day walk-away, a benefits list somebody wrote down and meant, and — very
soon — photographs of real people who have actually worked with him.

So the constraint stops being a budget and becomes a generator:

> **Build every feature out of something that is already true, and the no-stub
> rule cannot be violated by construction.** You can build as many as you like.

That is my answer to *what features?* — and it is why the answer is long rather
than short. The Framing wanted brevity and the Advocate showed brevity was
Foxy's inheritance, not Starvu's option. The page at month six is **not short**.
It is long the way a prospectus is long: nothing on it is padding, because
everything on it is a disclosure, and a disclosure that is deleted is just a
thing you have decided not to tell her.

## The page, working — three scenes

### Scene one: 11:40pm, a phone, one tap from a DM

She has been messaging with him for two days. He sends a link. She taps it
between two other conversations, standing up, one hand.

The first screen has his sentence on it — *You make the content. We run the
business around it.* — and beneath it, without scrolling, three things she can
check: **Starvu LLC, Pennsylvania**, a registration number, and a face with a
name under it. Not a stock photograph. Him. The primary action is *Book fifteen
minutes*, and next to it, quieter, *Read the agreement first*.

She does not book. Nobody books at 11:40pm from a standing start. She scrolls.

What she gets, in order, is **the whole shape of the arrangement before she is
asked for anything.** What Starvu does, in four blocks, each one written in the
present tense about work that is actually performed — chatting, advising,
content protection, runway and modeling opportunities — and each one ending in a
sentence about what it looks like in a week, not what it does for your brand.
Then **who Starvu will accept**: 18+, government photo ID, platform
verification, both partners individually if you are a couple. It is stated as an
admission standard, not as a legal footnote, and it is the first thing on the
page that reads as *expensive*, because a business that turns people away is a
business with a threshold.

Then the part no competitor has. **"What you would be signing."** The Talent
Management Agreement, present on the page — its clause headings expanded, the
term, the exit, the obligations both ways, and a button that downloads the
actual document. She does not read it. Almost nobody reads it. **The presence of
it is the message**, and she has three other agencies' sites in her memory where
the word "terms" appeared exactly once, in a footer, linking to a template.

Then **the first ninety days**, drawn as a schedule rather than promised as a
sentence: week one, what gets set up and by whom; the weekly meeting, on a day,
with named attendees; what "24/7 support" concretely means and what its actual
response time has been; and day 90, where the exit is — **you can walk, and here
is the clause that says so.** The flyer already promises this. The page turns it
into a calendar she can picture herself inside.

Then the money. **What Starvu takes, what she keeps, what Starvu pays for out of
its side.** One table. In the whole reference set exactly one number is
published by anyone.

Then the questions — the real ones, the ones creators have actually asked him on
calls, in their words, answered in his — and then the booking.

She closes the tab. She books at 9:15 the next morning, from the same link.

### Scene two: what the booking does, on both ends

She picks a slot. The slots are real: three of them this week, because he is one
man and three is what he has. **Scarcity that is true is the only scarcity
device in this record that is not a lie**, and it is the cheapest one to build —
it is just his calendar, connected.

She answers four things on the way through. Her platform and handle. Roughly
where her monthly revenue sits, in bands. What she most wants help with — the
list is Starvu's actual services, so the answer routes the call. And **"How did
you hear about Starvu?"**, which is the highest-value input on the page and
costs one field.

What she gets back is not a thank-you page. It is a **confirmation that names
the human, the length, and the agenda** — fifteen minutes, with him, and the
four things it will cover — plus the agreement attached, plus a WhatsApp thread
already opened with a message pre-filled, so that if something changes she can
say so in one tap instead of ghosting.

That last piece is not politeness. `0009` found the hazard nobody had priced:
**an open calendar is itself an affordance promising a prepared human at a named
time**, and three no-shows make anti-proof aimed at exactly the professional
this record cares about. The page cannot make him show up. It can make the
booking small, real, reminded, cancellable in one tap, and scarce enough that he
is never overbooked — and at month six that is a solved problem rather than an
open wound, because the slots were never more than he could keep.

On his end, the booking arrives with her handle, her band, her chosen service,
and where she came from. He opens the call already knowing what it is about.
That is the page doing work *after* its job is supposed to have ended.

### Scene three: month five, and the page has learned something

A creator he signed in month two tells a friend about him. The friend arrives
cold — no DM, no relationship, nothing but a name.

The page she lands on is the same page. What is different is what six months put
on it.

**There are photographs of real people on it now** — creators he actually works
with, published with their permission, which is the one asset in this entire
competitive set that is expensive to fake and cheap for Starvu to have honestly.
Foxy runs `freepik__` AI stock. AT scrapes its logos. Starvu has faces.

**There is a number on it that belongs to Starvu.** Not $124,000 a month. Small,
specific, checkable, and its smallness is not modesty — it is the point:
creators currently managed, calls taken, and the actual median time it took him
to answer a message last month. It is the only number in the category that its
own author could prove.

**The questions are hers.** The FAQ at month six is not invented; it is what
twenty creators actually asked on calls, transcribed and answered. That feature
did not exist on day one and *could not have* — and it is now richer than
anybody's, for free, because he was on the calls anyway.

**And one creator says one sentence** with her name and her face on it, about
what changed, containing no figure at all. One is enough. One that is true beats
three that are constants in a JavaScript bundle.

She books.

## The feature inventory

Three lists. The split is the deliverable — it is what tells T what to build
now, what to hold, and what to refuse no matter how good it looks.

### Real on day one

These need only the flyer, the friend, a calendar, and a form. Each is a
disclosure or a genuine mechanism; none asserts anything unseeable.

| # | Feature | Why it is real today |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identity block** — named human, his photograph, Starvu LLC, Pennsylvania, registration, contact | All true, all checkable, and Eros publishes none of it while AT and Foxy publish almost none |
| 2 | **The agreement, on the page** — clause headings, term, exit, obligations both ways, downloadable | The TMA exists and is already given before signing; publishing it earlier costs nothing and is unmatched in the set |
| 3 | **Eligibility standard** — 18+, government photo ID, platform verification, per-partner for couples | Verbatim from the flyer; it is Starvu's actual admission policy |
| 4 | **Eligibility check** — a short interactive "can I apply?" running on those published rules | Not a quiz over invented constants: it is arithmetic over a real policy, and it doubles as the category-standard second qualifier |
| 5 | **The first ninety days, as a schedule** — onboarding, the weekly meeting, support, the day-90 exit | Every element is a promise the friend already makes on paper; the page only draws it on a timeline |
| 6 | **Services, in the present tense** — chatting, advising, content protection, runway/modeling | Governed by *every service the site names is real*; content protection is named by nobody else in the set and is the record's live opportunity |
| 7 | **Booking against true availability** — few slots, because there are few | The scarcity is honest and the calendar sync is the mechanism, not the theatre |
| 8 | **Named-agenda confirmation** — the human, the length, the four things covered, the agreement attached | Defuses half of the no-show hazard `0009` found |
| 9 | **WhatsApp fallback with a pre-filled message** | Category-standard on all three references; the friend has a phone |
| 10 | **"How did you hear about Starvu?"**, required | One field. Settles warm-versus-cold empirically and permanently |
| 11 | **Per-channel link tags** — one query parameter per place he posts a link | Fifteen minutes, and it is the denominator the record does not have |
| 12 | **Compliance surface** — earnings-not-typical language, Fenix/OnlyFans non-affiliation, privacy, terms | Already on the flyer; a site carrying less than its own flyer got less careful as it got more public |

Two notes on this list, both load-bearing.

**Number 2 is a question before it is a build.** Publishing the agreement is the
friend's call, and `0009` amendment 3 already put the general form of it on the
errand: *will he put his name, the entity, and the terms on the site?* If the
answer is no, features 1 and 2 do not ship and this Horizon loses its spine.
That is one text message, and it is the cheapest thing on this record.

**Number 12 carries the caveat `0009` attached.** Earnings-not-typical language
presupposes an earnings claim. If the page carries the flyer's `$15K–$30K`
range, the disclaimer travels with it and must. If the page carries no number,
the disclaimer is furniture — and furniture is what we are avoiding.

### Real once one cheap thing exists

Each of these is blocked on exactly one named event. None is blocked on the
business being older in general — that is what separates this list from the
next.

| Feature | Blocked on | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| **Photographs of real creators** | permission landing | T says soon; this is the highest-value single unlock on the record |
| **A named creator's sentence, no figure** | one creator agreeing | one is enough; three is plenty |
| **Starvu's own numbers** — creators managed, calls taken, median response time | one month of operating and writing it down | small, true, checkable, and unmatched precisely because it is small |
| **The split, published** | the friend setting it | a business decision, explicitly out of this record's scope; the page can state it, never decide it |
| **The real FAQ** — actual questions from actual calls | ~10 calls and a habit of writing the questions down | costs a notes file; becomes the best FAQ in the category |
| **A services page as a route** | one service actually delivered end-to-end, so it can be described from a real week | the Framing left this on the line; it belongs here, not at v1 — a route with nothing behind it is thin, a route with a delivered week behind it is a case study without the word |

The discipline that makes this list safe: **each row names a trigger, and the
trigger is an event, not a date.** Nothing here is scheduled into existence. The
page ships without them and is complete; each one lands the week its fact does.

### Not yet — do not build these early, however good they look

| Feature | Why not | When, if ever |
|---|---|---|
| **Earnings calculator** | Refused. See below — this is the one I rule on rather than defer |
| **Testimonials with monthly figures** | The category's are literally constants in a bundle. Starvu's would be too | Only if a creator will state her own figure, with her name, and consent to it — and even then the disclaimer travels |
| **Any "live" dashboard or metrics surface** | There is no data pipeline; a `LIVE` pill over static markup is Foxy's exact defect | If a creator-facing product ever exists. Not on this page |
| **Social-proof toasts** | Requires a stream of applications. There isn't one, and a fabricated one is the cheapest possible way to become the least trustworthy business in the set | Never in hardcoded form |
| **Press / "as seen in" logo wall** | No press. AT's is scraped and the filenames say so | When press exists |
| **Chat input of any kind** | The record's most vivid warning. All three references route conversational intent to WhatsApp; Foxy fakes the widget instead of buying one | WhatsApp is the answer, permanently |
| **Creator portal, login, account** | Zero of three references have a working one and the only one that exists is an unstyled platform default nobody linked | Far horizon at the earliest |
| **Blog / lead magnet / referral programme** | All acquisition instruments, all far-horizon, and the referral one only becomes honest once there are signed creators to refer | Depends on the traffic answer; see the branch below |

### The calculator: refused, and this is my call

The Framing deliberately left the earnings calculator on the line as a *design*
call. I am taking it: **no calculator, at v1 or at month six, in the generator
form the category uses.**

The reason is structural rather than aesthetic. A calculator is an assertion
machine with a slider on it — it manufactures a personalised earnings claim for
every visitor, out of constants nobody can defend. Foxy's own source calls its
tiers *"realistic and believable,"* which is the sentence of a person who knows
they are not measured. Starvu's flyer already carries earnings-not-typical
language, which means **somebody at Starvu already understood this exposure
before there was a website.** Installing a machine that generates a fresh number
per visitor is the single cheapest way to convert the most verifiable business
in the reference set into the least, and it would do it in an afternoon, which
is exactly what makes it dangerous.

Two of three references have one. That makes it category furniture. It does not
make it a requirement, and the record's own Findings already class every number
on every one of these sites as **not evidence of anything true**.

**What replaces it, and it is better:** the split table. What Starvu takes, what
she keeps, what Starvu pays for. Real arithmetic over one real constant, once
the friend sets it. Only one site in the whole set publishes a price at all.
Where the category answers *what could you make?* with a fantasy, Starvu answers
*what would this cost you?* with a fact — and that is the question a professional
evaluating a vendor is actually asking.

The refusal is falsifiable and I say how below.

## Where the page branches on warm versus cold

One place, as instructed, because T wants momentum and not a fork.

**The page is the same page either way.** Every feature in the day-one list is
correct for both — a creator arriving from a DM is checking that the person who
messaged her is real, professional, and not going to embarrass her, and
disclosure is precisely what answers that; a creator arriving cold is evaluating
an unknown vendor, and disclosure is precisely what answers that too. That is
the useful property of building out of facts: it is register-neutral *and*
temperature-neutral.

**What differs is months four to six, and it differs in two ways.**

- **If warm** (he DMs the link — the only mechanism in evidence anywhere in
  Starvu's own material): the page's job is corroboration, so the second half of
  the six months goes *down* rather than *out*. Depth on the agreement, depth on
  the ninety days, depth on the post-booking experience, and the creator
  photographs sit mid-page as reinforcement. **No acquisition surface is built at
  all**, and the honest conclusion may be that the page was never the
  load-bearing instrument — which is itself worth knowing and is a falsifier, not
  a failure.
- **If cold**: the creator photographs and Starvu's own numbers move *above the
  fold* and become load-bearing, because a stranger needs a reason to stay before
  she will read anything. And months four to six buy exactly one acquisition
  instrument — **a referral mechanism with real attribution**, AT's, the cheapest
  in the set and the only one that becomes honest for Starvu specifically once
  signed creators exist to do the referring. Not a blog. ~115 posts is not a
  landing page's problem.

**The branch is decided by feature 10 — one field, on the booking.** After
roughly twenty bookings, the answers to *how did you hear about Starvu?* settle
it. Which means T does not have to choose now, and should not; he has to install
the field, which is already in the v1 design prompt.

## What I deliberately left to The Chancellor

Three live decisions on the record. I have taken none of them, and I designed so
that none of them blocks a build.

**Decision 1 (which feeling leads) and decision 3 (inherit the mark or set a new
register).** Every feature above is a *content* strategy, and content strategy is
orthogonal to visual register: an agreement viewer, a ninety-day timeline and a
split table all work in black-and-gold star-burst and in Foxy's navy-and-italic-
Playfair. But the Horizon does differ in one respect and I will name it rather
than pretend it doesn't:

- **If the flyer's register wins** (black, gold, condensed italic,
  sports-broadcast), the page runs *louder* and the flyer's `$15K–$30K` band
  probably rides on the hero — which means the earnings disclaimer becomes
  mandatory rather than optional, and the disclosure furniture reads as
  confidence-with-receipts. The mark has a future.
- **If the brief's register wins** (white, restraint, serif), the page needs
  **more** text rather than less — the Advocate demolished the idea that Starvu
  can inherit Foxy's silence, and a quiet page with little on it reads as absence
  rather than as expense. The disclosure instruments carry the entire load, and
  the "~80% of the effect is configuration" finding becomes applicable, because
  it is a description of Foxy's system.

Either way, "expensive" is bought here the same way luxury retail buys it:
**a threshold, a document, and a small number of appointments.** Not gloss.

**Decision 2 (gate the calendar or leave it bare).** I have designed the object
so it can go either way and so the no-show hazard is reduced under both: real
availability rather than fictional slots, few of them, a named-agenda
confirmation, a one-tap cancel, and a WhatsApp thread that makes changing plans
cheaper than disappearing. What I will offer The Chancellor as input, not as a
ruling: **feature 4, the eligibility check, is a gate that does not feel like
one** — it runs on Starvu's published admission rules rather than on an
interrogation, so it qualifies without the page having to demand seven fields
before it will speak. If the decision goes to *gated*, that is the gate. If it
goes to *bare*, the same component sits after the booking instead of before it
and loses nothing.

## What would make this wrong

I inherit the falsifier set from `0009` §4 and extend it. Every item here is an
observation someone could actually make, and most of them cost a text message or
a habit of writing things down.

**Inherited, unchanged:**

1. **A named traffic source per link.** One query parameter per channel.
2. **"How did you hear about Starvu?" on the booking.** The decisive one.
3. **A pre-registered number, written down before launch.** *Of the first twenty
   creators who reach this page from something that is not a personal
   introduction, if fewer than N book, the page is not doing the work.* T picks
   N. Any N. Writing one down converts the bet into a claim.
4. **The disconfirming observation:** if the friend closes creators over WhatsApp
   without them meaningfully reading the site, the page was never the
   load-bearing instrument and this Horizon's premise is wrong.

**Mine, and they aim at this Horizon specifically:**

5. **The disclosure bet — the sharpest one.** This entire page is built on the
   claim that *verifiability substitutes for a track record*. The test: at the
   end of each call the friend asks one question — *what made you book?* — and
   writes down the answer. **If twenty creators come off calls and not one of
   them mentions the agreement, the terms, the entity, the verification standard,
   or the ninety-day exit unprompted, the feature spine is wrong** and the page
   should be rebuilt around whatever they do say. This costs one sentence per
   call and it is the only way I can make my own design falsifiable.
6. **The friend-publishes bet.** If he will not put his name, the entity, and the
   agreement on the site — Eros chose exactly that and calls it discretion — then
   features 1 and 2 do not exist, and this Horizon loses more than it can absorb.
   Answerable today, by text, and it should be asked before anything is built.
7. **The photographs bet.** If permission has not landed by month two, the page
   at month six has no uncopyable asset and the whole second half of this drawing
   is materially weaker. Named as a dependency with a trigger rather than assumed
   away.
8. **The no-stub audit.** At month six, walk the page. **If any affordance has
   been quietly disabled, unlinked, or has drifted out of true** — a slot that
   isn't real, a service he stopped offering, a response-time number that is now
   last quarter's — the discipline failed and the page has become the thing it
   was designed against. This is checkable in ten minutes and should be a
   recurring habit, not a one-off.
9. **The calculator refusal is itself falsifiable.** If creators ask *what could
   I make?* on calls and the friend has to answer it by hand every single time,
   the refusal cost conversions. The correct revision is then a **published
   range with the flyer's disclaimer** — a stated fact — and never a generator
   that manufactures a personal number. I would rather be wrong in that direction
   and say so now than defend the refusal later.

Classification: **`horizon: falsifiable`.** Not because the drawing is
guaranteed, but because items 3, 5, 6, 7 and 9 each name an observation that
would show it wrong, and every one of them is affordable to a one-person
business.

## What this will not become

Named refusals, so the drawing has edges:

- **Not a site that claims performance it cannot substantiate**, in any register
  — no invented figures, no borrowed logos, no manufactured demand, no "live"
  anything.
- **Not a content operation.** ~115 blog posts is a different business with a
  different staff.
- **Not a product.** No portal, no login, no dashboard, no account. The page ends
  at a booked fifteen minutes and that is the honest limit of what it can do.
- **Not a substitute for the conversation.** `0008` had this right and it held
  under attack: *a better page makes failure faster, not smaller.* If the friend
  cannot convert a call, none of this matters, and no feature on this list
  pretends otherwise.

## Where this Horizon stops

I have not sequenced anything — `chart` and `phase` are The Surveyor's. I have
not taken any of the three live decisions. I have not resolved warm-versus-cold;
I have made the page indifferent to it for six months and named the one field
that settles it. I have not designed a surface, and I have deliberately said as
little as possible about colour and type, because two of the three open decisions
own that ground.

What I claim to have done: **the collision between "as many meaningful features
as possible" and "nothing may be a stub" is resolved rather than compromised** —
by sorting features into assertion machines, which need a business behind them
and which Starvu must refuse, and disclosure instruments, which need only a fact
in front of them and of which Starvu has more than any site in its reference set.
**Twelve of those are buildable this week.** Six more have a named trigger apiece.
Seven are named as forbidden, with the earnings calculator refused outright and a
better instrument put in its place. And the drawing carries five falsifiers of
its own, one of which — *what made you book?* — costs one sentence at the end of
a phone call.
