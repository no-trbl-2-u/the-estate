# The route from here to the Starvu page at month six

I am The Surveyor. The Architect drew the destination and the Forager found the
supply line to it. My job is the ground between: what order, waiting on what, and
where the route would have to change.

I take one thing as already moving. **The v1 design prompt has gone to T and is
in flight.** The page's visual first version is not step one waiting to begin; it
is work in progress, and the route starts around it rather than in front of it.

---

## The shape of the route, before the detail

It is not a page being built forward. It is this:

```
  NOW ─── an errand ─── launch ─── a long quiet middle ─── harvest ─── harvest
          (minutes)     (weeks)    (a month, four fuses    (page      (page
                                    burning, page still     grows      grows
                                    unchanged)              once)      again)
```

The counterintuitive part, and it is the whole reason a route was worth drawing:
**there is a month in the middle where the correct amount of visible progress on
the page is zero.** Four of the six "real soon" features are waiting on facts
that do not exist yet and cannot be hurried — they need a tool built, and then
they need *time passing with the tool running*. A roadmap that fills that month
with page work is a roadmap that will deliver those features a month late,
because the fuses were never lit.

### The sequencing principle

The Forager ranked the tools by **facts produced per hour of T's time**. That is
the right ranking for *value*. It is not the right ranking for *order*, and the
difference is a month.

Every supply-line tool has two costs, not one:

- **Build cost** — an afternoon, a weekend. T's time.
- **Fuse length** — how long the tool must sit running before it has anything
  to say. Nobody's time. Just elapsed calendar.

Fuse length dominates. An afternoon with a one-month fuse must be lit before a
weekend with no fuse, because the weekend can be bought back and the month
cannot.

| Tool | Build | Fuse | What the fuse burns down to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claims ledger | afternoon | **none** | works the day it ships |
| Notes capture | afternoon | **~10 calls → ~20 calls** | the real FAQ, then the disclosure bet |
| Response-time meter | afternoon | **one month** | the median-response number |
| Takedown assistant | weekend | **one month** | notices filed / removed / median days |
| Onboarding + verification tracker | weekend | **one onboarding** | "creators managed", portal items 1–3 |
| Weekly numbers pack | weekend | **one week** | the portal's recurring payload |
| Clause finder | afternoon | none | gated on a *permission*, not a fuse |

Read that column and the order argues itself. **Light the long fuses first, even
the cheap ones.** That single reordering moves one item materially, and I say so
plainly below.

## The route

### Leg 0 — The errand. Minutes. Monday.

Nothing here is a build. Everything here gates something months long, and every
item is answerable by one person in one sitting.

| Item | Whose | Costs | Gates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Will he publish the Talent Management Agreement?** | the friend | one text | page features 1–2, the clause finder, portal item 1 — *the Horizon's entire spine* |
| **Will he put his name and face on the page?** | the friend | same text | the identity block; the verifiability ground itself |
| **What is the split?** | the friend | a business decision | the split table, which replaced the refused calculator |
| **Is content protection a service he actually performs today?** | the friend | same text | the takedown assistant, and `0009`'s live doubt on assumption 3 |
| **Choose the verification vendor, and stop IDs arriving over WhatsApp** | T + friend | hours | *nothing downstream — and that is the point* |
| **Write down N** | T | one minute | falsifier 3, which is unavailable retroactively |

Two things about this leg.

**The verification vendor is the only item on the whole route where delay
compounds.** It gates nothing. It is not on the critical path of any feature. It
belongs at the front anyway, because every week it waits is another week of
photographs of driver's licences accumulating in a phone's cloud backup, outside
anything T builds and past any retention policy he could later write. Everything
else on this route gets cheaper or stays flat if deferred. This one gets more
expensive, silently, and the cost is somebody else's safety.

**The fourth question is mine and I am marking it as an addition.** T's opening
named two questions for the friend. The Forager and the Advocate both, from
different directions, arrived at a third live doubt — whether the named services
are actually delivered — and content protection is the sharpest case, because it
is the one service the entire reference set leaves unclaimed and therefore the
largest disclosure upside on the record. It costs one more sentence in a message
that is being sent anyway. **N** is likewise cheap and expires: a number
pre-registered after launch is not a pre-registration.

**What re-routes here.** If the agreement is not published, features 1 and 2 do
not ship, the clause finder leaves the route entirely, portal item 1 empties out,
and the Horizon loses its spine — that is falsifier 6 firing on day one, and it
fires *before* anything has been built, which is exactly where you want it. The
correct response is not to build the page anyway; it is to go back to `envision`
with a much harder problem, because the verifiability ground was funded by
publication.

### Leg 1 — Ship v1. Weeks. Runs on the design already in flight.

The twelve day-one features, minus whatever Leg 0 struck out. This is the leg T
is already standing in.

**Gated by:** Leg 0's two publication answers (features 1, 2). Nothing else.

**One item on this leg is fifteen minutes and is the most droppable thing on the
route, and dropping it costs six months.** Per-channel link tags and the *"How
did you hear about Starvu?"* field are the instrumentation. They are not
features; they are the denominator. Without them the ~20-booking checkpoint at
Leg 5 has nothing to read, the warm-versus-cold branch never settles, and the
record carries an unsettled fork into months four to six — which is precisely
the failure `0009` §4 diagnosed as *unfalsifiable by construction*. Under launch
pressure this is the first thing that gets cut, because it is invisible.

**Do not ship the page without it.** That is the strongest single instruction on
this route.

**Where the standing decisions bite here.** Decisions 1 and 3 — which feeling
leads, and inherit-the-mark-or-new-register — land on this leg and on no other.
The Architect made the content strategy indifferent to them and that holds.
**But there is one downstream consequence the record has not named:** if the
flyer's register wins, the `$15K–$30K` band probably rides the hero, which makes
the earnings-not-typical disclaimer mandatory rather than optional — and that
puts a *permanent row in the claims ledger* (Leg 2) that has to be carried and
re-verified for the whole six months. A visual decision acquires a maintenance
obligation. Small, but real, and it is the sort of thing that gets discovered at
month six by the no-stub audit rather than at month zero by choosing.

### Leg 2 — Light the fuses. An afternoon each, the same week as launch or the week after.

Three tools, and the ordering inside this leg is by fuse, not by value.

1. **The notes capture.** Longest fuse on the route: ten calls before the FAQ is
   real, twenty before the disclosure bet can be read. It also has the only
   *human* dependency on the route, which is why it carries its own checkpoint
   two weeks later.
2. **The response-time meter.** *This is the one item I am moving.* The Forager
   ranked it sixth — cheap, but "only worth the number once there is a month of
   it." That sentence is the argument for building it **first**, not sixth. An
   afternoon at position 6 delivers its number a month after an afternoon at
   position 2, for identical effort, and that number is one of the six things the
   Architect put on the page and had no mechanism for. It is also the only honest
   form of the flyer's most exposed line.
3. **The claims ledger.** No fuse, so by my own principle it should go last of
   the three — but it goes here anyway for a different reason: it is cheapest to
   install when there is almost nothing on the page to enumerate, and it is the
   thing that makes the page's growth auditable *as it grows* rather than
   reconstructable at month six.

**Gated by:** the meter needs support to run somewhere exportable. If the
friend's support currently lives across three apps and his head, the meter's
precondition is a channel decision, not a build — and that belongs in Leg 0's
message. I have not put it there because it is his operational call and it is not
free; I am naming it as the meter's gate.

### CHECKPOINT A — week two of the notes capture. The tooling bet.

*Hand him the notes capture and nothing else. If it is empty at week two, the
operation runs in his head and tooling is the wrong intervention.*

This is the sharpest and cheapest checkpoint on the route, and it is correctly
placed two weeks after Leg 2 rather than at the end of anything.

**What it re-routes — and I am refining the Forager's own falsifier here, in two
places, because as written it does more damage than it means to.**

*First:* "build nothing else internal" over-reaches. Half the supply line
**requires no habit at all.** The claims ledger runs at build time and touches T
only. The response-time meter parses an export and touches nobody. The takedown
assistant is used when a leak is found, which is event-driven rather than
habitual. What the checkpoint actually falsifies is **the class of tools that
requires the friend to change his Tuesday** — the notes capture, the verification
tracker, the numbers pack. Those are the ones that die at week two. The passive
observers survive, and the honest re-route is *build only tools that watch, never
tools that ask.*

*Second, and this is the more serious one:* **falsifier 10 firing disarms
falsifier 5.** The notes capture is the only instrument on the record that runs
the disclosure bet — *what made you book?*, twenty calls, the test of the entire
feature spine. If the capture is empty, the Architect's sharpest falsifier
becomes unrunnable, and the record loses its ability to check its own central
claim. That is a much larger loss than losing a FAQ.

So the re-route has a second half, and it should be pre-committed now rather than
improvised then: **move the question off his Tuesday and onto the machine.**
*What made you book?* goes into the booking confirmation or a one-question
follow-up after the call — the page already sends a named-agenda confirmation and
already opens a WhatsApp thread, so the carrier exists. The answers will be worse
than his notes would have been. Worse answers to a live falsifier beat perfect
answers to a dead one.

### Leg 3 — The quiet month. One weekend of build; otherwise, operating.

The page does not change on this leg. That is not stagnation, it is fuse burn.

- **Takedown assistant, manual-assist** (weekend) — gated on Leg 0's fourth
  answer. One-month fuse.
- **Onboarding + verification tracker** (weekend) — build the tracker, never the
  vault; it needs the vendor chosen at Leg 0. Its fuse is one onboarding, and it
  is a precondition for both "creators managed" on the page and items 1–3 behind
  the portal door.
- Everything else: calls happen, notes accumulate, the meter counts, the ledger
  holds.

**Gated by:** Checkpoint A for the tracker (it asks him for records); Leg 0's
service answer for the takedown assistant.

### CHECKPOINT B — one month of takedown work. The takedown bet.

*Zero true positives in month one means either the tool is wrong or the service
is not performed.*

**Re-routes:** the second answer removes content protection from the services
block on the page — which is the day-one feature governed by *every service the
site names is real*, and it is `0009`'s assumption 3 landing on the record's
largest unclaimed opportunity. It also removes the takedown log from the portal
and from the harvest at Leg 4. This checkpoint can shrink the page, and a
checkpoint that can only add is not a checkpoint.

### Leg 4 — First harvest. The page grows, in bursts, as fuses burn down.

Each row lands the week its fact arrives. They do not arrive together and the
route should not pretend they will.

| What lands | When its fuse burns down | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Photographs of real creators** | permission — **no fuse, no tool, any time** | the friend |
| Starvu's own numbers | one month of operating | tracker + meter |
| Median response time | one month | the meter |
| The real FAQ | ~10 calls | the notes capture |
| The split, published | the friend sets it | Leg 0 |
| One creator's sentence, no figure | one creator agreeing | the friend |
| A services page as a route | one service delivered end to end | operating |
| The clause finder | the agreement published | Leg 0 |
| Content-protection log with real numbers | one month of §1.3 running | Leg 3 |

**The photographs are the odd one and the route must be built to absorb them.**
They are the highest-value single unlock on the record, they depend on no tool of
T's, and they could land in week one or in month five. Nothing should be
sequenced behind them, and nothing should wait for them.

**One hard precondition, and I am promoting it from a note to a gate.** The
Forager observed that publishing a real face is irreversible and that permission
granted in month two may be regretted in month twelve. **The one-hour unpublish
path must exist before the first photograph ships**, not after. It is trivial if
decided at the start and it is the sort of thing that is never retrofitted under
calm conditions — by definition, you need it on the day everything is not calm.

### Leg 5 — The portal, on a spur.

**Trigger:** the second signed creator *plus* one repeating deliverable. Not a
date, and plausibly month two or three.

**Preconditions, in order:** the verification tracker (Leg 3) and the weekly
numbers pack must exist to give the door something to hold; the breach-minimising
design rules — no legal names, no documents, no warehoused earnings, magic-link
auth, expiring URLs — must be decided *before the first row is written*, because
the constraint is the design and it does not retrofit.

**This leg is a spur, not trunk.** If it does not happen, nothing else on the
route changes: the weekly numbers pack stays a WhatsApp attachment, the takedown
log stays a message, and the page is unaffected because the page never linked to
it in any branch.

**Where the standing decision bites.** The `0010`-versus-`0011` tension — *"no
portal, no login"* against *"the **page** is not a product"* — lands here and
only here. If The Chancellor upholds the refusal whole, Leg 5 is deleted and the
route is otherwise untouched. If she amends it to the page-only form, Leg 5 runs
under the resolution both artifacts can live with: **the portal is real and the
public site never links to it.** T does not need this decided before Leg 4, and
should not force it early — but he must have it before the weekend of Leg 5 is
spent, because the design rules are not additions to a built portal.

**Checkpoint on the spur:** if signed creators log in once and never return
within thirty days, the portal is a worse WhatsApp. The re-route is not a better
portal — it is delivery over WhatsApp with the portal demoted to an archive
nobody has to visit.

### CHECKPOINT C — roughly twenty bookings. The traffic read.

*The "How did you hear about Starvu?" answers settle warm versus cold.*

This is the checkpoint that decides Leg 6, and it is why Leg 1's fifteen minutes
of instrumentation is non-negotiable. Note the ordering consequence: this
checkpoint fires on *booking volume*, not on calendar, so it may fire before or
after Leg 4 depending entirely on how busy he is. The route must tolerate that.

**Where the standing decision bites, and this has not been weighed.** Decision 2
— gate the calendar or leave it bare — has a cost nobody has priced against it:
**a gate lengthens the fuse on this checkpoint.** Gating means fewer bookings per
visitor, so twenty bookings take longer to accumulate, so the warm/cold answer
arrives later; and the answers themselves are biased toward people willing to
clear a gate, which is a different population from the one the branch is about.
That is a real argument for *bare*, made on route grounds rather than on
conversion grounds, and I offer it to The Chancellor as input rather than taking
it.

### CHECKPOINT D — twenty calls. The disclosure bet.

*If twenty creators come off calls and not one mentions the agreement, the terms,
the entity, the verification standard or the 90-day exit unprompted, the feature
spine is wrong.*

**This is the only checkpoint on the route that can change the destination rather
than the path**, and it deserves to be seen as such. Everything upstream of it is
built on the claim that verifiability substitutes for track record. If it fires,
the correct response is not to adjust Leg 6 — it is to return to `envision` with
whatever the twenty creators *did* say, and the supply line survives intact
because the tools that produced the finding are the same tools the next Horizon
would need.

It depends entirely on Checkpoint A having passed, or on Checkpoint A's fallback
having been installed. Those two are wired together and the record did not know
it.

### Leg 6 — Months four to six. Down, or out.

**Warm** (he DMs the link — the only mechanism in evidence anywhere in Starvu's
own material): the page goes *down* rather than *out*. Depth on the agreement,
depth on the ninety days, depth on the post-booking experience, photographs
mid-page as reinforcement. **No acquisition surface at all.** And the honest
possibility that the page was never the load-bearing instrument, which is a
finding rather than a failure.

**Cold**: photographs and Starvu's own numbers move above the fold and become
load-bearing, and the six months buy exactly one acquisition instrument — a
referral mechanism with real attribution, which becomes honest for Starvu
specifically once there are signed creators to do the referring. Not a blog.

I am carrying the Architect's ruling without touching it: **the page is the same
page either way, and the branch lives only here.** Nothing before Leg 6 needs the
answer, which is the whole reason it is safe to defer.

### CHECKPOINT E — month six. The no-stub audit.

*Walk the page. If any affordance has been quietly disabled, unlinked, or drifted
out of true, the discipline failed.*

If the claims ledger shipped at Leg 2, this is not a walk — it is a build step
that has been failing loudly since the day it would have started drifting. That
is the conversion the Forager was after: **a habit becomes a mechanism.** If the
ledger did not ship, this is ten minutes of walking and it will be skipped,
because month-six habits set in month zero are the ones that do not survive.

## What runs beside the route, on no leg

- **`research` on platform and model-provider terms.** The only genuinely
  checkable unknown the Forager opened. It is not on the critical path of
  anything on this route, because everything on this route is business-facing
  text — call notes, FAQ clustering, DMCA drafting, meeting narration, retrieval
  over published text. It is on the critical path of the **chat drafting
  assistant**, which is off the route entirely and stays off it until that
  research returns. It can run at any time and should run before a line of that
  code is written.
- **The three live decisions.** They bite at Leg 1 (register), Checkpoint C
  (gating), and Leg 5 (the portal tension). None of them blocks the start.

## The first actionable step

Monday morning, before anything is built and while the design is still in flight,
one message to the friend and one decision by T:

> **Send the friend four questions in a single text:** will you publish the
> Talent Management Agreement? Will your name and face go on the page? What is
> the split? Do you actually perform content protection today — and if so, how
> does a creator's leak reach you right now?
>
> **And separately, today: choose the verification vendor, and stop identity
> documents arriving over WhatsApp.** If the current process is "text me a photo
> of your ID," changing that this week is worth more than every tool on this
> route.
>
> **And write down N** — the number of bookings out of the first twenty
> non-referral visitors below which the page did not do the work. Any N. Written
> before launch or not at all.

That is the whole of Leg 0. It costs an afternoon at the outside, most of which
is waiting for a reply, and it gates the Horizon's entire feature spine, the
clause finder, the takedown assistant, the split table, the portal's first three
rows, and the only falsifier on the record that expires.

Classification: **`trajectory: actionable`.** The route terminates in a message
that can be sent from a phone before coffee, and every leg after it names what it
waits on.

## What this route will not become

- **Not a schedule.** Every gate on it is an event — a permission, a reply, a
  signature, a count of calls, a count of bookings. Where I used time at all I
  used the record's own granularity, an afternoon and a weekend and a month, and
  `appetite: 1` remains a placeholder that nobody should read as a calendar.
- **Not a decomposition.** These are legs, gates and checkpoints. What "done"
  looks like inside each one is `phase`, and it runs after this.
- **Not a resolution of anything standing.** I have taken none of the three live
  decisions and I have not resolved the portal tension or warm-versus-cold. Where
  the route differs by how one lands, I said so at the point where it bites and
  kept going.
- **Not a route that survives its own checkpoints unchanged.** Checkpoint A
  deletes half the supply line. Checkpoint B can shrink the page. Checkpoint D
  can send the record back to `envision`. If none of them can move this drawing,
  it was a schedule wearing a route's clothes.

## Where this survey stops

Three things I found that the Horizon did not know about itself, stated plainly
so they can be argued with:

1. **Falsifier 10 firing disarms falsifier 5.** The notes capture is the sole
   instrument for the disclosure bet, so the cheap two-week test, if it fails,
   silently removes the record's ability to check its own central claim. The
   fallback — move *what made you book?* onto the booking flow — should be
   pre-committed now, not improvised at week two.
2. **Falsifier 10's re-route is too broad as written.** "Build nothing else
   internal" kills tools that ask *and* tools that watch, and only the first
   class is what the test falsifies. The passive observers — claims ledger,
   response-time meter, takedown assist — survive an empty notebook.
3. **Gating the calendar lengthens the fuse on the traffic checkpoint**, and
   biases its answers toward people who clear gates. That is a cost of decision 2
   that has not been weighed against it, and it is the kind that only shows up
   when you draw the route rather than the object.

And one reordering: **the response-time meter moves from sixth to second.** Same
afternoon, one month earlier, because it was ranked by what it costs rather than
by how long it has to sit still afterwards.

What I claim to have done: **turned the Forager's supply-line finding into an
ordering rule** — fuse length governs, so light the long fuses first even when
they are cheap — **placed the record's own falsifiers on the ground where they
fall**, each with the re-route it triggers, and put the entire load-bearing part
of the route into a single text message that costs an afternoon of waiting and
gates six months.
