# Phase 5 — First harvest

The page grows here, and it grows in bursts rather than in a release.

Each row lands the week its fact arrives. They do not arrive together and this
plan should not pretend they will. **Nothing is sequenced behind anything else in
this Phase**, which is unusual and deliberate: the only ordering is the order the
facts turn up in.

### The photographs are the odd one, and the plan must absorb them

They are the highest-value single unlock on the record — the one asset that is
expensive to fake and cheap for Starvu to have honestly, against Foxy's
`freepik__` stock and AT's `imageye___`-prefixed scraped logos. They depend on no
tool of T's. They could land in week one or in month five.

**Nothing is sequenced behind them and nothing waits for them.** If they land
during Phase 2, they ship during Phase 2. This Phase is where they are *expected*,
not where they are *permitted*.

### The gate on them, promoted from a note

> **The one-hour unpublish path must exist before the first creator photograph
> ships.**

Publishing a real face is irreversible, and permission granted in month two may
be regretted in month twelve. This is trivial if decided at the start and it is
never retrofitted under calm conditions — **by definition you need it on the day
everything is not calm.** The permission itself should be specific and revocable
in writing, and that is the friend's paperwork rather than T's build.

Phase 4 named building it as the honest use of a spare afternoon in the quiet
month. If that did not happen, it happens here, *before* the row ships and not
after.

### The clause finder, and why it is the only visitor-facing AI on the route

A visitor types *how do I get out?* and the page **scrolls to and highlights the
actual clause**, quoted verbatim, with its heading. It generates nothing. It
performs retrieval and stops.

The test that proves its species: **remove the AI and replace it with keyword
search over clause headings, and the feature still works, just worse.** Remove
the AI from a chat widget and you have Foxy's corpse. Its v1 has no model in it
at all.

Non-negotiable if it ships: never paraphrase; never answer what the document does
not answer (*the agreement does not address that; ask him on the call* is both
true and a better conversion than an invented answer); never touch money,
earnings or eligibility outside the published rules.

**Its most valuable output is its failures.** Log the queries it could not
resolve. That log is a list of things creators want to know that the agreement
does not say, and it is worth more to the friend's lawyer than the feature is
worth to the page.

### The careers row — arriving from T, and conditional

**Arriving from T, 2026-08-27, not through `capture`.** The flyer promises *"a
team that answers to you."* A careers surface is what a business that is building
a team actually looks like, and it is the first evidence on the record for a
claim the flyer already makes.

**It becomes evidence the day a role is actually filled, and not one day
earlier.** Until then it is a claim like any other and it lives in the claims
ledger as one. This is the same discipline the whole page runs on, pointed at the
newest surface: *a form with a human reading the inbox is real; a form nobody
reads is an assertion machine* of exactly the kind `0010` and `0011` refuse.

**And one hard ordering constraint, which is the sharpest thing the careers tab
brought to this route.** If the role filled is a **chatter**, the disclosure
clause must already exist. `0011` §4.5 calls it the strongest unclaimed position
on the record: *"our agreement names exactly who can speak as you, and what they
cannot say"* — a sentence no competitor can say, about a practice all of them
perform. A person hired to speak as a creator is precisely what that clause is
about.

> **The clause precedes the hire.** Hire first and the first creator's agreement
> is silent about the person now typing in her DMs, and the best unclaimed
> position on the record is spent before it was taken.

That clause is Phase 1 question 8 and it is the friend's and his lawyer's, not
T's. What this Phase does is name the deadline it acquired.

**A conditional that opens here if applications arrived:** the chatter assessment
(`0011` §1.11) becomes buildable, a weekend, once there are applicants to assess.
It is the category's own pattern — AT runs a `/chatting-test` route with a
lazy-loaded `ChattingPage` chunk, disallowed in `robots.txt`; Foxy recruits
German-speaking chatters through a role-prefilled Calendly (`0006`). **Chatting
is an operated function with recruiting behind it at both working businesses in
the set.** Starvu now has the surface earlier than either. This is a genuine fork
in the road and it is not on this route: it is where the business grows a second
employee, and it should be decided as a business question rather than absorbed as
a build.

## What starts it

**No single trigger.** Whichever fuse burns down first. Realistically that is the
photographs, because they have no fuse.

The Phase is *open* from the day Phase 4's month completes and *closes* when the
last row has either landed or been struck out by a checkpoint.

## What it is waiting on

Per row, per the table. Nothing waits on anything else in this Phase. That is the
property to protect: the temptation is to hold rows back so the page "launches"
the growth all at once, and holding a landed fact back is the only way to make
this Phase late.

## Cost

**An afternoon per row**, spread across weeks. No weekend on the whole Phase.

## Done looks like

- Every row above has either landed or has a written reason it did not.
- The claims ledger has a row per new claim, with a verification date, and the
  build still passes.
- **The unpublish path was exercised once**, on purpose, before a real face went
  up.
- Nothing shipped in this Phase that the tools of Phases 3 and 4 did not produce.
  That is the test of whether the quiet month was actually spent on fuse burn.

## CHECKPOINT D — twenty calls. The disclosure bet.

*If twenty creators come off calls and not one mentions the agreement, the terms,
the entity, the verification standard or the 90-day exit unprompted, the feature
spine is wrong.*

**This is the only checkpoint on the route that can change the destination rather
than the path**, and it should be read as such. Everything upstream of it is
built on the claim that verifiability substitutes for track record.

**The re-route.** Not an adjustment to Phase 7. **Back to `envision`**, with
whatever the twenty creators *did* say in hand. And the supply line survives
intact — the tools that produced the finding are the same tools the next Horizon
would need, so a firing here costs the drawing and not the machinery.

**It depends entirely on Checkpoint A having passed, or on Checkpoint A's
fallback having been installed.** Those two are wired together and the record did
not know it before the route was drawn. If neither happened, this checkpoint
cannot run, and the honest thing is to say the central claim is unchecked rather
than to read a checkpoint that has no instrument behind it.

## Where a standing decision changes this Phase

**Decision 1's consequence is maintained here rather than taken here.** If the
flyer's register won and the `$15K–$30K` band rides the hero, the disclaimer row
in the ledger is carried and re-verified as the page grows around it.

**Decision 2 is invisible here** and decides nothing about which rows land.

**The portal tension does not bite here** — Phase 6 is a spur and this Phase does
not link to it in any branch.

**Checkpoint C may fire during this Phase**, because it fires on booking volume
rather than on calendar. It belongs to Phase 7 and is documented there. The only
thing this Phase owes it is tolerance: **the two are not ordered relative to each
other and neither waits for the other.**

## What this Phase is not

- **Not a redesign.** The page at month six is the same page with more true things
  on it. `0010`'s branch on warm-versus-cold lives in Phase 7 and nowhere else.
- **Not testimonials with figures.** One creator's sentence, her name, her face,
  **no number**. One that is true beats three that are constants in a JavaScript
  bundle.
- **Not the calculator.** Refused at v1 and at month six in the generator form the
  category uses. The refusal is itself falsifiable — if creators ask *what could I
  make?* on every call and he answers by hand every time, the correct revision is
  a **published range with the disclaimer**, a stated fact, and never a machine
  that manufactures a personal number.
- **Not a careers site.** One row, conditional on a role being filled.
