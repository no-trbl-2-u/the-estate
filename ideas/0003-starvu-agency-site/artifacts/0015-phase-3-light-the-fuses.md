# Phase 3 — Light the fuses

Three afternoons. No visitor sees any of it. It exists so that Phase 5 has
something to publish.

The ordering *inside* this Phase is by fuse, not by value. That is the whole
Trajectory's rule applied at small scale, and it survives decomposition because
the alternative — building the useful one first — costs a month for nothing.

| Tool | Build | Fuse | Burns down to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Call-notes capture** | afternoon | ~10 calls → ~20 calls | the real FAQ, then the disclosure bet |
| **2. Response-time meter** | afternoon | one month | the median-response number |
| **3. Claims ledger** | afternoon | none | a build that fails when the page drifts |

---

## The reordering this decomposition found

The Trajectory placed this leg "the same week as launch or the week after." That
was wrong about item 1, and it was wrong by the Trajectory's own rule.

**The notes capture's fuse is measured in calls, not in weeks, and the friend is
already taking calls.** He is bundling existing freelance creators into the
company; he is talking to creators on Instagram; the consultation call is not an
artifact of the page, it is a thing he does. Every call taken before the notebook
exists is a call that produced no fact, and the fuse is not buyable back.

**So item 1 does not wait for Phase 2.** It is handed over the moment it is
built, in parallel with the page, and the two-week clock on Checkpoint A starts
then. Items 2 and 3 do belong after launch — the meter measures support that is
already running but reports a number the page will carry, and the ledger is
cheapest to install against a page that exists.

This is the one place where the Phase sequence is not a strict order. **Phase 3
item 1 overlaps Phase 2.** I am naming the overlap rather than pretending the
numbering is a queue.

---

## What each item delivers

### 1. The call-notes capture

Ninety seconds after each call, into one place: what she asked, in her words;
what he answered; and her answer to **what made you book?** The AI part is
trivial and real — cluster twenty raw notes into the eight recurring questions
and draft the FAQ in his phrasing rather than marketing's.

Most of the afternoon goes on **activation energy**, not on the model. A phone
shortcut, a message to himself, a bot — anything cheaper than opening a laptop.
That is the build.

**Hazard, carried from `0011` §1.1 unchanged:** notes *after* a call, never
recording *during* one. Pennsylvania is all-party consent for oral
communications. The safe build is also the cheap one.

**This is the highest-leverage object on the record** because `0010` depends on
it twice and had no mechanism for it. It funds the real FAQ and it is the only
instrument that runs the disclosure bet.

### 2. The response-time meter

Parses the support channel's export, reports the median. **Gated on Phase 1
question 5** — if support lives across three apps and his head, this cannot be
built and the gate is an operational decision, not a build.

**This is the item I moved, and I am holding it there.** The Forager ranked it
sixth: cheap, but "only worth the number once there is a month of it." That
sentence is the argument for building it *second*, not sixth. Same afternoon,
one month earlier, for one of the six numbers the Architect put on the page and
had no mechanism for. It is also the only honest form of the flyer's most exposed
line — *24/7 support*. Every agency claims it. One agency can publish what it
actually was.

### 3. The claims ledger

Every factual claim on the page as a row: the claim, its source, who can verify
it, when it was last verified. The build reads the file. A stale claim fails the
build or renders with a visible *last verified* date, or both.

No fuse, so by my own rule it should be last of the three — and it is. It is here
rather than later for a different reason: **it is cheapest to install when there
is almost nothing to enumerate**, and if Phase 2 kept the plain list while
writing the page, this afternoon is mostly wiring.

It converts Checkpoint E from a habit into a mechanism. That conversion is the
single reason Checkpoint E will happen at all.

**Two rows it must carry from day one, both of which the decomposition added:**

- The **earnings disclaimer** row, if decision 1 put a figure on the hero. That
  is the maintenance obligation a visual decision acquired.
- The **careers** row — *we are hiring for ⟨roles⟩* — verified by *the roles are
  open and the inbox is read*, and stale the day the last role is filled. A
  careers tab advertising a role that closed in month two is exactly the drift
  Checkpoint E is looking for, and it is the kind nobody notices because nobody
  on the creator funnel ever goes there.

## What starts it

**Item 1: the friend's next call.** Which is to say, now.
**Items 2 and 3: the page being live** (Phase 2), and for item 2, Phase 1's
answer about the support channel.

## What it is waiting on

- Item 1: nothing. Deliberately nothing.
- Item 2: an exportable support channel. **His operational call.** If that answer
  has not come back, item 2 slips and its one-month fuse slips with it — which is
  the cost of the delay, stated so it can be seen.
- Item 3: a page with claims on it.

## Cost

**Three afternoons**, spread across roughly two weeks. Plus two weeks of elapsed
time in which nothing is built and Checkpoint A ripens.

## Done looks like

- Three tools exist and are *running*, not merely written.
- The friend has had the notes capture in his hands for **fourteen days**.
- The meter has begun accumulating; it says nothing yet and is not expected to.
- The build fails, deliberately, on a claim with no verification date. Prove it
  fails once.

## CHECKPOINT A — two weeks after the capture is handed over. The tooling bet.

*Hand him the notes capture and nothing else. If it is empty at week two, the
operation runs in his head and tooling is the wrong intervention.*

The cheapest and sharpest checkpoint on the route. It sits at the end of this
Phase and it governs Phase 4.

### The re-route, in two halves, both pre-committed here

**First half — the narrowed scope.** `0011`'s falsifier 10 says *build nothing
else internal*. That over-reaches and I narrowed it in the Trajectory; the
narrowing survives decomposition unchanged and I restate it as a rule rather than
as a remark:

> **Build only tools that watch. Never tools that ask.**

What Checkpoint A falsifies is the class of tools requiring the friend to change
his Tuesday: the notes capture, the verification tracker, the weekly numbers
pack. Those die at week two. The passive observers survive an empty notebook
entirely — the claims ledger runs at build time and touches T only; the
response-time meter parses an export and touches nobody; the takedown assistant
is used when a leak is found, which is event-driven rather than habitual.

**And one arriving from T that lands on the survivor side:** *reading the careers
inbox* is a watch, not an ask. It requires no new habit, only that somebody
opens a mailbox that already receives. If Checkpoint A fires, the careers tab's
obligations do not die with the notebook — which is convenient, and also means
the tab cannot hide behind a failed checkpoint. See Checkpoint F, Phase 4.

**Second half — the fallback, and this is the serious one.** **Falsifier 10
firing disarms falsifier 5.** The notes capture is the only instrument on the
record that runs the disclosure bet — *what made you book?*, twenty calls, the
test of the entire feature spine. If the capture is empty, the Architect's
sharpest falsifier becomes unrunnable and the record loses the ability to check
its own central claim. That is a far larger loss than losing an FAQ.

So the re-route has a second half and it is **pre-committed now, not improvised
then**:

> **Move the question off his Tuesday and onto the machine.** *What made you
> book?* goes into the booking confirmation, or into a one-question follow-up
> after the call. The page already sends a named-agenda confirmation and already
> opens a WhatsApp thread — the carrier exists and costs an afternoon to point at
> a new question.

The answers will be worse than his notes would have been. **Worse answers to a
live falsifier beat perfect answers to a dead one.**

Checkpoint D in Phase 5 depends entirely on this checkpoint having passed, or on
this fallback having been installed. The two are wired together and the record
did not know it until the route was drawn.

## Where a standing decision changes this Phase

**None bite directly.** One arrives indirectly: decision 1's outcome determines
whether the disclaimer row exists in the ledger, which is a row rather than a
design. Nothing in this Phase waits on The Chancellor.

## What this Phase is not

- **Not the portal, and not the numbers pack.** Both are weekends, both ask the
  friend for something, and both sit downstream of Checkpoint A on purpose.
- **Not page work.** Nothing shipped in this Phase is visible to a visitor except
  a *last verified* date, and that is the ledger disclosing rather than the page
  growing.
- **Not the chat-drafting assistant.** Off the route until `research` returns
  (`0011` §4.4). A hired chatter does not change that; it is the tool that is
  gated, not the person.
