# Framing, challenged: Martial Havoc, mobile edition

The Advocate, dispatched fresh-eyes with the packet, the Framing, the
explored Spark, the Decision, the two Findings, state 0006 and the two text
extractions, and no memory of the session that argued the Framing into
being. The operator was live and answered three questions through the
Steward; their words are quoted verbatim where they land, and an attack
they answered is marked as pressed or conceded on what was actually heard.
Every other attack is filed unanswered, which is weaker evidence than a
defended one. Where the Framing held it says so and names the cost. Where
it bent, the revised text stands in the body and the attack that bent it
sits beside it. Nothing here is a gate.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The Framing's centre holds and the book
itself backs it: Appendix B opens by saying Solo RPG can be "a sandbox
experience in which the protagonist lives endless adventures, between
tables and dice rolls, until the demiurge decides to change the game", so
"the sandbox is the real game" is a reading the author offered, not one
the operator imposed. The Framing bends at four joints. The reading it
rejected, adventures as data with a persistent Master, is not a rival of
the reading it chose but its mechanism, and the operator's defence confirms
it: "player's choice at each scene", both save shapes, "a world you keep
returning to". Its falsifier, by the operator's own word, waits for the
sandbox, which leaves the first release with no test at all; the milestone
now has one. Its advisory posture on creation, which the Framing said
arrived "through a side door", is by the operator's word the front door:
"never corrected; rolled Masters get the same leeway", so the tension
between fidelity and legality is a decision waiting to be sealed, not a
tension. And its account of the fumble as filling a silence is wrong: the
parent rules automatic success at 12 on purpose, so the fumble repeals a
ruling, and it is also the only thing that keeps the Final Blow LUCK
exception from making Technique learning free. A dozen smaller assumptions
were pressed; the village is the one that turned into a proposal.

## The problem underneath

The operator's answer to "what is this really about", verbatim:

> A rules engine for Martial Havoc for a way to create multiple Wuxia
> style dnd gameplay.

Not "the book, playable on a phone" (the Gardener's recommendation, which
survives as the first release's shape), not the film, not a personal tool.
**The engine is the product.** Martial Havoc's rules, made to run, so that
many wuxia games can be played on them the way a table plays many
campaigns on one rulebook. Asked which reading of "multiple" the Framing
carries, the operator chose: **the sandbox is the real game; adventures
are scenes in it.** *Load-bearing.* The rulebook's dice-thrown regions,
monasteries, thirty-six hooks and encounter matrices are the engine's end
state; The 5 Treasures is the first authored island inside a world the
engine will one day generate.

Asked what "multiple Wuxia style dnd gameplay" assumes, the operator
answered, verbatim through the Steward: **"Player's choice at each
scene"** — continue with a living Master carrying XP, treasures, Dishonor
and passages, or start fresh; both save shapes supported. And "dnd" means
campaign continuity: **"a world you keep returning to"** — sessions
accumulate, the Master and the world change between them. So the reading
the Gardener recommended and the operator set aside, *adventures as data
with a persistent Master*, is not rejected; it is how the chosen reading
works. A scene in a sandbox is an adventure the engine can host, which
makes it data; a Master who walks from one scene into the next is a
persistent Master. The Framing had the two competing for one sentence when
one is the claim and the other is its machinery.

The question this record works on is therefore: **can Martial Havoc's
rules become an engine that plays a whole wuxia world, starting from one
cave, for a Master and a world that both outlive the cave?** Three things
shape that. The book delegates by design ("some terms or mechanics are
intentionally ambiguous", p. 3), and the Findings list 63 places where the
engine must decide (§6). The rulebook's region generation is a physical
scatter of dice on paper with "arbitrary" distance, so the sandbox the
operator names as the real game is the part the book describes least. And
the first release, by the operator's word, is a milestone that ships the
engine with one scene and no sandbox.

> **Attack: a plan for one thing and a claim about another.** The Framing
> claims the sandbox and plans the cave. The book's own sandbox is R52 and
> R53: "roll a handful of d6 on a sheet of paper. Mark where the dice
> land" and "decide on an arbitrary scale". The Findings say any digital
> version is an invention (A33). So the real game is the part that does
> not exist in the book as a procedure a machine can run, and the first
> release does not attempt it.
> **Outcome: held, with the cost named.** The claim is honest about what
> the record is *for*, and the book supports it: Appendix B's first
> sentence describes exactly this mode. The cost is that "the engine is
> the product" is unfalsifiable until a sandbox exists, and the operator
> agrees (see *The falsifier*). The parent's numeric distance rule
> (Findings 0004, `I-15`: sum of the two dice in miles, with a route band)
> is the one piece of the sandbox that is not an invention, and the
> Framing should say so rather than "arbitrary" alone.

> **Attack: the chosen reading needs the rejected one.** "Adventures as
> data with a persistent Master" was offered as a rival and set aside.
> But "adventures are scenes in it" has no meaning unless adventures are
> data the sandbox can host, and "dnd gameplay" has no meaning unless
> something persists between scenes. The Framing never states what.
> **Outcome: bent; revised, and answered.** The operator's defence
> supplies the mechanism: a living Master by choice at each scene, both
> save shapes, and a world that changes between sessions. The record now
> carries three things the Framing implied and never wrote: a **campaign
> record** (Master, world state, passages) that outlives any one
> adventure; an **adventure format** of which The 5 Treasures is the first
> file; and a **fresh-start mode** as a second way to open a scene. None
> of these are the sandbox, and all of them are first-release scope,
> because the cave is the first instance of each. See *In scope*.

> **Attack: "a world you keep returning to" is bigger than "a small
> explicit world-state layer".** The exploration sized the world state
> for one cave: day and night, hints revealed, key held, named foes
> defeated, per-area flags. A world that persists across sessions and
> "changes between them" is not a flags map owned by the adventure; it is
> a record the adventure writes into and the next adventure reads. A
> defeated Senior King is gone from the cave; is he gone from the world?
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The world-state layer stays small in the
> first release, but its owner changes: the campaign record owns it and
> the adventure contributes to it. The cost is a schema decision before
> the cave is authored, which the exploration deferred. Filed.

## Who it is for

- **The operator first.** Private builds; the store is a later `decide`.
  The falsifier is personal, the operator's own: **"if I stop playing it
  myself"** — which, by the operator's word, is checked against the
  sandbox, not the cave. The first release has its own test, below.
- **Solo wuxia players**, on a phone, without the PDF, the dice or the
  bookkeeping, when it is published.
- **Gianluca Monaco** is not the audience but the source: CC BY-SA 4.0 on
  the text means no gate on the rules, tables and adventure, and the whole
  repository ships under the same licence with attribution inside the
  game. The art is a separate question, below.

> **Attack: "CC BY-SA means no gate" is true of the words and unproven of
> the pictures.** The licence page (p. 93) is the author's declaration for
> the work, and it sits under a separate credit: "Cover artwork (except
> title) and pages 21, 33, 41, 57, 65 by: Cristian Cammarata". Nothing on
> that page says the artist licensed those pages CC BY-SA; the author
> credits them, which is what BY requires of him, and that is all the
> page shows. The adventure's icons and cave map are links to two itch.io
> pages (limofeus, watabou) with their own terms, which neither Findings
> read. And the Italian itch page discloses "AI Assisted, Graphics" while
> the English page says "No generative AI was used" (Findings 0004
> §2.12): the art's provenance is, on the author's own two pages,
> inconsistent. An app that reuses the cover and chapter art reuses the
> part of the work whose licence is least established.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** "No gate" now covers the text. The art is
> credited, not licensed, by the page it appears on; reuse waits on the
> artists' terms or a redraw, and the AI disclosure is a store-listing
> fact the later `decide` must carry. The whole-repository CC BY-SA
> choice holds with a cost: the ShareAlike obligation reaches the rules
> text by inheritance (`P-07`), while putting *code* under CC BY-SA is a
> choice Creative Commons itself advises against for software; the
> shelved split licence (content CC BY-SA, code MIT) is the fallback the
> exploration already named, and it stays on the shelf, not in the bin.

## In scope

The first release, as the exploration fixed it and this framing confirms,
with three additions the operator's defence made explicit:

- **The engine, whole.** Master creation with all 18 Martial Arts, the 72
  Techniques and Rituals each with a hand-authored effect record under the
  five-class scheme, actions, combat with Opening and Final Blow and
  Unexpected Events, multiple combat, escape and Dishonor, healing, XP and
  advancement, the market, the Oracle with Inspirations and Sparks, the
  encounter rules (R74–R77; the encounter *matrix* is sandbox content),
  treasures and Special Items, the 50 opponents. Pure functions over
  immutable state; dice injected; the UI never computes a rule.
- **The five sealed rules** of the Decision: the parent's ATTACK, the Final
  Blow LUCK exception, a night's rest at +4, the double-six fumble, Morale
  on retreat rows. And every remaining `I-nn` reading as a labelled,
  reversible default **whose id rides in the save**, so that reversing a
  reading later is a migration of recorded state, not a retcon of it.
- **The campaign record.** One Master, the world state the adventures
  have written, and the player's passages, persisting across scenes;
  opened either by continuing that Master or by starting fresh
  ("player's choice at each scene", both save shapes). **Export and
  import of that record** as a file: not sync, not an account, the only
  backup an offline app has.
- **The adventure format.** The schema The 5 Treasures is the first file
  in: areas, an event table, per-area encounter tables, spoiler-gated
  Hints, foes, treasures, and the world flags the adventure reads and
  writes. No authoring tool; a format.
- **The 5 Treasures**, complete: eight areas, the event table, nine foes,
  five treasures, spoiler-gated Hints, the key, the world-state flags (day
  and night, defeated foes, per-area state) written into the campaign
  record, the Monk as a rescue, act markers and a freeze-frame ending.
- **A trail-head village** at the foot of Flat-top mountain, built as
  **the engine's City on fixed data**: the book's City Services price list
  (R64), Temple (R58) and Inn as three of the seven city locations,
  placed by hand, no exploration. So that buying, LUCK recovery, meals and
  a night's rest have a home, and so that the first release touches the
  sandbox once, through the front door.
- **Menus and authored short text with an optional player-written
  passage**, the operator's own phrasing; the passages kept in the
  campaign record. The authored lines are the first release's largest
  content item and are unsized; see the attack below.
- **The eight printed Masters as presets, as printed**, overspend flagged
  and allowed — and **the same for a rolled Master**: creation's rules are
  checks the engine reports for everyone.
- **In-app dice with manual entry.** Expo and React Native for iOS,
  Android and web. Offline; no server. A `relate` edge to idea-0002 and
  nothing shared but patterns.

Beyond the first release, in scope for the record and named as the end
state: **the sandbox.** Region generation, routes and roads, monasteries,
city exploration beyond the village, the Adventures table, the encounter
matrices, the Master and the world carrying between all of it. The
Findings warn that any digital region generator is an invention, not an
implementation (A33); the parent offers a numeric distance rule the child
made arbitrary (Findings 0004 §3, `I-15`). Naming the sandbox as the real
game means that invention is owed, not avoided.

> **Attack: the village is content the book never wrote, in a framing
> that ships the presets "as printed" for fidelity's sake.** The
> adventure's Flat-top mountain is "a wild and vast territory covered
> with pines and willow trees, deep valleys and steep rocks, difficult
> paths for horses"; there is no settlement in either PDF. The Framing
> invents one, with a market, a temple and an inn, and calls the release
> faithful.
> **Outcome: bent; revised into a proposal.** The village is not the
> invention; the *bespoke* village would be. The rulebook already has a
> place where markets, temples and inns live: the City chapter, with
> seven locations and a rule that "every city is equipped with all basic
> services" (R64). A village at the trail head is a City with three
> locations on fixed data, and the engine's City module is sandbox code
> the first release then owns. Building it that way makes the village the
> first sandbox scene rather than the first departure from the book, and
> it costs a City module a release earlier than planned. The revised
> scope says so. The Framing should also drop the implication that the
> first release is faithful *in content*: it is faithful in rules and
> inventive in content, and the honest sentence is that one.

> **Attack: where else the first release invents.** A list, so the record
> stops thinking the village is the exception: the 72 effect records (72
> rules the book wrote as prose, the largest invention in the release);
> the Monk's Dishonor cost (an invention at confidence 50); Ogres absent
> by night (`I-45`); the Devil servant's loot on a 6 as a Hint (`I-08`);
> the cave's adjacency (`I-42`, inferred from a drawing); the act markers
> and freeze-frame (the adventure has no acts); the −2 for "very
> difficult" (`I-54`, "numbers are invented"); the Minions d6 fold
> (`I-33`); the fumble; and every authored line behind every menu.
> **Outcome: held, with the cost named.** None of these are wrong; they
> are what an engine for a delegating book must do. The cost is a label:
> the record labels *readings* "reading, not rule" and has no label for
> *inventions*. An invention is not a reading of the text; it is text the
> app adds. The rules panel should carry both marks, and content the
> book never wrote (village, Dishonor for the Monk, night Ogres) should
> wear the second.

> **Attack: "authored short text" is the unsized item.** The operator's
> phrasing has every Oracle reading, Unexpected Event and Technique effect
> resolving "to a short menu with one authored line each". The Oracle has
> 66 cells; the Unexpected Event table 11 rows; Inspirations 72 words;
> Sparks 216; the 72 effects each need at least one line; the adventure
> has eight areas and nine foes, each with reactions. And no model, on
> device or off, may write any of it. The Framing lists this in one
> bullet and never says who writes the lines or how many there are.
> **Outcome: held, with the cost named.** The operator is the author, by
> the out-of-scope rule on generated prose, and the count is in the
> hundreds before the sandbox. The exploration's shelved variant
> ("mechanical subset first, the other 64 effects narrative") is the
> release valve if the count proves too many; it should stay named. The
> cost of holding is that "engine plus one scene" is also "engine plus
> several hundred authored lines", and `envision` should size it.

> **Attack: the remaining readings are "reversible" only until something
> depends on them.** `I-05` was reversed by the Decision at no cost
> because nothing had been built. Once a campaign record persists,
> reversing a reading changes the meaning of saved state: a Master who
> learned four Techniques under the LUCK exception, an area whose Ogres
> were absent under `I-45`, a Hint revealed under `I-06b`. The 72 effect
> records sit on the five-class scheme; reversing the scheme rewrites
> them all. "Overturned by a later `decide` without touching the engine's
> shape" was true of a stateless engine and stops being true the day a
> save exists.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** Reversible defaults now carry their id into
> the save, and reversal is named as a migration. The cost is small now
> (a field) and real later (a migration per reversal), which is the point
> of paying it now.

## Out of scope

Stated by the operator as certain:

- Accounts, login, cloud sync.
- Multiplayer of any kind.
- Generated prose: no model, external or on-device, writing narration.
- Monetisation: no ads, purchases or paid tiers.

And by earlier word: other games in the Havoc family (the edge to
idea-0002 is kinship, not a shared engine); the sandbox in the first
release; **authoring tools** for adventures (the format is in; a tool to
write files in it is not).

> **Attack: the exclusions were written for the cave and the claim is
> about the sandbox.** "Adventures are scenes in it" and "a world you
> keep returning to" need two things the out-of-scope list either
> excludes or never mentions. *Save portability:* "no cloud sync" read
> together with "offline, no server" means a campaign lives on one phone
> and dies with it; for a game whose point is a world you return to, a
> lost save is the falsifier firing for the wrong reason. *Content
> authoring:* "multiple" gameplay needs multiple adventures, the book's 36
> hooks are one-line premises and not adventures, and no one but the
> operator can write in a format that has no tool.
> **Outcome: bent on the first, held on the second.** Export and import of
> the campaign record moves into scope as a file, which is neither sync
> nor an account. Authoring tools stay out, honestly, with the cost
> named: until a tool exists the operator is the only author, and the
> sandbox's "many games" are as many as the operator writes. The format
> is the thing that keeps the door open; it is in.

## The tension that makes it interesting

**An engine that must resolve everything, built for a book that delegates
by design, on a phone where every reading becomes a rule.** The book hands
the player its gaps as a gift; the engine cannot. The exploration's answer
was labelled, reversible readings, and the Decision showed the pattern the
operator follows when the parent game speaks: the child's words where it
wrote them, the parent's where the child only echoed, and the operator's
own invention (the fumble) where the operator wants a floor of risk that
both sources deliberately decline to give. The sandbox sharpens this to its
edge: the part of the book the operator names as the real game is the part
the book describes as a handful of dice thrown on paper.

One smaller tension rides along, and one has stopped being a tension. The
book's advice against ending fights with Techniques, which the engine can
show but not impose, is still alive. Fidelity to the print against
legality is not: by the operator's word, Yin's sheet ships as printed
**"never corrected"**, and **"rolled Masters get the same leeway"** —
creation's rules are advisory for every Master. That is a decision, and
the Framing records it as one for `decide` to seal.

> **Attack: the double-six fumble fills a silence that is not there.** The
> Decision says "both sources let a threshold of 12 succeed by
> arithmetic" and the Framing says "neither source gives the game a floor
> of risk". The parent does more than let it happen; it rules on it. Its
> FAQ, in the author's voice: a hero with ABILITÀ 12 "avrà sempre successo
> automatico nelle prove? Sì" — and the same when skill plus specialisation
> reaches 12 — and this "deriva direttamente dal gioco originale a cui
> Heroic Havoc si ispira" (Findings 0004 §2.10). Automatic success at 12
> is an inherited feature the parent kept on purpose and confined
> ("il successo automatico non si applica al combattimento"). The fumble
> repeals a ruling, not a gap. And the arithmetic says who it bites: a
> check with threshold 11 or less already fails on 6-6, so the fumble
> changes nothing below 12; it exists only for SKILL + Proficiency ≥ 12,
> and three of the eight printed Masters start there (Beggar So 10 + 4,
> Chen Zhen 9 + 4, Sun Wukong 10 + 4). "A Master whose threshold reaches
> 12" is not a maxed veteran; it is Beggar So on his first roll. On LUCK
> checks the fumble is nearly inert, since LUCK 12 lasts exactly one check
> under R21.
> **Outcome: held as the operator's sealed word, with the description
> revised.** Challenge classifies; it does not unseal. But the Framing's
> account of *why* is corrected above: the operator chose a floor of risk
> against the parent's explicit choice not to have one, and that is a
> stronger and more honest sentence than "neither source". Two things the
> Decision should name and does not: the Final Blow doubles roll (R30) and
> the Chaguan doubles roll (R63) are not checks, so a 6-6 there lands and
> wins rather than fumbles — the Decision names Attack Strength only.
> Filed.

> **Attack: the Final Blow LUCK exception against the child's own drafting
> habit, and against its own arithmetic.** The Decision reads "on a
> failure, lose 1 LUCK" as an exception to R21. The child's own book shows
> what an exception to R21 looks like when the author means one: the
> Gambling House says outright "You do not have to decrease your LUCK
> points when rolling for a bet" (p. 46). The Final Blow paragraph says no
> such thing; it says what happens on a failure and is silent on success,
> which is exactly the shape of a restatement (Findings 0004 `P-06`). The
> defence that the gambling roll is a comparison and not a LUCK check, so
> the author *had* to say it there, is fair — and it cuts the other way
> too: the Final Blow roll *is* a LUCK check ("roll against your current
> LUCK"), so R21 applies to it by default and silence keeps the default.
> Then the arithmetic. Under the exception, a Master at LUCK 12 passes the
> roll on every result, and a landed Final Blow becomes a new Technique
> at no cost, every time; at LUCK 10 it is 33 in 36. The Decision's own
> reopening condition, "play showing free Technique learning trivialises
> LUCK", is reachable on paper before play. The one thing that stops it
> being certain at LUCK 12 is Decision 4: the fumble makes it 35 in 36.
> The two rules the operator sealed against recommendation hold each
> other up, and the Decision does not say so.
> **Outcome: held as sealed, with the cost named.** The Framing's phrase
> "the child's words where it wrote them" is revised to what it is: the
> operator's reading of the child's words, against the parent's precedent
> and the child's drafting habit. The cost is a LUCK economy with one sink
> fewer and a Technique economy with one gate fewer, and a coupling
> between Decisions 2 and 4 that reopening either must reckon with.

> **Attack: the advisory posture arrives by accident and the Framing says
> so.** "That is the advisory posture arriving through a side door" is
> the exploration's own sentence, and the Framing carried it as "at least
> for presets", leaving open whether a rolled Master is gated. Either
> answer is a design: gate rolled Masters and Yin is a privileged sheet,
> a typo with a passport; do not gate them and the engine that "must
> resolve everything" declines to resolve its own creation chapter.
> **Outcome: bent; answered and revised.** The operator's word closes it:
> never corrected, same leeway for everyone. The posture is now the front
> door and the Framing says so. What it does to rolled Masters, named
> plainly: creation is a sheet the engine checks and reports, so a player
> may build SKILL 12 with four Proficiencies at 4 and the engine will say
> so and let them; the XP economy becomes optional for anyone willing to
> ignore a flag; and the flag must therefore persist on the campaign
> record, because a Master built over the rules stays one. The operator
> is the first audience and cheating is self-cheating; the cost is
> accepted, not absent. One consequence the operator did not address: a
> sheet's overspend is *which* number is wrong as much as *that* one is,
> and "never corrected" means the engine ships a sheet whose Proficiency
> line (10 against a pool of 9) and Ritual line (12 against 8 Resource
> points) both exceed the pool, with no way to say which the author
> meant. That is fidelity to a slip, chosen with open eyes. Filed as a
> decision for the Chancellor, not as a tension.

## Constraints carried in

From the operator's standing preferences, unchanged: functional style,
pure functions over immutable game state, no classes; explicit
documentation for every function from the lowest abstraction up; no
external service the game cannot run without; the whole repository CC
BY-SA 4.0 with attribution shipped inside.

> **Attack: none that lands.** The constraints are the operator's and
> they are coherent with the engine-as-package choice. The one place they
> pull against the record is the whole-repository licence on code,
> named above under *Who it is for*; it is a cost, not a contradiction.
> **Outcome: held.**

## The falsifier

The Framing's falsifier is the operator's own: "if I stop playing it
myself". Asked whether it tests the cave release, the operator answered,
verbatim: **"A milestone; the falsifier waits for the sandbox."** The cave
release proves the engine; only the sandbox is judged by disuse.

> **Attack: the falsifier tests the right framing and leaves the first
> release with none.** Filed before the defence: "if I stop playing it
> myself" would fire on a finished cave (a solo adventure ends; the Kings
> are dead; there is nothing to return to), which is the rejected
> framing's failure, not this one's. The defence agrees, and moves the
> falsifier to the sandbox. Conceded on that. But "proves the engine" is
> now a milestone with no test, and the estate's own named failure mode
> is that building is more fun than using (`system/FALSIFIERS.md`); an
> engine that is "the product" and cannot fail for as long as it takes
> to build a sandbox is that failure mode with a wuxia name.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** A Framing is not a Horizon and does not owe
> a dated falsifier; that is `envision`'s work. But a milestone that is
> allowed to be untested is a milestone that cannot fail, so the record
> now carries two falsifiers, one per claim, for `envision` to date:

- **The engine is proven** by the cave release if the operator plays The
  5 Treasures through in the app with the PDF closed and never has to
  open it — and is *falsified* if, finishing the cave with a living
  Master, the operator has nowhere to take them and stops. Not disuse;
  the moment the sandbox is owed, arriving before the sandbox does.
- **The sandbox is the real game** is falsified by the operator's own
  sentence, "if I stop playing it myself", checked once a sandbox exists
  and dated then.

The second is the one the Framing had. The first is the one it could not
see from inside a session that had already decided the cave was a
milestone: **a milestone's falsifier is the thing it was supposed to make
possible, not happening.**

## What a stranger can do with this

Build the engine now from the two Findings, the Decision and this
framing: every table is inventoried, every ambiguity has a default with an
id, five contested rules are sealed and their coupling is named. Design
the campaign record and the adventure format, because the cave is the
first file in one and the first writer to the other. Author The 5
