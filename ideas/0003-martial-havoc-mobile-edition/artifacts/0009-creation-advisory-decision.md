# Decision: creation is advisory at the pools, and the presets are never corrected

The Chancellor, recording what T decided after the Advocate's challenge
put the question and T answered it twice: once through the Steward during
the challenge ("Never corrected; rolled Masters get the same leeway") and
once here, on the three points the seal needed.

## What was decided

1. **The point pools at creation are advisory.** The engine computes the
   pools as the rulebook defines them: Proficiency points equal to the
   *rolled* SKILL (before Training deductions), no Proficiency above 4 at
   creation, and 4 resource points for Techniques and Rituals per
   Training point. It checks every Master against them, flags any
   overspend with the numbers, and never refuses.
2. **The overspend flag persists, visible on the sheet for the Master's
   life.** It travels in the campaign record and the save file; the rules
   panel shows the pool, the spend and the excess. No penalty attaches.
3. **Every other creation rule stays enforced.** SKILL, ENDURANCE and LUCK
   are rolled, not typed; one Martial Art is chosen; starting gold follows
   the social-status roll; an armed Proficiency adds nothing without a
   weapon. Only the pools bend.
4. **The author's eight pre-generated Masters ship exactly as printed and
   are never corrected**, Yin's sheet included: 10 Proficiency points
   against a pool of 9, and Rituals costing 12 against 8 resource points.
   The overspend flag marks them as it marks any rolled Master.
5. **Recorded as the successor to the earlier Decision's rules 2 and 4:**
   the Final Blow LUCK exception (−1 on failure only) and the double-six
   fumble on every check hold each other up. At LUCK 12 the fumble is the
   only cost on learning a Final Blow Technique, 35 in 36 free. T kept
   both with that in view; any later reopening treats them as one. The
   fumble's scope is checks only; the Final Blow and Chaguan doubles rolls
   are non-checks and a double six lands.

## What was rejected

- Correcting Yin's sheet to legal totals, with or without a note.
- Gating rolled Masters while presets keep their overspend.
- A one-time warning with nothing persisted; a persisted flag hidden after
  creation.
- Making all of creation advisory, or the attributes typeable.
- Reopening rules 2 and 4 now, together or singly.

## Why

T's reasons were the choices themselves; the Chancellor records the
following as inference, marked so no later reader mistakes it for T's
words. The author's own sheets break the pools, so an engine that refused
them would refuse the book (confidence 70). Extending the same leeway to
rolled Masters keeps one rule for everyone rather than a preset privilege
(confidence 65). Keeping the flag visible makes the leeway honest: the
record says what was spent, and a stranger reading the sheet sees it
(confidence 60). Enforcing the rest keeps "a rolled Master" meaningful;
the pools are where the print itself wavers, the attributes are not
(confidence 55).

## What would reopen it

- **Play showing overspent Masters trivialise The 5 Treasures**: a
  playtest where the excess decides the Kings' fights.
- **The author correcting Yin** in a later edition of the rulebook; the
  print changes, so the preset follows it.
- For rules 2 and 4: what the earlier Decision names, now read together.

## Tensions

**Resolved:** fidelity to the print against legality; the Advocate's
"advisory posture through a side door" is now the front door by T's word.

**Left alive:** advice against enforcement (the book's counsel on
Techniques is still shown, not imposed); an engine that must resolve
everything for a book that delegates; faithful in rules, inventive in
content.
