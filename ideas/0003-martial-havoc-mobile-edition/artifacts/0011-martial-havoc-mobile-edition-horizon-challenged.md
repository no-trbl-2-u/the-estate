# Horizon, challenged: Martial Havoc, mobile edition, on 2027-03-05

The Advocate, dispatched fresh-eyes with the packet, the Horizon, state
0010, the explored Framing, the two Decisions, the two Findings and the
challenged Framing for its shape, and no memory of the session that drew
the Horizon. The operator was live and answered three questions through
the Steward; their words are quoted verbatim where they land, and an attack
they answered is marked pressed or conceded on what was actually heard.
Every other attack is filed unanswered, which is weaker evidence than a
defended one. Where the Horizon held it says so and names the cost. Where
it bent, the revised text stands in the body and the attack that bent it
sits beside it. Nothing here is a gate.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The Horizon is a real picture, not a wish:
it names a day, a phone, a Master, a region, a fight, and a record, and it
says in its own words what would make it wrong in March. The Architect
then classified it on the wrong falsifier. The milestone test of
2026-12-05, as written, could be passed by a Master who kills the Junior
King on the Flat-top mountain and never enters the cave, and it *fires by
construction* if the Master lives, because on that date there is nowhere
to go by design; worse, the picture's own chronology puts the first
finished cave in January, so the Horizon fails its own milestone as drawn.
The claim that carries the Horizon, a playable sandbox on 2027-03-05, was
never listed as a falsifier at all, though the paragraph before the
falsifiers states it plainly and the operator has now said what
"playable" means: **"the whole sitting."** So the Horizon is
**`falsifiable`**, on two dated tests it did not quite write down, and
this version writes them. The sitting bends in six places where it drew
features the record never designed or numbers the sealed rules do not
give: the ledger holds a dead Master it cannot hold ("One ledger per
campaign record, one Master"), Beggar So wears Yin's overspend, a King
who fled on a 4 made a cautious retreat under the Decision's own table and
did not drop his sword, "two days' walk" is four miles of mule track under
the rule the Horizon itself cites, the authored lines finish after the
milestone that needs them, and a fourth label appears in a scheme of
three. The engine, the content, the exclusions and the claim against the
recommendation hold, each with a cost named.

**The classifier, stated plainly.** `horizon: falsifiable`. Two of its
three claims carry a dated test after this revision: the engine by the
cave on 2026-12-05 (evidence: a campaign record whose cave reached the
freeze-frame ending screen), and the picture on 2027-03-05 (evidence: the
whole sitting performed on a phone). The third, "the sandbox is the real
game", is tested by "if I stop playing it myself" and stays undated by
calendar by the operator's word; this version dates it by event, so that
it has a clock without having a date. A Horizon can be falsifiable while
one of its claims is still a hope; the classifier is for the Horizon, and
this artifact says which claim is which.

## A sitting

It is an evening in March. The operator opens the app on a phone. It was
installed from an internal track, not a store; the web build is a bookmark
on the laptop with the same save imported. No network request happens
after the install on the phone, and none after the first load on the
laptop, because the engine, the content, the rules panel and the adventure
files are one bundle.

The campaign record opens where it was left. Beside it, closed, is the
first record: Wei, a Long Weapons Master rolled in October, dead in the
Dining Hall in November on the second attempt at the Kings, on a night
when the gourd had been opened and the Ogres were out hunting. Wei's world
died with Wei. The open record is Beggar So's, chosen from the eight
presets, his sheet as printed and no mark on it: he spends 8 of 12
Proficiency points and 7 of 8 resource points, under on both. The mark the
Decision promised sits on Yin's preset, *overspent at creation, 10 of 9
Proficiency points and 12 of 8 resource points; allowed*, and on any
rolled Master who does the same. Beggar So finished The 5 Treasures in the
first days of December, before the milestone check. His ledger says Senior
King Golden Horn is dead everywhere, Junior King Silver Horn fled on a
Morale 2 and is alive somewhere with the seven-star sword still at his
belt, the Old Vixen was Kind and taught the cord's spell, the Monk was
rescued, and the Plantain fan and the Dazzling Golden Cord are in Beggar
So's pack. The Cord is inert until its spell is known; the spell is on
Beggar So's sheet beside his Techniques, learned in the Women quarter.

> **Attack: the ledger holds a Master it cannot hold.** The Framing chose
> "one record per campaign", and the record is Master plus ledger plus
> flags. The sitting drew Wei's death in the same ledger as Beggar So's
> deeds, which is a world-level ledger shared by successive Masters, a
> thing the record never designed. Put to the operator. The answer,
> verbatim: **"One ledger per campaign record, one Master."** A fresh
> Master gets a fresh world; the Kings are alive again; the world does not
> outlive the Master.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** Wei is a closed record beside the open one,
> not a line in Beggar So's ledger. The cost is now visible and is filed
> as a tension: in a game where Wei died on the second attempt, every
> death resets the world, and the region Beggar So threw in February dies
> with him. "A world you keep returning to" lasts exactly as long as one
> Master does.

> **Attack: the overspend is Yin's, not Beggar So's.** Findings §3.25:
> Beggar So spends 8 Proficiency points against an implied rolled SKILL of
> 12 and 7 of 8 resource points; Yin spends 10 of 9 and 12 of 8. The
> sitting put "12 of 8 resource points" beside Beggar So's *Proficiencies*,
> wrong sheet and wrong pool. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** Beggar So carries no mark. The Decision's
> flag is shown where it is true, on Yin.

> **Attack: a King who fled on a 4 did not flee, and did not drop his
> sword.** Decision 0005, rule 5: Morale 1d6, "1–3 the foe flees; 4–5 a
> cautious retreat; 6 the foe rallies". A 4 is a cautious retreat, whatever
> that costs the Master, and the Decision never says what it costs. And the
> seven-star sword is the Junior King's loot, which drops once on his
> defeat (I-33c); the Treasures roll follows "a victory" (R78). A foe who
> retreats has not been defeated, so the sword in Beggar So's pack is a
> drop the rules never give. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The Junior King fled on a 2 and keeps his
> sword; the ledger holds him as alive and fled, which is the more
> interesting ledger anyway. Two things the sitting exposed are filed for
> the Steward: what a "cautious retreat" does mechanically, and whether a
> fled named foe returns to the tables he appears in (I-33b removes him
> only when defeated).

> **Attack: the cord's spell is a fact with no home.** The Framing's
> ledger holds four kinds of entry: named foes killed, treasures held,
> NPCs rescued, Dishonor earned. Per-adventure flags never leave their
> namespace. "The spell is known" is none of these, and it must work in a
> monastery in March, outside the cave's namespace. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The spell lives on the Master's sheet beside
> Techniques and Rituals, an invention labelled from the adventure file.
> The record section below adds it, and adds "named foes fled" to the
> ledger, both marked as the Advocate's additions for the operator to
> accept or strike.

Tonight Beggar So is not in the cave. He is on a plain four miles from the
trail-head village, City 1 and Plain 3 by the parent's rule, four miles of
mule track, in a region the engine threw in February: seven dice on a
plane, each showing its Location; the other columns rolled as the book
says, Landmark, Resources and Risk when a location is first visited, Event
on every move and every new scene. Each location is linked to its nearest
one or two by the scatter, and each link takes its miles and its route
band from the two Location dice it joins, 2 to 12, Nothing to Paved road.
The region screen shows it as an SVG: circles, the links between them
labelled in miles, the village at the centre. The positions are
decorative and the screen says so; the miles are the distance. The app
labels the links *invention* and cites the Findings row that says why
(A33), and labels the miles *rule* with the parent's page. The operator
taps the Monastery landmark. A monastery generates room by room as it is
entered, from the rulebook's monastery table. Event roll: Encounter.
Oracle, Encounter Outcome: 5, a reaction. Encounter column: Monastery.
2d6: Shi Di. Oracle, Creature Reaction: Territorial. The menu offers what
the rules allow: talk, fight, leave, use a Technique; beside it, a text
field for what Beggar So says. The operator writes two lines. They are
kept. Beggar So fights.

> **Attack: the Horizon draws two distance rules at once, and the sitting
> obeys neither.** The Findings' I-15 makes distance the rounded Euclidean
> distance of the scatter, in scenes; the parent's rule (Findings 0004,
> I-15) makes it the sum of the two connected dice in miles, with the
> route band from the same sum. The Horizon cites the parent's rule and
> draws "a plain two days' walk from the village": a City die shows 1 and
> a Plain die shows 3, so the parent's rule gives four miles on a mule
> track, an afternoon. And the book never says which dice are connected;
> "throw dice on a plane" resolves in code to seven random points and a
> rule for which pairs get a link, and the link rule is the whole
> invention. Put to the operator. The answer, verbatim: **"Parent's rule
> carries distance; positions decorative; connect nearest neighbours."**
> Each location links to its nearest one or two by scatter geometry; the
> link's miles and route band come from the two dice values.
> **Outcome: bent; revised, and now a procedure.** Seven points; for each,
> links to its nearest one or two; each link's length is the sum of its
> two Location dice in miles and its route band from that sum. One rule
> from a source, one small invention for connectivity, both labelled. The
> cost, named: the parent's rule makes two Cities two miles apart on
> Nothing and two Waters twelve miles apart on a Paved road, because the
> distance is the terrain, not the throw. The Horizon accepts that
> because the book's "arbitrary scale" (R53) is exactly a licence to. And
> "arbitrary scale" on a phone screen resolves to this: the SVG is a
> diagram of links, not a map to scale, and it must say so or it lies.

> **Attack: the sitting rolls the region's columns at the wrong moment.**
> R51 step 3 rolls a location's characteristics when you visit it, step 4
> rolls an Event "when you move to a location or start a new scene"; R52
> rolls "additional d6 to determine other characteristics, if necessary".
> The Horizon rolled all five columns for all seven dice at the throw,
> including Event, which is per move. For a Horizon whose proof is that
> "every rule wears its label", the sandbox's first procedure was
> paraphrased. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** Location at the throw; Landmark, Resources
> and Risk on first visit; Event on every move. Held as a choice the
> engine may still make (rolling everything at the throw is a *reading*,
> and legal, if labelled).

> **Attack: R51 step 5 was skipped.** An Encounter goes first to the
> Oracle's Encounter Outcome row; only a 5 or 6 reaches a reaction. The
> sitting went straight to Creature Reaction. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; one line added.** Encounter Outcome 5, then the
> reaction. Small, and the kind of thing the labels exist to catch.

The fight is three rounds. The Attack Strength comparison shows both
rolls, the Proficiency each side added, and the difference. Round three is
a tie: an Unexpected Event, 2d6, a 4, *enemy retreat*, Morale 1d6, a 2,
the Shi Di flees. The panel beside the roll says *invention, Decision
0005, from Heroic Havoc* on the Morale line and *rule, p. 27* on the
Unexpected Event. Nothing was refused; nothing was invented silently.

> **Attack: a three-round fight that ends in round two, and a fourth
> label.** The sitting said three rounds and ended the fight on the tie in
> round two. And the Morale line wore "from Heroic Havoc", a label the
> Decision coined and the Framing's three-label scheme (rule, reading,
> invention) never admitted; the Horizon asserts "one of three labels" in
> the engine section and shows a fourth in the sitting. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The tie is round three. "From Heroic Havoc"
> is the citation's text under the label *invention*, because a rule
> borrowed from another book by a Decision is, for this engine, a Decision
> and nothing else; the parent's page rides in the citation. Three labels
> hold.

## The engine

A TypeScript package with no dependency on React. Pure functions over an
immutable game state; dice are an injected source, so the test suite plays
every rule with fixed rolls and the sandbox with a seeded scatter. Master
creation computes the pools, flags overspend and allows it, and takes
attributes as dice, rolled in the app or entered as dice, never typed as
totals. Combat, multiple combat with one Master roll against each attacker
up to ATTACK, escape and Dishonor, healing including a night's rest at +4,
XP by the SKILL band, the market, the city procedures the village runs on
(the seven locations, the Gambling House, the Temple, City Services and
city encounters), the Oracle with Inspirations and Sparks, encounters,
treasures, Special Items, 50 opponents, 18 Martial Arts, 72 effect records
under five classes. A double six fumbles every check and lands every
doubles roll. Every function documented from the lowest up.

Every behaviour carries one of three labels with a citation: *rule* (a
page), *reading* (an `I-nn` id), *invention* (a Decision or a content
file; a Decision that borrows from the parent cites the parent's page in
its text). The rules panel lists them all, filterable, and the campaign
record stores the ids active when each save was written. Reversing a
reading in February re-labelled three saves and said so; nothing was
re-played, because a save records what happened, not what would have.

> **Attack: "refuses to let them be typed" is false under the Spark's own
> dice.** The Spark chose in-app dice with manual entry. A player who may
> type the dice may type 6 and 6 for SKILL; the engine sees integers. The
> Decision's "rolled, not typed" is a distinction between entering dice
> and entering a total, and the sitting overstated it. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; one clause revised.** The engine refuses totals, not
> dice. The cost is honest: creation's enforcement is exactly as strong as
> the player's honesty with the manual entry, which is the book's own
> position.

> **Attack: a migration cannot re-play a reading.** "Migrated three saves"
> reads as if a reversed Final Blow reading could recompute the LUCK a
> Master would have had. It cannot; the dice are gone. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** A migration re-labels the ids a save was
> played under and, where a reading changes a stored value's meaning,
> converts what it can and says what it could not. That is still worth
> drawing; it is smaller than it sounded.

> **Attack: the village's engine is missing from the engine.** The content
> section runs the trail-head village "using the rulebook's own city
> procedures and price lists", and the engine section listed the market
> and nothing of R57–R63: the Gambling House, the Temple, the seven
> locations, services, city encounters. The largest unlisted subsystem in
> the picture, and it sits before the milestone. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The city procedures are named in the engine.
> Held with cost: the milestone now carries them, and `phase` should size
> them beside the 72 effects.

The rest of this section **held**. The test suite with fixed rolls, the
seeded scatter, the injected dice, the reading ids in the save, all follow
from the Spark and the Framing and add nothing the record did not choose.
The cost is what the Framing already said and the Horizon repeats: 84
rules, 63 readings and 72 effect records is the engine, and the effects
are design, not typing.

## The content

Four kinds of file, all CC BY-SA, all in the public repository:

- **The rulebook's tables as data**, every cell, with an authored line
  each: 66 Oracle cells, 11 Unexpected Events, 72 Inspirations, 216
  Sparks, 72 effects. The operator wrote the effects and the Oracle first,
  in October and November, because the cave cannot be played without them
  and the milestone is the fifth of December; the Inspirations and the
  Sparks between the milestone and the new year. The fallback (mechanical
  subset, word tables as words) was named and never taken; on 2027-03-05
  the count of shipped authored lines is a number the record can read,
  and it is 437.
- **The 5 Treasures** as the first adventure file in the documented
  format: eight areas, the event table, encounter tables, nine foes, hints
  gated until earned, the key, the treasures, per-adventure flags, act
  markers and a freeze-frame ending screen.
- **The trail-head village**, as a City on fixed data: market, temple,
  inn, using the rulebook's own city procedures and price lists, labelled
  *invention* for its existence and *rule* for its prices.
- **The sandbox tables**: region, route, road features, monastery, the
  five encounter columns, the 36 hooks. And the region generator, which is
  the one thing the book never wrote as a procedure a machine can run, and
  which is now written above as one: seven points, nearest-neighbour
  links, the parent's miles.

No art. Text and SVG only, by the operator's word. The cave is a room list
and an original SVG; the credited plates, icons and map are not in the
bundle.

> **Attack: the lines finish after the milestone that needs them.** The
> Horizon wrote the lines "between October and December, the effects
> first", and dated the milestone 2026-12-05. The cave needs the Oracle's
> Encounter Outcome and reaction rows on every Encounter event, the
> Unexpected Events on every tie, and every effect a Master or a King
> uses. A milestone checked on the fifth of December against content
> finishing in December is a milestone the picture schedules itself to

> **Outcome: bent; revised.** The order is turned: effects and Oracle by
> the milestone, Inspirations and Sparks after it. The cost: the two
> largest word tables (288 lines) are now the first thing the sandbox
> quarter spends its time on, in a quarter that also owes the generator,
> the monastery, the village and three builds.

> **Attack: "never taken" forecloses a scope decision the Framing kept
> open.** The Framing named the fallback so that taking it later would be
> a scope decision, not a retreat; the Horizon draws a March in which it
> was not needed, which is a hope about writing speed. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: held, with the count made checkable.** A picture may draw
> the full count; the falsifiable form is the number of shipped lines on
> the day, and the text now says the number is readable from the record.
> If it is under 437 in March, the fallback was taken and the picture was
> optimistic in that line, not wrong in its direction.

## The campaign record and the save

One record per campaign, one Master: the Master's sheet, with the
overspend flag, the passages, and the spells learned from adventures (the
Advocate's addition, for the cord); the deeds ledger, world-wide within
that campaign: named foes killed, named foes fled (the Advocate's
addition), treasures held, NPCs rescued, Dishonor earned; per-adventure
flags in their namespaces; the reading ids. Opening a scene asks: continue
with this Master, or start fresh. Both work while the Master lives; a
fresh start opens a second campaign record beside the first, with a fresh
world, the Kings alive in it. A dead Master's record stays, closed, and
can be read. Export writes one versioned JSON file; import reads it and
runs migrations if the reading ids differ. The operator moved a campaign
from phone to laptop in January and back.

> **Attack: the Horizon drew ledger features the Framing's schema does not
> carry.** Four entry kinds were chosen; the sitting used a dead Master
> from another campaign (not an entry kind, and not the same record by the
> operator's word), a fled King (not an entry kind), and a known spell
> (not an entry kind, and not a per-adventure flag either, since it must
> work outside the cave). The "dead King everywhere" is the one feature
> the schema supports as designed (I-33c, "defeating him anywhere removes
> him everywhere"). Answered on the first point by the operator; the other
> two filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent; revised, with two additions marked.** One ledger, one
> Master, by the operator's word. "Named foes fled" and "spells learned"
> are the Advocate's additions and the operator may strike either; if
> struck, the sitting loses the living King and the working cord, and the
> record should say so.

## What it is not

No account, no server, no sync. No multiplayer. No generated prose. No
money. No authoring tool, so every adventure file in the repository is the
operator's. No store listing yet; the internal track and the web build are
the whole distribution. Said precisely: no account *in the app*, and no
server *the app talks to*. The internal track is a developer account and
a tester's sign-in on the store side, and the web build is a static bundle
on a static host, installed as a web app so that it runs offline after
its first load; the host serves files and nothing else.

> **Attack: the exclusions are quietly violated by the distribution.**
> "No network request happens after the install" was said of a web build
> that is "a bookmark on the laptop": a bookmark fetches on every load
> unless the page installs itself for offline use, and a host that serves
> the page is a server. An internal track is Google Play's or TestFlight's
> internal testing, which needs a developer account and signed-in
> testers, so "no account" holds for the player and not for the builder;
> and the iOS leg needs a paid developer membership, so "no money" is the
> player's, not the builder's. Migrations against "offline" hold: they run
> in the app. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: bent on the wording, held on the substance.** The exclusions
> are the app's, and the revised text says so. The cost the Horizon did
> not name and now does: the three-target build has a builder-side
> account, a builder-side fee, and (for iOS) a Mac in it, and the sitting
> assumes all three exist. If they do not, the Spark's own shelved variant
> (installable web app only) is the fallback, and the record should know
> which it is before `chart`.

cave and its content done by the fifth of December. If March arrives with
the cave done and the sandbox begun, this Horizon is wrong in its picture
and right in its direction, and the record will say which. If March
arrives with the cave undone, it is wrong in both.

> **Attack: where does the picture assume time it does not have?** Not
> in the sandbox quarter, which is smaller than it looks: the sandbox
> tables are the book's own tables as data, the generator is now twenty
> lines of geometry and one rule, the monastery is the same procedure as
> the region with a different table. The time is assumed in September to
> December: every rule, 72 effect records that are each a design decision
> (A23), the Oracle, the adventure format and its first file, the campaign
> record, export and import, the village on the city procedures, the rules
> panel with three labels, and a build on three targets, in three months.
> The parts of the sitting that could not exist on that day if the cave
> slips by a month are all of it, because the sandbox quarter starts when
> the cave ends. Filed unanswered.
> **Outcome: held, with the cost moved to where it is.** The Horizon said
> the cost was "in six months"; it is in the first three. `chart` should
> draw the Trajectory with the December milestone as the load-bearing
> beam and the sandbox quarter hanging from it, and `phase` should size
> the effects and the city first.

## What would make this wrong

Rewritten. The Horizon had two falsifiers and classified itself on the
first; the first was passable from the mountain and fired by
construction, and the claim that carries the Horizon had no listed test.
Three now, each with a date or a clock, and the estate's own beside them.

1. **The engine is proven by the cave.** Checked on **2026-12-05**, by the
   operator's word. Evidence, in the operator's words: **"Ending screen
   reached."** Proven if on that day there exists a campaign record whose
   cave reached the freeze-frame ending screen, played in the app with the
   PDF closed. Stricter, and the Advocate's addition: the record's log
   shows no manual entry used for anything but dice, every effect that
   came up resolved from its record, and nothing was adjudicated outside
   the menu; the rules panel was the only text consulted. Falsified if no
   such record exists on that day, whatever the reason: not built, or
   built and unplayed, which is the estate's own failure mode arriving
   with a wuxia name. The clause the Architect wrote ("finishing the cave
   with a living Master, the operator has nowhere to take them and
   stops") is not this milestone's falsifier: on 2026-12-05 there is
   nowhere to go *by design*, and a dead Master (Wei) would exempt the
   test. It is the event that starts the third falsifier's clock, below.

   > **Attack: the milestone test tested nothing the milestone claimed.**
   > "A campaign record whose ledger holds a defeated or fled King" is
   > earned by a Master who meets the Junior King on the Flat-top mountain
   > (a 6 on its table) and kills or routs him without entering the cave;
   > it proves one combat, not the engine. And "never had to open the
   > PDF" is a weak proof when the app shows the book's table text on
   > screen: the player never opens the PDF because the app is the PDF,
   > rearranged. Put to the operator. The answer, verbatim: **"Ending
   > screen reached; and the whole sitting."**
   > **Outcome: bent; revised to the operator's evidence, with a stricter
   > proof offered beside it.** The stricter proof is about *resolution*,
   > not *reading*: the count of times the engine was overridden or
   > bypassed is what proves an engine, and the app can keep that count.

2. **The picture holds on 2027-03-05.** New as a listed falsifier; the
   Horizon stated it in the section above and did not count it. Evidence,
   in the operator's words: **"the whole sitting"**: a region thrown, a
   location entered, an encounter resolved, the record carrying all of it,
   on a phone, exported and imported once. Falsified if any part of that
   cannot be performed on the day. The count of authored lines and the
   presence of the village are read from the record on the same day and
   noted, not judged: they say whether the fallback was taken. If this
   falsifier fires and the first did not, the Horizon was wrong in its
   picture and right in its direction, as it says of itself. If both fire,
   it was wrong in both.

3. **The sandbox is the real game.** Falsified by the operator's own
   sentence: **"if I stop playing it myself."** Undated by calendar, by
   the operator's word, and this version leaves the calendar alone. It is
   dated by event: its clock starts on the day the second falsifier is
