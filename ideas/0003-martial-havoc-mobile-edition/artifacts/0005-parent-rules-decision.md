# Decision: five rules where the parent speaks

The Chancellor, recording what T decided after the Factor's second
Findings put Heroic Havoc v1.6, the parent game, beside Martial Havoc. Five
places where the parent contradicts or extends the child, each put to T
with a recommendation. Two went against the recommendation and are marked.

## What was decided

1. **ATTACK takes the parent's meaning.** An opponent's ATTACK is the
   number of opponents it can wound with one attack. Against a lone Master
   it is inert: a creature with ATTACK above 1 facing one opponent delivers
   one attack. Group size comes from the narrative or the Oracle's
   *No. of enemies* row; the Woodgatherers' "They" and the Devil servants
   are counted that way. Readings `I-05` and `I-05b` are retired. The
   exploration house rule (headcount when a group, SKILL penalty when
   alone) is withdrawn by T's word.

2. **The Final Blow LUCK roll is an exception.** Rulebook p. 25, "roll
   against your current LUCK. On a failure, lose 1 LUCK", is read as
   written: −1 on failure, nothing on success. The general rule R21 (−1
   after every LUCK check regardless) does not apply to this roll. Reading
   `I-12` stands. **Against the recommendation** (the parent's precedent
   is −1 always, and the Factor read the child's clause as a restatement).
   *Load-bearing.*

3. **A night's rest heals +4 ENDURANCE.** Restored from the parent ("una
   notte di sonno indisturbato"), which the child's Healing section
   dropped. The Rest event now has a mechanical payload. Labelled "from
   Heroic Havoc" in the rules panel.

4. **A double six is a fumble on every check.** Any SKILL or LUCK check
   that rolls 6 and 6 fails, whatever the threshold. This keeps a 1-in-36
   failure for a Master whose threshold reaches 12. **Against the
   recommendation** (add nothing; both sources let a threshold of 12
   succeed by arithmetic). An invention of T's, in neither source.
   *Load-bearing.* Its scope is checks only; an Attack Strength roll is a
   comparison, not a check, and is untouched.

5. **The parent's Morale table is borrowed for Unexpected Event rows 4
   and 10 only.** When the Unexpected Event is "Enemy retreat", roll 1d6:
   1–3 the foe flees; 4–5 a cautious retreat; 6 the foe rallies and gains
   1d6 ENDURANCE, and the fight resumes. Nothing else borrows Morale; no
   standing check at half ENDURANCE. Labelled "from Heroic Havoc".

## What was rejected

- ATTACK as the exploration house rule; ATTACK as one extra attack for a
  lone named monster.
- LUCK −1 always on the Final Blow roll; LUCK −1 always plus −1 on failure.
- Leaving the night's rest dropped; restoring it only in adventure rest
  spots.
- No fumble rule; an effective threshold cap of 11.
- Morale as a standing rule at half ENDURANCE; no borrowing.

## Why

T's reasons were not stated beyond the choices; the Chancellor records the
choices as T's and the following as inference, marked so no later reader
mistakes it for T's words. Decisions 1, 3 and 5 follow the parent where
the child is silent or drifted, consistent with the exploration's
"labelled, reversible readings" and the research T commissioned for
exactly this purpose (confidence 70). Decision 2 keeps the child's own
sentence over the parent's precedent: the child wrote "on a failure", and
fidelity to the print was already T's pattern in shipping Yin's sheet as
printed (confidence 55). Decision 4 adds tension for a maxed Master that
neither source provides; T chose a game with a floor of risk over a
faithful arithmetic (confidence 50).

## What would reopen it

- **1, ATTACK**: play showing the Kings or the Skillful Beast trivial
  alone; or the child's author ruling on the itch page that ATT means
  simultaneous attackers.
- **2, LUCK exception**: a later edition of Martial Havoc restating the
  Final Blow roll under R21; or play showing free Technique learning
  trivialises LUCK.
- **3, night's rest**: a later edition confirming the omission was
  deliberate.
- **4, fumble**: playtest showing the 1-in-36 fumble punishes the wrong
  moments (a rescue, a Temple check); or T naming it a mistake.
- **5, Morale rows**: play showing rallies on a 6 make retreat rows worse
  than the child's plain "the fight resumes".

## Tensions

**Resolved:** the child's gloss of ATTACK against the parent's (parent
wins); the parent has grown past the child (settled per rule: borrow where
T says, not wholesale).

**Left alive:** intentional ambiguity against code that must resolve it
(most readings are the child's inventions; T remains the rules authority);
fidelity to the print against legality; advice against enforcement; the
world-state layer's size; open sandbox against one adventure; art
provenance; the child's true source version.
