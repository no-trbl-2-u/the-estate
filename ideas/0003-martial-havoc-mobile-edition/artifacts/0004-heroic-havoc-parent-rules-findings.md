# Findings: what the parent game settles, and what it does not

The Factor, back from the walls. The commission was to read the game
Martial Havoc declares itself a hack of - "This game is a hack of Heroic
Havoc - ruolatorisolitari.itch.io/heroic-havoc"[^mh-rulebook-txt] - and to
settle from it the terms the child uses but never defines, listed in the
record's §6 (`A01`-`A63`, readings `I-nn`).[^hh-md]

The short of it: **Heroic Havoc is free, complete, and readable as plain
Markdown** (v1.6, 2024-10-03, Italian), so every answer below that says
*settled* rests on the parent's own text, not on a summary. But the parent
is a far smaller game than the child - 2,060 lines against Martial Havoc's
94 pages - and most of the child's undefined terms are **the child's own
inventions**, with nothing upstream to consult. Where the parent does speak,
it speaks clearly, and in one place it contradicts the record's reading
outright (ATTACK, `I-05`).

Throughout, **known** is cited; **inferred** is marked and given an id
(`P-nn`) so the operator can accept or reject it. All English renderings of
Italian text are **my translations** unless the source is quoted in
Italian; the Italian is given wherever the wording carries weight.

## 2. The twelve questions

Each answer is headed by its verdict: **settled by the parent**,
**contradicted by the parent**, **partly settled**, or **not settled**.

### 2.1 "Special" enemy attack versus "Normal" (Oracle row)

**Not settled - inherited undefined.** The parent's Oracle carries the same
row, equally bare: `Attacco Creatura | Normale (1-4) | Speciale (5-6)`,
with no definition anywhere in the book.[^hh-md] Galactic Havoc v1.0 has
`Attacco Nemico | Normale | Speciale`, also undefined.[^gh-txt] Neither
zine defines it.[^aa1][^aa2] Martial Havoc inherited the row and its
silence.

What the parent *does* give, which bounds the reading:

- Combat: "Tira due dadi; aggiungi il risultato del tiro e il punteggio di
  qualsiasi Competenza Specializzata rilevante all'azione di combattimento
  alla tua ABILITÀ ... Il tuo avversario fa lo stesso."[^hh-md] The
  opponent adds *any relevant* Specialised Skill every round - relevance
  is the trigger, not an Oracle result.
- Creature skills are two-or-three-word flavour with a value 1-4
  ("Sguardo Petrificante (3)", "Respiro di Fuoco (3)", "Attacco a Sorpresa
  (3)"); the creature template says "[due/tre parole, valori da 1 a
  4]".[^hh-md] The author, asked on the itch page whether enemy skills are
  "da interpretare liberamente", replied: "Le Competenze dei nemici sono da
  interpretarsi nel contesto della scena di gioco in corso"[^hh-itch]
  (my translation: *the enemies' Skills are to be interpreted in the
  context of the scene being played*).

`(inferred, P-01)` Read against that, "Special" is the round in which the
creature's named skill is *in play* narratively (the gaze, the breath, the
surprise attack), and "Normal" a round of plain blows. The record's `I-07a`
(Special = add a listed Proficiency; Normal = SKILL + 2d6 only) is
compatible with the parent and slightly *stricter* than it - the parent
would add a relevant skill on any round. Nothing upstream forbids either
reading.

### 2.2 "Ambush" versus "Attack" (Encounter Outcome)

**Not settled - the child's own.** "Imboscata"/"Ambush" appears nowhere in
Heroic Havoc v1.6, Galactic Havoc, or either zine (searched:
`imboscata`, `sorpresa`, `agguato`). The parent has no surprise, initiative
or first-strike mechanic; every Attack Round is simultaneous.[^hh-md] The
parent's nearest row is `Azione Creatura | Attacca (1) | ... | Sospetto (4)
| Ignaro (5) | Fuga (6)` and, for people, `Azione PNG | Fornisce indizio |
Allerta nemico | Mercanteggia | Attacca | Rivela scorciatoia | Chiede
aiuto`.[^hh-md] The parent's procedure step 5 reads: "Se la reazione è
ostile, tira sulla riga Reazione Nemico per determinare se avviene o meno
un attacco" - a row that no longer exists under that name in 1.6 (it is
"Reazione Nemico" in Galactic Havoc[^gh-txt]), which is the trace of the
1.5 table the child was probably built from `(inferred, P-02)`.

So the child's `Encounter Outcome | Ambush | Attack | NPC/Creature
Reaction` collapses the parent's two-step (reaction, then does it attack?)
into one row and adds "Ambush" as a third state with no rule behind it.
Note that the child also uses "Ambush (3)" as a *Proficiency* of the Bandit
opponent[^mh-rulebook-txt] - the same word as a stat, which is the only
place it has a number. `I-08a` (opponent's first round unopposed) has no
parent support and no parent contradiction; it is a design choice.

### 2.3 The Event outcomes: Rest, Effect Expiration, Weather Change, Conditions Change, Hint, Free Exploration

**Partly settled - the meanings, not the mechanics.** The six outcomes
are the parent's, verbatim: the Lande (wilderness) table's Evento column
is `Incontro / Riposo / Scadenza Effetto / Cambio Clima / Indizio /
Esplorazione Libera`, and the Dungeon table's swaps `Cambio Clima` for
`Cambio Condizioni`.[^hh-md] The child translated the two columns exactly.

The parent never defines them as rules either. But **v1.6 added
"specification tables"** ("Aggiunte le tabelle di specificazione dei
risultati di Esplorazione"[^hh-v16]) that roll each outcome into six
concrete results, and these fix what each word *means* even though they
give no numbers. My translation, parent's Italian in parentheses:

| Outcome | Wilderness 1-6 | Dungeon 1-6 |
|---|---|---|
| Incontro (Encounter) | Group of 1d6 creatures / Lone creature / - / Group of 1d6 NPCs / Lone NPC / - | same |
| Riposo (Rest) | Short pause / Natural shelter / Makeshift camp / Rough shelter / Full camp / Safe refuge (Breve pausa, Riparo naturale, Campo provvisorio, Rifugio rudimentale, Accampamento completo, Rifugio sicuro) | Short stop in a safe corner / Improvised shelter / Rest in an empty room / Camp found / Rest guarded by guards / Destroyed camp |
| Scadenza Effetto (Effect Expiration) | Food/water / Weather / Road / Direction / Part of the day / Rest (Cibo/acqua, Clima, Strada, Direzione, Parte del giorno, Riposo) | same |
| Cambio Clima (Weather Change) | Storm / Rain / Cloudy / Sunny / Sun / Scorching | - |
| Cambio Condizioni (Conditions Change) | - | Thickening fog / Total darkness / Rising damp / Sudden cold wind / Air turned suffocating / Sudden tremor |
| Indizio (Hint) | Track of passage / Remains of a camp / Anomalous natural sign / Trail in the ground / Lost object / Natural symbol | Abandoned diary pages / Symbol carved on the wall / Footprints / Blood traces / Voices / Verses |
| Esplorazione Libera (Free Exploration) | Beaten path / Clearing / Hidden stream / Cave / Rock formation / Old ruined bridge | Discovery of a secret passage / Room full of rubble / Unexplored well / Access to a side gallery / Door sealed by a spell / Forking corridor |

The settlement chapter (also new in 1.6) glosses Scadenza Effetto as
"Fine di un incantesimo / Cessazione di un contratto / Termina una
benedizione / Scadenza di una licenza / Pozione che svanisce / Protezione
magica"[^hh-md] - so in the parent's own usage the phrase covers *any*
running state ending: supplies, weather, a road, a spell, a potion, a
contract.

Bearing on the record: `I-17` is right in kind and too narrow in one cell.
"Rest" = a resting opportunity/place (the parent's healing rule then
applies: a night's undisturbed sleep restores 4 TENACIA[^hh-md] - see
2.10); "Effect Expiration" = *some running effect or condition ends*, of
which a timed Technique/Ritual/item effect is one case among supplies,
weather, route and time-of-day; "Weather/Conditions Change" = re-roll a
descriptor, exactly as the parent's sub-table does; "Hint" = a physical
trace pointing somewhere (the parent's word is *Indizio*, "clue", and its
examples are all traces, not oracle answers); "Free Exploration" = a
feature with no forced event. All six remain narrative in the parent: no
outcome carries a number, a check or a cost. Note the "Incontro" sub-table
gives blanks at 3 and 6 - the parent's own tables have holes.

### 2.4 Monastery/dungeon "Openings"

**Settled by the parent.** The child's Monastery column `Openings | 1 | 2 |
2 | 3 | 3 | 4` is the parent's Dungeon column `Num. Ingressi | 1 | 2 | 2 |
3 | 3 | 4` - *number of entrances*.[^hh-md] Same six values, same
position between the location-feature column and Atmosfera. "Openings" is
the translator's word for *ingressi*. The parent says nothing about what
an entrance leads to; its dungeon is generated by scattering dice
("tira una manciata di d6") so entrances are read off adjacency on the
table. `I-18` (number of exits, each to a newly rolled location) is
confirmed in its first half; the "each leads to a newly rolled location"
half is the child's exploring-as-you-go procedure, not the parent's.

### 2.5 Minions and "1-4 Minions"

**Not settled - the child's own.** The parent has no Minions rule, no
ENDURANCE-1 chaff, no "1-4" of anything (searched: `gregari`,
`scagnozz`, `subalterno`, `1-4`). Its group sizes are `Gruppo di 1d6
creature` in the Event tables and the Oracle row `N. Creature | 1d6 | 1 | -
| 2 | - | 3`.[^hh-md] Its enemy tiers are `Ruolo Creatura | Subalterno | - |
Guerriero | - | Boss`; Galactic Havoc's are `Tipo Nemico | Subalterno |
Guerriero | Boss`.[^gh-txt] The child's `Enemy Type | Minion | Subordinate
| Warrior | Boss` adds a fourth, lowest tier under the parent's three, and
its `No. of enemies | 1d6 | 3 | 2 | 1` mirrors the parent's row with the
fixed counts reversed. `(inferred, P-03)` "Minion" was coined for the
Unexpected Event reinforcement rule and then retrofitted into the Oracle as
a tier; its "1-4" is the child's, with no d4 in the parent either. The
record's `I-33` (a d6 folded to 1-4) stands as the operator's call; the
parent's own instinct for a group is plainly `1d6`.

### 2.6 ATTACK, and multiple combat

**ATTACK: settled by the parent - and it contradicts the record.** The
parent's FAQ, asked and answered in the author's voice:

> **Cosa rappresenta la caratteristica "ATTACCA" delle creature?** (Cfr.
> pagina 60) - Indica il NUMERO di avversari che può attaccare
> simultaneamente. Se la creatura ha un punteggio di ATTACCA superiore a 1
> ma sta attaccando solo 1 avversario, comunque l'avversario riceverà 1 solo
> attacco (la creatura non attacca 2 volte!).
>
> *Esempio:* una creatura con "ATTACCA: 2" è in grado di colpire con un
> singolo attacco 2 avversari contemporaneamente (può succedere se il
> vostro eroe ha un gregario al suo seguito!). Se la stessa creatura stesse
> attaccando solo 1 eroe, effettuerà sempre 1 singolo attacco in caso di
> successo.[^hh-md][^hh-faq-devlog]

My translation: *It indicates the NUMBER of opponents it can attack
simultaneously. If the creature has ATTACCA above 1 but is attacking only
one opponent, that opponent still receives only one attack (the creature
does not attack twice!). Example: a creature with "ATTACCA: 2" can hit two
opponents at once with a single attack (it can happen if your hero has a
henchman in tow!). If the same creature were attacking only one hero, it
would always make a single attack.*

The creature template repeats it: "ATTACCA [numero di avversari che può
attaccare contemporaneamente da 1 a 4]".[^hh-md] So in the parent:

- ATTACK is a *breadth* stat: how many of the hero's side one attack can
  wound. Against a lone hero it does **nothing**.
- It exists for parties - the henchman (Heroic Henchman is a third-party
  supplement[^hh-site]), Appendix A's group play, the animal companion of
  Arcane Almanac #2.[^aa2]
- Its range is 1-4; the parent's bestiary has 60 creatures at 1, 19 at 2,
  13 at 3, 4 at 4, and no ranges.[^hh-md] The child's Dapeng 10, Woodgatherers
  5, Skillful Beast 5, Brawler "2-4" are outside the parent's scale and
  convention.

Bearing on the record: `I-05`'s second reading ("for a lone monster it is
the multiple-combat SKILL penalty it imposes - an ATT-5 monster fights like
five") is **not** the parent's meaning and the parent's FAQ excludes it for
a single creature. The first reading (number of simultaneous attackers the
encounter fields) is also not quite it: the parent counts *targets hit*,
not *attackers*. The child's own gloss - "indicates how many enemies can
attack at the same time"[^mh-rulebook-txt] - has drifted from the parent's
"how many opponents it can attack at the same time", and that drift is the
whole ambiguity. `(inferred, P-04)` For a single-Master engine the honest
parent-faithful rule is: ATTACK is inert unless the Master has companions;
a *group* opponent's count comes from the narrative or the `No. of enemies`
row, and the multiple-combat SKILL penalty counts opponents, not ATTACK.
`I-05b` (Woodgatherers = a band of five) then has no parent basis; the
plural "They" is the only evidence and ATT 5 is off the parent's scale.

**Multiple combat resolution: not settled, with a lean.** The parent's rule
is complete on the penalty and silent on the dice: "Puoi scegliere quale
nemico attaccare all'inizio di ciascun Round d'Attacco. Il combattimento
procede come descritto in precedenza, ma puoi infliggere danni solo al
bersaglio che hai scelto. Tieni presente, però, che il punteggio di
ABILITÀ è ridotto di un ammontare di punti pari al numero degli avversari
che si stanno affrontando per la durata della lotta."[^hh-md] (*You may
choose which enemy to attack at the start of each Attack Round. Combat
proceeds as described, but you can deal damage only to the target you
chose. Bear in mind that ABILITÀ is reduced by the number of opponents
faced, for the duration of the fight.*) Two clues lean toward one roll per
opponent: "puoi infliggere danni solo al bersaglio che hai scelto" implies
the others are still rolling against you (otherwise there is nothing to
choose among), and Appendix A's GM-less mode has "ciascun giocatore
effettua i tiri per la propria creatura avversaria" - each player rolls for
their own opposing creature.[^hh-md] `(inferred, P-05)` `I-06` (one Master
roll; each opponent rolls; each pair resolves independently, damage only
to the chosen target) is the reading most consistent with the parent. The
SKILL penalty is the parent's own invention, not the 1980s gamebook's,
which fought each opponent in turn - so the parent already softened the
source once.

### 2.7 Opening, Final Blow, and the LUCK roll to learn a Technique

**Opening / Final Blow: not settled - the child's own.** The parent's
combat has exactly one winner's option: subtract the difference from the
loser's TENACIA.[^hh-md] There is no Opening, no Final Blow, no doubles
roll, no learning-by-doing. The child's four options (damage / Technique /
change weapon / create an Opening) and the whole Final Blow chapter have no
upstream.

**The LUCK decrement: contradicted by the parent's precedent, not by its
letter.** The parent's rule is absolute: "Dopo aver effettuato una prova di
FORTUNA, sottrai un punto dal tuo punteggio di FORTUNA, indipendentemente
dall'esito" (*after a LUCK test, subtract one point from LUCK regardless of
outcome*), and the FAQ hammers it: "indipendentemente dal risultato (che tu
sia fortunato oppure no), devi sottrarre 1 punto all'attributo
FORTUNA".[^hh-md] The child inherited that rule as its `R21`. The child's
Final Blow text - "roll against your current LUCK. On a failure, lose 1
LUCK" - is therefore either a restatement of the failure half of the
standing rule (with the success half assumed) or a deliberate exception.
The parent cannot decide that, since it has no Final Blow; but the
parent's precedent is that **every** LUCK test costs 1, and it offers no
exception anywhere. `(inferred, P-06)` `I-12` (no decrement on success) is
the reading that *departs* from the parent; the parent-faithful reading is
"-1 regardless, and nothing extra on failure" - i.e. the child's clause is
a restatement. Either way, the answer to the record's question "R21 plus
-1, or instead of" is: **not "plus"** - no source stacks two decrements.

### 2.8 Numeric effects for Techniques; a parent equivalent of the 72

**Partly settled.** The parent's counterpart is its spell list: 36
spells (a d66 table, `Lista degli Incantesimi`), each with a cost 1-4 in
parentheses and a one-line prose effect.[^hh-md] The mechanics around them:

- Casting is a check: "lanciando 2d6 e confrontando il risultato ottenuto
  con la soglia definita dal punteggio di ABILITÀ + Magia"; the cost in
  TENACIA is paid "indipendentemente dal risultato".[^hh-md]
- Buying them: Punti Magia = Magia x 3, spent against the spells' costs at
  creation; later, a new spell costs XP equal to its cost.[^hh-md]
- Each point of Magia permanently costs 1 ABILITÀ.[^hh-md]

The child kept the *shape* (a value 1-4 that is both the price and the
weight; a pool to buy from - Training skill x 4 Resource Points, from the
XP example[^mh-rulebook-txt]) and changed the *use*: Techniques need no
roll ("without making a roll check"[^mh-rulebook-txt]) and cost ENDURANCE
on use, where the parent's spells roll and cost on every attempt. Then it
doubled the list into 36 Techniques + 36 Rituals - the "Rituals require
preparation time" split has no parent; the parent's spells are all
instant.

As to numbers: the parent's 36 effects are as prose-only as the child's
72. Exactly one carries a figure - "Tocco Curativo (1): Restituisci una
piccola quantità di salute (2 punti di ABILITÀ o 4 punti di
TENACIA)"[^hh-md] - and it matches the child's Healing chapter (+1 SKILL
from spiritual regeneration, +4 ENDURANCE from healing
techniques[^mh-rulebook-txt]). Two others map cleanly onto child entries by
sense: "Resurrezione (4): Riporta in vita un alleato caduto con salute
limitata" ~ Punishing the sky's "revives with reduced ENDURANCE" (the
parent is equally unquantified, so `I-28` gets no help); "Invisibilità (3)",
"Levitazione (2)", "Intrappolamento (1)" ~ the child's stealth, flight and
immobilising Techniques. The parent's answer to `A23` is the same as the
child's: interpret in scene. Arcane Almanac #1's "Non si scherza con la
Magia!" adds optional doubles rules (extreme success/failure on matched
dice) and side-effect tables[^aa1] - a parent-ecosystem precedent for
giving a prose effect a mechanical edge, optional and third-party.

### 2.9 Unexpected Event entries with mechanical effects

**Not settled - the child's own table; the parent supplies only
precedents.** The parent has no tie rule at all: "Chi ha la Forza
d'Attacco più alta ferisce l'altro"[^hh-md] - on a tie nobody is higher,
so nobody is wounded, as in the gamebook (implied, not stated). The child's
"a draw ends the combat phase and rolls an Unexpected Event" is new.
Precedents the parent does offer:

- **Doubles trigger an event** - in the Oracle, not in combat: "Quando i
  due dadi riportano un doppio si scatena un Evento Random", with a Focus
  row (Rimuovi evento / Relativo a PNG / Relativo alla missione / Relativo
  all'Eroe / Evento Ambiguo / Situazione corrente) and an Effetto row
  (Negativo 1-3 / Positivo 4-6).[^hh-md] Rewritten in 1.6.[^hh-v16]
- **Enemy retreat** has numbers in 1.6's optional "IA del nemico": Strategia
  `1-4 Normale | 5 Difesa - Si ritira e subisce 2 danni | 6 Avanza`, and a
  Prova di Morale "quando si dimezza la TENACIA o il loro numero": `1-3
  Fugge | 4-5 Ritirata Cauta | 6 Rinnovato vigore, riacquista 1d6
  TENACIA`.[^hh-md] The child carries none of this (searched `morale`); it
  is a parent-side option the engine could borrow for rows 4 and 10.
- **Weapon loss** would have *no* effect in the parent: "in Heroic Havoc
  l'equipaggiamento non ha un impatto sui tiri dei dadi, ma ha due scopi:
  dare colore all'ambientazione e fornire un possibile bottino".[^hh-md]
  The child reversed this - armed Proficiencies need a weapon - so `I-30`'s
  weapon-loss reading (drop the armed bonus) is the child's logic, not the
  parent's, and correct on its own terms.
- **Injury, environmental change, divine intervention**: nothing upstream.
  The parent's only "divine" material is the Temple in the settlement
  tables (Risorse: "Oggetti benedetti").[^hh-md]

### 2.10 Escape and Dishonor, healing amounts, XP

**Escape: settled - the child changed it.** Parent: "la fuga comporta
sempre una ferita automatica inflitta dal tuo avversario mentre scappi.
Sottrai 2 punti di TENACIA dal tuo totale"[^hh-md] (*escape always carries
an automatic wound inflicted by your opponent as you flee; subtract 2
TENACIA*). No stratagem exemption, no Dishonor. The child kept the -2,
added the "Technique or some other stratagem" exemption, and added Dishonor
Points as XP subtraction. On `I-32`'s question whether the -2 is "damage":
the parent calls it a *ferita automatica* - a wound - which supports
counting it as damage suffered. Dishonor is entirely the child's.

**Healing: settled - inherited with one deletion.** Parent, `Guarigione`:
ABILITÀ potion or Tocco Curativo restores part; full after a whole night's
rest. TENACIA "si recupera parzialmente (4 punti) con il Tocco Curativo,
con una notte di sonno indisturbato o mangiando una razione. Si recupera
completamente con una settimana intera di riposo." FORTUNA "si recupera
completamente con una pozione di FORTUNA (che aumenta anche il suo totale
di 1 punto)".[^hh-md] The child: SKILL +1 from spiritual regeneration
Techniques, full after a night; ENDURANCE +4 from healing techniques,
spiritual regeneration, a meal, or a Health Elixir; full with a week; LUCK
+1 via a Spirituality check at a Temple.[^mh-rulebook-txt] So the +4 and
the week are the parent's; the child **dropped "a night of undisturbed
sleep = +4 ENDURANCE"** (only SKILL recovers overnight in the child) - an
inherited-then-removed rule the engine should not assume. `I-48` (one meal
per day): the parent's phrasing "mangiando una razione" ties the +4 to
consuming one ration; frequency is unstated there too. `I-46` (Special
Item 7): confirmed by the parent's Pozione della Fortuna, "ripristina la
FORTUNA e la aumenta di 1", and the Guarigione line says the *total*
(initial value) rises by 1.[^hh-md] Note the parent's cap is the gamebook's
- "I punteggi non possono mai superare i valori iniziali"[^hh-md] - where
the child's is a flat 12 for SKILL and LUCK.

**XP: contradicted - the child replaced it.** Parent: score 1-3 on
*Successo della missione / Uso dell'equipaggiamento / Evitare la violenza /
Pensiero laterale*, "Somma i punteggi e dividi il totale per 4,
arrotondando per eccesso" (so 1-3 XP per adventure); spend 4 XP per
ABILITÀ point (max 12), 2 XP per point in a known skill (beyond 4), 1 XP
for a new skill; a spell costs XP equal to its cost.[^hh-md] Child: four
categories (two shared: Mission Success, Lateral thinking; "Evitare la
violenza" became "Combat spectacularity" - a deliberate genre inversion),
no division, minus Dishonor, and a SKILL-banded price table.[^mh-rulebook-txt]
`I-52` (self-assessed) is the parent's method too. `I-53`'s "what is an
adventure's end" is inherited verbatim: "Alla fine di ogni avventura" is
all the parent says either. The band question has no parent.

Two more inherited facts worth the engine's attention: attribute
generation is the parent's (ABILITÀ 1d6+6, TENACIA 2d6+12, FORTUNA
1d6+6)[^hh-md]; and the parent's FAQ rules that a threshold of 12 or more
is **automatic success** on checks - "Un Eroe che ha ABILITÀ pari a 12 avrà
sempre successo automatico nelle prove? Sì, lo stesso succede se la somma
di ABILITÀ e Competenze Specializzate è pari o superiore a 12 ... il
successo automatico non si applica al combattimento"[^hh-md] - a
consequence of 2d6 ≤ 12 that the child, with SKILL up to 12 plus
Proficiencies up to 4+, inherits silently.
