# Seed: The Starvu agency site

A landing page for a Pennsylvania talent-management LLC handling promotion for
OnlyFans and premium-content creators, and the small amount of internal
machinery that makes the page able to say true things.

**Provenance:** `origin: idea-0003 @ state/0011`. That line is the only thing in
this document that points backward. Everything else stands on its own.

---

## 0. How to read this

Five parts, in the order you will need them.

1. **The generator** — the one idea the whole design falls out of. Read this
   first or nothing below will look like anything but taste.
2. **The Horizon** — the page at month six, and what would make it wrong.
3. **The Trajectory** — the route, and the one rule that governs its order.

## 1. The generator

Every impressive feature on the three reference sites is the same species.

A **live dashboard** needs a data pipeline behind it. Foxy's makes zero
`fetch()` calls on the whole homepage. **Social-proof toasts** need a stream of
applications behind them; Foxy's is a hardcoded array with Sarah from Miami in
it. A **chat input** needs a person behind it; the string `faq-chat-send`
appears in no script anywhere on the site. An **ROI calculator** needs outcome
data behind it; Foxy's own source comments call its constants *"realistic and
believable."* **Press logos** need press; AT's filenames begin
`imageye___-_imgi_2_`. A **login stack** needs a product; Eros's six live
membership routes are all titled *"Copy of Template."*

> **An assertion machine claims a thing the visitor cannot see, and needs a
> business behind it to be true.**
>
> **A disclosure instrument claims nothing the visitor cannot see. It shows her
> something that already exists and lets her check it. It needs no machine
> behind it — it needs a fact in front of it.**

Starvu has almost no track record and an unusually deep pile of facts: a real
entity in a real jurisdiction, a named man who answers the phone, a written
Talent Management Agreement that exists today, an admission policy with rules in
it, a stated 90-day walk-away, and — soon — photographs of real people who have
actually worked with him. On every axis a creator could independently verify —
named legal entity, jurisdiction, named answerable human, registration,
18+/ID verification stated, per-partner verification for couples,
earnings-not-typical language, non-affiliation with OnlyFans/Fenix, terms
deferred to a written agreement — **Starvu beats all three references.** Eros, a
working business, publishes none of it.

So the constraint stops being a budget and becomes a generator:

> **Build every feature out of something already true, and the no-stub rule
> cannot be violated by construction. You can then build as many as you like.**

The page at month six is **not short**. It is long the way a prospectus is long:
nothing on it is padding, because everything on it is a disclosure, and a
disclosure you delete is just a thing you have decided not to tell her.

### The test that sorts any feature in ten seconds

> **Remove the AI. If the feature still works, just worse, it is a disclosure
> instrument. If it collapses, it was an assertion machine.**

Remove the AI from the clause finder (§2.4) and you have keyword search over
clause headings — worse, still useful. Remove the AI from a chat widget and you
have Foxy's corpse.

Use this test on every feature anyone proposes, including ones not in this
document. It is the whole design discipline in one sentence.

### The related rule, for anything the site *says*

The no-stub rule, in its final wording after attack:

> **No interactive affordance may promise a capability that does not exist
> behind it.** A form must go somewhere a human reads. A calendar must have real
> availability. A chat input must chat or must not appear. A "portal" link must
> not exist.

And separately: **every service the site names is real.** Naming a service is a
claim about the business, not a stub — a different condition, governed
differently.

## 2. Horizon — the page at month six

One object: the landing page. Not the web presence, not the business.

### The scenes it has to work in

**11:40pm, a phone, one tap from a DM.** The first screen carries his sentence —
*You make the content. We run the business around it.* — and beneath it, without
scrolling, three checkable things: **Starvu LLC, Pennsylvania**, a registration
number, and a face with a name under it. Not stock. Him. Primary action: *Book
fifteen minutes.* Quieter, beside it: *Read the agreement first.*

She does not book at 11:40pm. Nobody does. She scrolls, and gets **the whole
shape of the arrangement before she is asked for anything**: what Starvu does in
four blocks, each written in the present tense about work actually performed;
then **who Starvu will accept** — 18+, government photo ID, platform
verification, both partners individually if a couple — stated as an admission
standard rather than a legal footnote, which is the first thing on the page that
reads as *expensive*, because a business that turns people away is a business
with a threshold.

Then the part no competitor has. **"What you would be signing."** The Talent
Management Agreement, on the page: clause headings expanded, the term, the exit,
the obligations both ways, and a button that downloads the document. She will not
read it. Almost nobody reads it. **The presence of it is the message.**

Then **the first ninety days as a schedule** rather than a promise: week one and
who does what, the weekly meeting on a named day, what "24/7 support"
concretely means and what its actual measured response time has been, and day 90
— *you can walk, and here is the clause that says so.*

Then the money: **what Starvu takes, what she keeps, what Starvu pays for.** One
table. In the entire reference set exactly one number is published by anybody.

Then the real questions creators have actually asked on calls, in their words,
answered in his. Then the booking.

She closes the tab. She books at 9:15 the next morning from the same link.

**The booking, both ends.** Three slots this week, because he is one man and
three is what he has — **scarcity that is true is the only honest scarcity
device available, and it is the cheapest to build: it is his calendar,
connected.** She answers four things on the way through: platform and handle,
revenue band, what she most wants help with (the list is Starvu's actual
services, so the answer routes the call), and **"How did you hear about
Starvu?"** What comes back is not a thank-you page: a confirmation naming the
human, the length and the four things it will cover, the agreement attached, and
a WhatsApp thread pre-filled so that if something changes she can say so in one
tap instead of ghosting. On his end the booking arrives with her handle, her
band, her chosen service and her source.

**Month five, a cold arrival.** Same page. What six months put on it:
photographs of real creators published with permission — the one asset in this
competitive set expensive to fake and cheap for Starvu to have honestly; a number
that belongs to Starvu, small and specific and checkable (creators managed, calls
taken, the actual median time he took to answer a message last month); an FAQ
that is twenty real creators' real questions; and one creator's sentence with her
name and face on it and **no figure in it at all.**

### The inventory — three tiers

**Real on day one.** Needs only the flyer, the friend, a calendar and a form.

| # | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Identity block** — named human, his photograph, Starvu LLC, Pennsylvania, registration, contact |
| 2 | **The agreement, on the page** — clause headings, term, exit, obligations both ways, downloadable |
| 3 | **Eligibility standard** — 18+, government photo ID, platform verification, per-partner for couples |
| 4 | **Eligibility check** — a short interactive "can I apply?" running arithmetic over those *published* rules |
| 5 | **The first ninety days, as a schedule** — onboarding, weekly meeting, support, the day-90 exit |
| 6 | **Services in the present tense** — chatting, advising, content protection, runway/modeling |
| 7 | **Booking against true availability** — few slots, because there are few |
| 8 | **Named-agenda confirmation** — the human, the length, the four things, agreement attached |
| 9 | **WhatsApp fallback with a pre-filled message** |
| 10 | **"How did you hear about Starvu?"**, required |
| 11 | **Per-channel link tags** — one query parameter per place he posts a link |
| 12 | **Compliance surface** — earnings-not-typical language, Fenix/OnlyFans non-affiliation, privacy, terms |

Two notes that are load-bearing. **Feature 2 is a question before it is a
build** — publishing the agreement is the friend's call, and if the answer is no,
features 1 and 2 do not ship and the Horizon loses its spine (§4, question 1).
**Feature 12's disclaimer presupposes an earnings claim**: carry it *if* the page
carries a number. A disclaimer over a page with no number is furniture, and
furniture is what this design avoids.

**Real once one cheap thing exists.** Each blocked on exactly one named event —
an event, never a date. The page ships without them and is complete.

| Feature | Blocked on |
|---|---|
| Photographs of real creators | permission landing — the highest-value single unlock on the record |
| A named creator's sentence, no figure | one creator agreeing; one is enough |
| Starvu's own numbers — creators managed, calls taken, median response time | one month of operating *and writing it down* |
| The split, published | the friend setting it |
| The real FAQ — actual questions from actual calls | ~10 calls plus a habit of writing them down |
| A services page as a route | one service delivered end to end, so it can be described from a real week |
| Content-protection log with real numbers | one month of the takedown assistant running |
| Client portal, unlinked (see §2.5) | second signed creator + one repeating deliverable |

**Not yet — do not build these early, however good they look.** See §5; each has
a reason and the reasons are the point.

### 2.3 The three surfaces, which have different rules

T's build question splits into three genuinely different objects, and the honesty
test binds them with wildly different force.

| Surface | Who sees it | Does the honesty test bind? |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal tooling** | the friend, his managers | **No.** A tool that makes no claim to anybody cannot be an assertion machine. A broken internal tool is a bad Tuesday, not a lie. |
| **AI on the marketing page** | a stranger, pre-signing | **Maximally.** The tightest-bound surface here; an AI widget is the closest possible thing to Foxy's dead chat input. |
| **Creator tooling behind a login** | a signed creator, post-agreement | **Differently.** She is not a stranger being persuaded; she is a counterparty checking a thing she was promised. That is disclosure, not assertion. |

Almost all the value is in surfaces 1 and 3. Surface 2 is where the instinct goes
first and is the one place the answer is mostly *no*.

And the second asymmetry, which decides the build order: **surface 1 tools
manufacture the facts that surfaces 2 and 3 are built out of.** Four of the
middle-tier features are blocked on somebody writing something down. An internal
tool that makes the writing-down automatic is not a side quest — it is the supply
line to the page.

> **Build the tools that produce facts, because this page is made of facts.**

### 2.4 The only visitor-facing AI on the whole route: the clause finder

A visitor types *how do I get out?* and the page **scrolls to and highlights the
actual clause**, quoted verbatim, with its heading. It generates nothing. It
performs retrieval and stops. v1 has no model in it at all — clause headings, a
search box, scroll-and-highlight; the model is a later upgrade to recall.

It passes the removal test (§1). Non-negotiable if it ships: **never paraphrase;
never answer what the document does not answer** (*the agreement does not address
that; ask him on the call* is both true and a better conversion than an invented
answer); **never touch money, earnings or eligibility outside the published
rules.**

**Its most valuable output is its failures.** Log every query it could not
resolve. That log is a list of things creators want to know that the agreement
does not say, and it is worth more to the friend's lawyer than the feature is
worth to the page.

**Translation, split in two:** machine-translate the marketing prose (the
category is international and nobody translates anything); **never translate the
agreement.** Translating a contract produces a second contract, and the two
disagree the moment the model picks a synonym. Publish it in one language and say
which one governs. **Translate the pitch, never the terms.**

### 2.5 The word "portal" names two different objects

*A login on a public marketing page with nothing behind the door* is Eros's
"Copy of Template" — an assertion machine with a password field. **Refused
forever.**

*A portal holding a signed creator's own agreement, her verification status, her
90-day clock, her weekly numbers and her takedown log* is a **disclosure
instrument with an audience of one.** Nothing in it is a claim to a stranger,
because a stranger cannot get in. Every item is already true the day she signs.

The resolution that keeps both true:

> **The portal is real, and the public site never links to it.**

No `/login` in the nav. No account affordance offered to a stranger. The URL is
*given* when she signs, or arrives by magic link at the address on her agreement.
Eros's failure was not having a login; it was linking one with nothing behind it.

**Note this is a live disagreement, not a settled ruling — see §6.**

### 2.6 What would make this wrong

The Horizon is classified **`horizon: falsifiable`** because of these. Every one
is an observation a one-person business can actually make.

1. **A named traffic source per link.** One query parameter per channel. Fifteen
   minutes. Without it, nothing below is readable.
2. **"How did you hear about Starvu?" on the booking.** The decisive one.
3. **A pre-registered number, written down before launch.** *Of the first twenty
   creators who reach this page from something that is not a personal
   introduction, if fewer than N book, the page is not doing the work.* T picks
   N. Any N. **This is the only falsifier on the record that expires** — it
   expires the day the page goes live.
4. **The disconfirming observation.** If the friend closes creators over WhatsApp
   without them meaningfully reading the site, the page was never the
   load-bearing instrument and this Horizon's premise is wrong.
5. **The disclosure bet — the sharpest.** At the end of each call the friend asks
   *what made you book?* and writes the answer down. **If twenty creators come
   off calls and not one mentions the agreement, the terms, the entity, the
   verification standard or the 90-day exit unprompted, the feature spine is
   wrong** and the page should be rebuilt around whatever they do say.
6. **The friend-publishes bet.** If he will not put his name, the entity and the
   agreement on the site, features 1 and 2 do not exist and the Horizon loses
   more than it can absorb. Answerable today, by text, before anything is built.
7. **The photographs bet.** If permission has not landed by month two, the page
   at month six has no uncopyable asset.
8. **The no-stub audit.** At month six, walk the page. If any affordance has been
   quietly disabled, unlinked, or drifted out of true — a slot that isn't real, a
   service he stopped offering, a response-time number that is now last
   quarter's — the discipline failed.
9. **The calculator refusal is itself falsifiable.** If creators ask *what could
   I make?* on every call and he answers by hand every time, the refusal cost
   conversions. The correct revision is then a **published range with the
   disclaimer** — a stated fact — and never a generator that manufactures a
   personal number.
10. **The tooling bet — the cheapest, two weeks long.** Hand him the call-notes
    capture and nothing else. **If it is empty at week two**, the operation runs
    in his head and tooling is the wrong intervention. See Checkpoint A (§3) for
    the narrowed re-route, which matters: as written, this over-reaches.
11. **The portal bet.** If signed creators log in once and never return within
    thirty days, the portal is a worse WhatsApp. Measure second visits. The
    re-route is not a better portal — it is delivery over WhatsApp with the portal
    demoted to an archive nobody has to visit.
12. **The takedown bet.** If the first month of content-protection work produces
    zero true positives, either the tool is wrong or the service is not being
    performed.
13. **The clause-finder bet, which fails usefully.** If nobody uses it, that cost
    an afternoon and the log is still worth reading. **If visitors repeatedly ask
    things the agreement does not answer, the finding is about the agreement, not
    the feature.**
14. **The surface-2 bet.** The claim is that there is essentially no honest
    visitor-facing AI besides retrieval over published text. Disconfirming
    observation: if creators come off calls saying they wanted to ask something at
    2am and had nobody, the conversational need is real — and the answer is still
    not a confabulating widget. It is WhatsApp with honest triage in front of it,
    disclosing the wait rather than inventing an answer.

## 3. Trajectory — the route to it

It is not a page being built forward. It is this:

```
  NOW ─── an errand ─── launch ─── a long quiet middle ─── harvest ─── harvest
          (minutes)     (weeks)    (a month, four fuses    (page      (page
                                    burning, page still     grows      grows
                                    unchanged)              once)      again)
```

**There is a month in the middle where the correct amount of visible progress on
the page is zero.** Four of the middle-tier features are waiting on facts that do
not exist yet and cannot be hurried: they need a tool built, and then they need
*time passing with the tool running.* A roadmap that fills that month with page
work delivers those features a month late, because the fuses were never lit.

### 3.1 The ordering rule

Every supply-line tool has two costs, not one. **Build cost** is T's time.
**Fuse length** is how long the tool must sit running before it has anything to
say — nobody's time, just elapsed calendar.

| Tool | Build | Fuse | Burns down to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claims ledger | afternoon | **none** | works the day it ships |
| Call-notes capture | afternoon | **~10 calls → ~20 calls** | the real FAQ, then the disclosure bet |
| Response-time meter | afternoon | **one month** | the median-response number |
| Takedown assistant | weekend | **one month** | notices filed / removed / median days |
| Onboarding + verification tracker | weekend | **one onboarding** | "creators managed", portal items 1–3 |
| Weekly numbers pack | weekend | **one week** | the portal's recurring payload |
| Clause finder | afternoon | none | gated on a *permission*, not a fuse |

> **Fuse length dominates build cost. Light the long fuses first, even the cheap
> ones.** An afternoon with a one-month fuse must be lit before a weekend with no
> fuse, because the weekend can be bought back and the month cannot.

That single rule moves the response-time meter from sixth to second. Same
afternoon, one month earlier, for one of the six numbers the page needs and has
no other mechanism for.

### 3.2 The seven Phases

| # | Phase | Starts on | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The errand** | nothing — Monday morning, from a phone | an afternoon, mostly waiting |
| 2 | **Ship v1, instrumented** | the v1 design returning from flight | **weeks** — the only multi-week Phase |
| 3 | **Light the fuses** | item 1: the friend's next call. Items 2–3: page live | three afternoons over two weeks |
| 4 | **The quiet month** | Checkpoint A resolving | two weekends inside a month of nothing |
| 5 | **First harvest** | whichever fuse burns down first, likely the photographs | an afternoon per row, no weekend |
| 6 | **The portal spur** | second signed creator + one repeating deliverable | two weekends — **deletable, a spur** |
| 7 | **Down, or out** | Checkpoint C firing at ~20 bookings | the remaining months; near zero in the warm branch |

**Phase 1 — The errand.** Nine written answers in one file, dated. Nothing here
is a build; everything here gates something months long, and two items expire. It
waits on nothing. This is §4 in full.

**Phase 2 — Ship v1, instrumented.** The twelve day-one features, minus whatever
Phase 1 struck out, plus two things the feature list did not have: the fifteen
minutes of instrumentation (§3.3) and the careers tab brought under the no-stub
rule (§3.4). Only questions 1 and 2 gate anything here, and only features 1 and 2.
**The page can be built while the friend is thinking.**

**One free thing to do while building, which is not a build.** Keep a plain list
of every factual claim the page makes as the page is written — the claim, its
source, who can verify it. **Do not build anything to read it**; the reader is the
claims ledger in Phase 3. Writing the list while the claims are being authored
costs zero minutes. **Reconstructing it later from a finished page is
archaeology, and archaeology is where claims get missed.** Available only during
this Phase.

**Phase 3 — Light the fuses.** Three afternoons; no visitor sees any of it.
Ordered by fuse, not by value.

1. **The call-notes capture.** Ninety seconds after each call, into one place:
   what she asked in her words, what he answered, and her answer to *what made
   you book?* Most of the afternoon goes on **activation energy**, not on the
   model — a phone shortcut, a message to himself, a bot; anything cheaper than
   opening a laptop. That is the build. The AI part is trivial and real: cluster
   twenty raw notes into the eight recurring questions and draft the FAQ in his
   phrasing rather than marketing's. **Notes after a call, never recording during
   one** — Pennsylvania is all-party consent for oral communications; the safe
   build is also the cheap one. **This item does not wait for Phase 2.** Its fuse
   is measured in calls and he is already taking calls; every call taken before
   the notebook exists is a call spent, and the fuse is not buyable back. It
   overlaps Phase 2 deliberately.
2. **The response-time meter.** Parses the support channel's export, reports the
   median. Gated on question 5. It is the only honest form of the flyer's most
   exposed line: every agency claims 24/7 support; one agency can publish what it
   actually was.
3. **The claims ledger.** Every factual claim on the page as a row — claim,
   source, who can verify it, when last verified. The build reads the file; a
   stale claim fails the build, or renders with a visible *last verified* date, or
   both. No fuse, so it goes last of the three — but it goes here rather than
   later because it is **cheapest to install when there is almost nothing to
   enumerate.** It converts the month-six audit from a habit into a mechanism,
   which is the single reason that audit will happen at all.

**Phase 4 — The quiet month.** Two weekend builds — the manual-assist takedown
assistant (gated on question 4) and the onboarding-and-verification tracker
(gated on Checkpoint A, and on the vendor chosen in Phase 1) — inside a month of
elapsed operating in which **the page does not change.**

> **The test of this Phase: at the end of it, the page is byte-for-byte what it
> was at the end of Phase 2**, except for `last verified` dates advancing. If the
> page changed, ask what fuse got shortened to pay for it.

At the end of the month four facts exist that did not before: a median response
time, a takedown count, at least one completed onboarding, and roughly ten calls'
worth of real questions. **Those four facts are what Phase 5 publishes.** Nothing
else on this route can manufacture them.

If T has a spare afternoon in this month, the two honest uses are: the `research`
on platform and model-provider terms that runs beside the route, or **building the
one-hour unpublish path** that Phase 5 requires as a gate — build it now, while
everything is calm, because by definition you need it on the day nothing is.

**Phase 5 — First harvest.** The page grows in bursts, one row at a time, as each
fuse burns down. **Nothing is sequenced behind anything else here**, and the only
way to make this Phase late is to hold a landed fact back so the growth can
"launch" all at once.

| What lands | When its fuse burns down | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Photographs of real creators** | permission — no fuse, no tool, any week | afternoon |
| Starvu's own numbers | one month of operating | afternoon |
| Median response time | one month | an hour |
| The real FAQ | ~10 calls | afternoon |
| The split, published | the friend sets it | an hour |
| One creator's sentence, no figure | one creator agreeing | an hour |
| A services page as a route | one service delivered end to end | afternoon |
| The clause finder | the agreement published | afternoon, no model in v1 |
| Content-protection log with real numbers | one month of takedown work | afternoon |
| **The team, evidenced** | one role actually filled — *not* the day the tab shipped | an hour |

**Phase 6 — The portal spur.** Behind a door nobody advertises: her signed
agreement (the version she signed, dated), her verification status, her 90-day
clock and the clause that lets her walk, her weekly numbers pack, her takedown
log. Items 1–3 are **already true on day one of a signed relationship and cost an
afternoon between them** — and they are exactly what a creator in this industry is
most anxious about: *what did I sign, is my ID handled properly, and can I
actually leave?*

**The design rules are the design and do not retrofit:** no legal names in it
(the verification vendor holds identity; the portal stores `verified: true` and a
date); no identity documents in it ever; earnings displayed from a store she can
already see, never warehoused; magic-link auth so there is no password to reuse;
signed, expiring URLs on every file; short retention on anything she does not
need. **Done looks like:** assume total compromise and list what an attacker now
knows. If a legal name is on that list, the rules were not followed.

**This is a spur, not trunk.** If it never happens nothing else changes: the
numbers pack stays a WhatsApp attachment, the takedown log stays a message, and
the page is unaffected because the page never linked here in any branch.

**Phase 7 — Down, or out.** The page is the same page in both branches; only what
gets added around the booking differs, which is why this fork was safe to carry
unresolved through five Phases.

- **Warm** (he DMs the link — the only distribution mechanism in evidence
  anywhere in Starvu's own material, and the branch to expect rather than hope
  against): the page goes **down, not out**. Depth on the agreement, depth on the
  ninety days, depth on the post-booking experience, photographs mid-page as
  reinforcement. **No acquisition surface is built at all.** And the honest
  possibility — a finding, not a failure — that the page was never the
  load-bearing instrument.
- **Cold**: photographs and Starvu's own numbers move above the fold and become
  load-bearing, and the six months buy **exactly one acquisition instrument** — a
  referral mechanism with real attribution, which becomes honest for Starvu
  specifically once there are signed creators to do the referring. **Not a blog.**
  ~115 posts is a different business with a different staff.

**Not both branches.** Building depth *and* an acquisition instrument is what the
checkpoint exists to prevent, and it is what will feel reasonable on the day.

### 3.3 The fifteen minutes

Per-channel link tags and the required *"How did you hear about Starvu?"* field
are not features. **They are the denominator.**

Without them Checkpoint C has nothing to read, the warm/cold branch never
settles, and the record carries an unsettled fork into months four to six — the
exact failure mode of a claim that is unfalsifiable by construction. **Under
launch pressure this is the first thing that gets cut, because it is invisible and
serves nobody looking at the page.**

> **Do not ship v1 without it.** This is the strongest single instruction in this
> document.

If the page ships and this did not, Phases 4 through 7 are being run blind, and
the honest thing is to write that down rather than read the numbers anyway.

### 3.4 The careers tab

**Note on provenance: this arrived from T in conversation on 2026-08-27 — "we've
added a 'careers' tab to the site where people can sign up to work for the
company", past tense, it exists — and it did not go through `capture`.** It is
worked into the Phases as a given, not established as a finding. Treat it as a
fact about the near horizon reported by the operator, and be aware the record
carries it without an artifact of its own.

It puts a second person with a different job on an instrument designed for one
visitor with one job. The answer is a **seam, not a wall.**

**What the two funnels share, and should:** all the disclosure furniture — the
identity block, Starvu LLC and the jurisdiction, the registration, the named
human, the compliance surface, the non-affiliation line. An applicant evaluating
an employer wants the same three things a creator wants: that the entity is real,
that a person is answerable, that the terms exist. **The verifiability ground is
audience-neutral, which is why the tab costs an afternoon.**

**What they must not share:**

| Must not share | Why |
|---|---|
| The **"How did you hear about Starvu?"** field | Checkpoint C reads that field to settle warm versus cold **for creators**. Applicants answering into the same field put a second population into the one instrument the record has. **It is not a contaminated signal that can be cleaned later — it is a signal that cannot be un-mixed.** If the careers surface asks at all, it asks into a separate store. |
| The **link-tag namespace** | Same reason, one layer down. A job post shared in a chatter community and a creator DM must not increment the same counter. One prefix per funnel, decided now, costs nothing. |
| The **booking calendar** | The creator call is fifteen minutes against true scarcity, and it is the page's terminal action. An applicant consuming a slot is consuming the scarce thing. The cheap correct answer: a second Calendly event type, one link per role, role pre-filled. **No ATS.** |
| The **eligibility standard** | 18+/ID/platform verification/per-partner is Starvu's *creator admission policy*. It is not a hiring policy and must not read as one. |
| The **split table and any earnings language** | Those describe what a creator keeps. Reused on a careers surface they describe compensation — a different claim with a different exposure. |

**Applicant data is worker data.** Free at build time, expensive to retrofit:
**no identity document, ever, at any stage of application** (right to work is
verified at hire, by whoever handles payroll, not by a form on a marketing site);
**the doxxing threat model extends to applicants** — no names in URLs, no names
in email subject lines, no third-party analytics on the submission path; **short
retention** on applications that go nowhere.

**The gate on shipping it** is question 6. If there is a named reader and a
stated window, the tab ships and **the window goes on the page** — *we reply to
applications within X days* — which is the same disclosure move the whole page
makes, pointed at a second audience, and nobody in the reference set makes it. If
there is no reader, the tab does not ship in its current form; **the replacement
is one line and one link:** *we hire through referrals — write to ⟨channel⟩.*

### 3.5 The checkpoints, with their re-routes

Six. A checkpoint that can only add is not a checkpoint — **B and F can shrink
the page, and D can change the destination.**

**A — two weeks after the call-notes capture is handed over. The tooling bet.**
*If the notebook is empty at week two, the operation runs in his head.*

The re-route as originally written — *build nothing else internal* — over-reaches,
because half the supply line requires no habit at all. The correct narrowing:

> **Build only tools that watch. Never tools that ask.**

What dies: the notes capture, the verification tracker, the weekly numbers pack —
the class that asks the friend to change his Tuesday. What survives: the claims
ledger (build time, touches T only), the response-time meter (parses an export,
touches nobody), the takedown assistant (event-driven, used when a leak is
found). **And reading the careers inbox is a watch, not an ask** — so the tab's
obligations survive a failed A, which also means the tab cannot hide behind one.

**The second half of the re-route, pre-committed now rather than improvised
then, and it is the serious one: falsifier 10 firing disarms falsifier 5.** The
notes capture is the only instrument that runs the disclosure bet, so an empty
notebook silently removes the record's ability to check its own central claim.

> **Move the question off his Tuesday and onto the machine.** *What made you
> book?* goes into the booking confirmation, or a one-question follow-up after the
> call. The page already sends a named-agenda confirmation and already opens a
> WhatsApp thread — the carrier exists and costs an afternoon to point at a new
> question.

The answers will be worse than his notes would have been. **Worse answers to a
live falsifier beat perfect answers to a dead one.**

**B — one month of takedown work. The takedown bet.** Zero true positives means
either the tool is wrong (a build problem, cheap) or the service is not performed
— and the second answer **removes content protection from the services block**,
from the harvest, and from portal item 5. The instinct on the day will be to keep
the services row and try harder at the tool. **The honest move is to delete the
row and record why.**

**F — one month of the careers tab. The inbox bet.** *If applications arrived in
month one and nobody answered them, the tab is an affordance promising a capability
the business does not have.* The creator funnel's failure mode is a stranger who
does not book. **The careers funnel's failure mode is a person who applied and was
ignored** — aimed at exactly the labour pool this business needs, in a small
industry where people talk to each other. The exposure is not detection
probability, it is blast radius. **Re-route:** name a reader and publish the
window, or take the tab down and replace it with one line and a channel. Both are
honest. Leaving an unread form up is the only dishonest option, and it is the
default one. **For free, this checkpoint also reads:** how many applied, for which
roles, and from where.

**C — roughly twenty bookings. The traffic read.** Fires on *booking volume*, not
calendar, so it may arrive before or after the harvest; the plan tolerates that.
**If the fifteen minutes were cut, this checkpoint cannot be read** — not "is
harder to read", cannot. The honest response is to record the fork as
**permanently unsettled** and say so, rather than read a field that cannot support
the reading.

**D — twenty calls. The disclosure bet.** *If twenty creators come off calls and
not one mentions the agreement, the terms, the entity, the verification standard
or the 90-day exit unprompted, the feature spine is wrong.* **This is the only
checkpoint that changes the destination rather than the path.** The re-route is
not an adjustment — it is **back to `envision`**, with whatever the twenty
creators *did* say in hand. **The supply line survives intact**: the tools that
produced the finding are the tools the next Horizon would need, so a firing here
costs the drawing, not the machinery. **It depends entirely on Checkpoint A
having passed, or on A's fallback having been installed.** If neither happened,
this checkpoint cannot run, and the honest thing is to say the central claim is
unchecked rather than read a checkpoint with no instrument behind it.

**E — month six. The no-stub audit.** Walk the page. A slot that isn't real. A
service he stopped offering. A response-time number that is now last quarter's.
**A careers tab advertising a role that closed in month two — the likeliest drift
on the whole page, because nobody on the creator funnel ever visits it, which is
exactly the condition under which things rot.** If the claims ledger shipped,
this is not a walk; it is a build step that has been failing loudly since the day
the drift started. If it did not ship, this is ten minutes of walking and **it
will be skipped**, because month-six habits set in month zero are the ones that
do not survive.

### 3.6 Three gates that must precede something

Each is free if noticed now and unrecoverable if noticed after.

1. **The one-hour unpublish path must exist before the first creator photograph
   ships.** Publishing a real face is irreversible, and permission granted in
   month two may be regretted in month twelve. Trivial if decided at the start,
   never retrofitted under calm conditions — **by definition you need it on the
   day everything is not calm.** Exercise it once, on purpose, before a real face
   goes up. The permission itself should be specific and revocable in writing;
   that is the friend's paperwork, not T's build.
2. **The disclosure clause must exist before the first chatter is hired.** A
   person hired to speak as a creator is precisely what the clause is about. Hire
   first and the first creator's agreement is silent about the person now typing
   in her DMs — **and the strongest unclaimed position on the record is spent
   before it was taken.** *"Our agreement names exactly who can speak as you, and
   what they cannot say"* is a sentence no competitor can currently say, about a
   practice all of them perform. **The clause precedes the hire.** It is question
   8, and it is the friend's and his lawyer's, not T's.
3. **The verification vendor must be chosen before any ID moves.** See §4 and
   §5.2. It gates nothing downstream, which is exactly why it slips — and it is
   the only item on the route where **delay compounds.**

### 3.7 What runs beside the route, on no Phase

**`research` on platform and model-provider terms.** The only genuinely checkable
unknown on the record. It is not on the critical path of anything in Phases 1–7,
because every tool on this route handles *business* text. It is on the critical
path of the chat-drafting assistant, which is off the route entirely and stays off
it until that research returns. It can run at any time and **should run before a
line of that code is written.** Hiring a chatter does not change this: it is the
*tool* that is gated, not the person.

## 5. Refusals

**These are load-bearing, not garnish.** Each is refused for a reason, and the
reasons are the most valuable thing in this document. A refusal without its
argument will be overturned by the first person who wants the thing back — and
somebody will want the calculator back.

### 5.1 Refused on the page

**The earnings calculator. Refused outright, at v1 and at month six, in the
generator form the category uses.** A calculator is an assertion machine with a
slider on it: it manufactures a personalised earnings claim for every visitor out
of constants nobody can defend. Foxy's own source comments call its tiers
*"realistic and believable"* — the sentence of a person who knows they are not
measured. Starvu's flyer already carries earnings-not-typical language, which
means **somebody at Starvu understood this exposure before there was a website.**
Installing a machine that generates a fresh number per visitor is the single
cheapest way to convert the most verifiable business in the reference set into the
least, and it would do it in an afternoon, which is exactly what makes it
dangerous. Two of three references have one; that makes it category furniture, not
a requirement. **What replaces it, and it is better: the split table.** What
Starvu takes, what she keeps, what Starvu pays for. Real arithmetic over one real
constant. Where the category answers *what could you make?* with a fantasy, Starvu
answers *what would this cost you?* with a fact — which is the question a
professional evaluating a vendor is actually asking. *(Falsifiable: see
falsifier 9.)*

**Chat of any kind, including — especially — a working AI chat.** The original
argument was the no-stub one: Foxy ships `faq-chat-send` with no script behind it
anywhere on the site. An AI widget appears to solve that, because it has something
behind it. **That is the problem. Foxy's chat input lies by doing nothing. An AI
chat input would lie by doing something.** Consider what a visitor asks a chat box
on *this* page: *What's the split? Can I leave? What happens to my ID? Do I have
to do calls? What if my partner won't verify? What could I make?* Every one is a
question about the terms of a written agreement, an admission policy, or an
earnings expectation. Every plausible answer is **a representation by a
Pennsylvania LLC to a prospective counterparty, generated at scale, unreviewed, at
2am, with no transcript discipline and no human in it.** The last of those
questions is the exact one the calculator was refused for answering — and prose is
worse than a slider, because it sounds like a person promising. The failure mode
is not a dead widget; it is **a live widget that confabulates a term.** And a
business with a handful of creators, in an industry where creators talk to each
other, cannot absorb one bad answer the way Foxy can. **WhatsApp is the answer,
permanently.**

**Testimonials with monthly figures.** The category's are literally adjacent
constants in a JavaScript bundle — $38k / $82k / $124k with flags and "Top 0.8%"
badges. Starvu's would be too. Only if a creator will state her own figure, with
her name, and consent to it — and even then the disclaimer travels. **One
creator's sentence with no number in it beats three that are constants in a
bundle.**

**Any "live" dashboard or metrics surface on the page.** There is no data
pipeline. A `LIVE` pill over static markup is Foxy's exact defect. (The signed
creator's own figures behind a login are a different object — §2.5.)

**Social-proof toasts.** They require a stream of applications. There isn't one,
and a fabricated one is the cheapest possible way to become the least trustworthy
business in the set. **Never in hardcoded form.**

**Press / "as seen in" logo wall.** There is no press. AT's is scraped and the
filenames say so. When press exists, revisit.

**A public login, account, or portal link offered to a stranger.** Eros's six
"Copy of Template" routes are an assertion machine with a password field.
**Refused permanently**, independently of whether the private portal is ever
built.

**AI-personalised copy by traffic source.** An assertion machine that also breaks
the claims ledger, because a page that says different things to different people
cannot be audited for drift.

**An AI-augmented eligibility check.** The published-rules version is honest
*because* it is arithmetic over a published policy: auditable, and it always gives
the same answer. A model over the same rules is strictly worse — same output,
unauditable.

**An AI "which agency is right for you" comparator.** A machine generating
competitor comparisons, unsupervised, in a category where two of three references
manufacture their proof.

**AI-generated imagery, anywhere.** Foxy runs `freepik__` stock. The single most
uncopyable asset available here is **real faces with permission.** Generating
images spends the one advantage that is expensive to fake.

**A content operation.** ~115 blog posts is a different business with a different
staff.

**A substitute for the conversation.** The page ends at a booked fifteen minutes
and that is the honest limit of what it can do. **A better page makes failure
faster, not smaller.** If the friend cannot convert a call, none of this matters.

### 5.2 Refused in the tooling

**No tool that sends as a creator without a human in the loop.** The distinction,
in four words: **drafts versus sends.** A tool that puts words in front of a human
is a productivity tool. A tool that puts words in front of a fan is a different
product, in a different regulatory position, with a different consent structure,
and it must not be reachable by sliding a setting.

**No system of yours that stores an identity document. Ever, anywhere, in any
surface.** Vendor-held, pass/fail, timestamp. **Build the tracker, never the
vault.** And it converts into the best disclosure asset on the record: *"We use
⟨vendor⟩. Starvu never holds your ID."* No site in the reference set mentions
verification at all — that sentence answers a fear the category does not
acknowledge exists.

**No model computes a number.** Arithmetic computes; the model may narrate what
arithmetic produced. That is also just better engineering.

**Display, never project.** The calculator refusal travels through the login. The
moment a model writes *you're on pace for $12k next month* into something she can
screenshot, Starvu has made a written earnings representation to a client — and it
is **easier** to violate on the private side, because the audience is friendly and
the number feels like a courtesy.

**Not an AI product.** This is a talent-management business with tooling in it.
The tools exist to produce facts and save the friend's afternoons. The day one of
them becomes the thing being sold, this is a different company and this whole
document is aimed at the wrong target.

**Not developed at all, and deliberately:** an AI that talks to fans as a creator
unsupervised; an AI that generates content; anything that touches a creator's
platform credentials. The first is the drafting tool with its guardrail removed,
the second is a different business, and the third is what makes a breach
unbounded.

## 6. The hazards that bite

Seven, named where they bite.

**1. Identity documents — the one hard rule.** Something already collects
government photo ID and platform verification, per partner for couples. **Nothing
you build should ever store them.** A vendor holds the document and returns
pass/fail plus a timestamp; your systems store only the result. That is a weekend
cheaper than doing it properly in-house and roughly infinitely safer. **The
operational half matters as much as the technical half: IDs must not arrive over
WhatsApp either.** A photograph of a driver's licence sent to the friend's phone
lives in that phone's cloud backup indefinitely, outside anything you build, past
any retention policy. If the current process is "text me a photo of your ID,"
changing that is worth more than every tool in this document.

**2. Doxxing is the threat model — not fraud.** Ordinary web apps threat-model
against fraud and spam. This one threat-models against **a hostile party trying to
link a stage name to a legal identity**, and the consequence is physical, not
financial. That changes concrete decisions: what goes in analytics, what goes in
logs, which third-party scripts touch pages a signed creator visits, whether
creator names appear in URLs, what is in an email subject line, what a support
tool retains. **None of it is expensive if decided at the start. All of it is
expensive to retrofit.**

**3. Model-provider content policy — the constraint that re-prices the obvious
tool.** Mainstream commercial model APIs prohibit generating adult sexual content.
That is a term of service on the *supplier* side, entirely separate from
OnlyFans's rules and from anything in Pennsylvania. Tools that touch **business**
text — call notes, FAQ clustering, DMCA notices, meeting narration, brand-deal
summaries, the clause finder — are ordinary business use and fine. Tools that
generate **the creator's** material — fan DMs, explicit promotional captions — are
not, on a mainstream provider. **So the chat-drafting assistant is not an
afternoon. It is an infrastructure decision**: a permissive provider or
self-hosted open weights, with the operating cost, evaluation work and maintenance
that implies. Weekend becomes project. *(Verify against current documents before
writing a line of that code — this is exactly what the `research` in §3.7 is for.)*
**The clean consequence:** the business-facing tools are unblocked and the
creator-content-facing tools are gated, and that ordering is identical to the one
you would choose on value grounds anyway. The constraint and the strategy agree.

**4. Platform automation lands on *her* account.** Third-party automation of
messaging is broadly prohibited across the platform tier, and enforcement lands on
**the creator's account, not Starvu's.** That converts a compliance question into
a **fiduciary** one: the downside is borne by the person who trusted you. An
agency that gets a creator's account restricted has destroyed the thing it was
hired to grow. That is a different argument from "we might get in trouble," and it
is much harder to wave away — and the rules are ambiguous, which is exactly when
*who pays if I am wrong?* is the right question.

**5. Pennsylvania is all-party consent** for oral communications, unlike the
one-party rule in most states. **Notes after a call, never recording during one.**
The safe build is also the cheap one. If transcripts are ever wanted, the consent
line goes in the booking confirmation, which the page already sends. *(Not legal
advice; check with counsel.)*

**6. The calendar is the riskiest object on the page, not the copy.** A booking is
itself an affordance promising a machine: it promises a named human will be on
that call at that time, prepared. The friend is one person running a new business.
**Three no-shows and the most expensive-looking page in the world has produced
anti-proof, delivered to exactly the professional this whole design cares about,
with his name on it.** The mitigations are all in the day-one list — real
availability rather than fictional slots, few of them, a named-agenda
confirmation, a one-tap cancel, and a WhatsApp thread that makes changing plans
cheaper than disappearing.

**7. Foxy's mobile defect, inherited free.** A 24px-radius `overflow: hidden`
wrapper amputating the right edge of the booking UI at 375px. Check the booking
flow at 375px before shipping.

## 7. What this Seed does not decide

Three standing decisions and one logged disagreement. **None was ever taken, and
this export does not take them.** Each is carried to the point where it bites, so
the plan is honest about what it does not settle. Resolving one quietly would be
worse than naming it.

**Decision 1 — which feeling leads.** The flyer and logo are high-contrast
black-and-gold, star-burst, sports-broadcast energy: loud. The brief says *not*
dark, not neon, not sleazy; "✨expensive✨". **Bites in Phase 2 and nowhere else.**
Every feature above is a *content* strategy and content strategy is orthogonal to
visual register — an agreement viewer, a ninety-day timeline and a split table all
work in either. But two consequences differ:

- **If the flyer's register wins**, the page runs louder and the `$15K–$30K` band
  probably rides the hero — which makes the earnings-not-typical disclaimer
  *mandatory* rather than optional, **and puts a permanent row in the claims
  ledger to be carried and re-verified for six months.** A visual decision
  acquiring a maintenance obligation. Small, real, and the sort of thing found at
  month six by an audit rather than at month zero by choosing.
- **If the brief's register wins**, the page needs **more** text, not less. Starvu
  cannot inherit Foxy's silence — Foxy's short pages are short because seventeen
  sections said everything upstream. A quiet page with little on it reads as
  absence rather than expense, and the disclosure instruments carry the entire
  load.

Either way, "expensive" is bought the way luxury retail buys it: **a threshold, a
document, and a small number of appointments.** Not gloss.

**Decision 2 — gate the calendar or leave it bare.** **Bites in Phase 2 on what
gets built, and again at Checkpoint C on what can be read.** Offered as input, not
taken: **the eligibility check is a gate that does not feel like one**, because it
runs on published admission rules rather than an interrogation. If *gated*, that
is the gate; if *bare*, the same component sits after the booking and loses
nothing. And a cost nobody had weighed: **a gate lengthens the fuse on
Checkpoint C** — fewer bookings per visitor means twenty bookings take longer, so
the warm/cold answer arrives later, and the answers themselves are biased toward
people willing to clear a gate, which is a different population from the one the
branch is about. That is a route-grounds argument for *bare*.

**Decision 3 — inherit the mark, or set a new register.** Bites with decision 1,
in Phase 2, and nowhere else. One dependency worth knowing: the finding that "~80%
of the effect is configuration" describes *Foxy's* system specifically and
transfers only if the restrained register wins.

**The logged disagreement — the portal.** The Horizon at one stage refused *"Not
a product. No portal, no login, no dashboard, no account,"* and later argued that
the refusal had been applied to the wrong object: a public login with nobody
behind the door stays refused forever, while a client portal for signed creators
is a disclosure instrument by the refusal's own test. The proposed resolution both
readings can live with is **"the page is not a product; the business may grow one
behind a door."** It was marked as a live tension rather than resolved. **It bites
in Phase 6 and only there.** Upheld whole, Phase 6 is deleted and nothing else on
the route moves. Amended, Phase 6 runs as written. **Do not force it early — and
do not spend the weekend without it**, because the breach-minimising rules are not
additions to a built portal.
